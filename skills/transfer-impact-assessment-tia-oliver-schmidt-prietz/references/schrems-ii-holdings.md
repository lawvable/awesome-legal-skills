# Schrems II — Key Holdings and TIA Implications

Source: CJEU Case C-311/18, Data Protection Commissioner v. Facebook Ireland Ltd and Maximillian Schrems (Grand Chamber, judgment of 16 July 2020).

This file distils the holdings of Schrems II that drive TIA practice. The judgment invalidated Privacy Shield and confirmed that Art. 46 transfers require an assessment of third-country law — i.e., a TIA.

---

## The Four Core Holdings

### Holding 1 — Privacy Shield invalid

The Commission's Privacy Shield adequacy decision (2016/1250) was invalidated because:
- US surveillance laws (Section 702 FISA, EO 12333, PPD-28) did not respect the principle of proportionality
- The ombudsperson mechanism did not provide data subjects with a cause of action before an independent and impartial tribunal
- Therefore, the level of protection in the US was not "essentially equivalent" to that in the EU

**TIA implication:** No US adequacy basis between 16 July 2020 and 10 July 2023 (Privacy Shield replacement via DPF). Transfers in this period required Art. 46 + supplementary measures or Art. 49.

### Holding 2 — SCCs remain valid in principle

The 2010 SCCs (Decision 2010/87) were upheld in principle. SCCs are a valid Art. 46 tool.

**TIA implication:** SCCs are usable, but the exporter cannot rely on SCCs alone if the third country's legal regime would prevent the importer from complying with them.

### Holding 3 — Exporter must assess third-country law

The exporter (and importer where applicable) must verify, on a case-by-case basis, whether the law of the third country ensures adequate protection. This is the legal foundation of the TIA obligation.

**TIA implication:** This is the *raison d'être* of the TIA. The exporter has an active duty to assess.

### Holding 4 — Suspension obligation

If the third-country law does not ensure adequate protection and no supplementary measures can compensate, the exporter must suspend the transfer (or the SA must order suspension).

**TIA implication:** A TIA can legitimately conclude "do not proceed" or "suspend." The skill does not push toward a permissive outcome.

## Implications for TIA Practice

### What Schrems II *requires*

1. **Case-by-case assessment** of third-country law (not categorical decisions).
2. **Supplementary measures** where the legal regime is deficient.
3. **Suspension** where measures are insufficient.
4. **Documentation** of the assessment and measures.

### What Schrems II *does NOT* require

- A specific numerical risk score
- A specific methodology (EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 is *guidance*, not a binding methodology)
- That every transfer to a non-adequate country be suspended

### The "essential equivalence" standard

The third-country law must provide protection "essentially equivalent" to EU law — not identical. The four essential guarantees (EDPB Recommendations 02/2020) operationalise this standard.

The standard is *outcome-oriented*: does the third-country regime *protect* the data subject's rights in practice, not whether it formally mirrors EU law.

## Post-Schrems II Developments

### EU-US Data Privacy Framework (DPF) — 10 July 2023

The Commission adopted the EU-US DPF adequacy decision (Implementing Decision 2023/1795) on 10 July 2023. The DPF:
- Provides an adequacy basis for transfers to DPF-certified US organisations
- Rests on EO 14086 (signed October 2022) and the Data Protection Review Court
- Covers FTC/DoT-jurisdictioned organisations only

**TIA implication for DPF transfers:** Lightweight assessment. Verify certification at dataprivacyframework.gov. Note political fragility (EO can be rescinded). Recommend SCCs as fallback.

### DPF political fragility

EO 14086 is an executive-branch construct, not legislation. It can be rescinded by a future US administration. Schrems III is a foreseeable challenge.

**TIA implication:** Do not rely on DPF as a sole basis for long-term transfers. Maintain SCCs (with TIA) as a fallback.

### OLG München, 21 U 3882/25 e (11.05.2026)

A German Higher Regional Court accepted that Meta's Facebook/Instagram data transfers to the US were:
1. Lawful under Art. 46 SCCs + supplementary measures (encryption, minimum-necessary data, transparency reports)
2. Could ALSO be justified under Art. 49(1)(b) — contract performance — because the service is inherently international

**TIA implication:** Courts are accepting practical supplementary measures (encryption, transparency reporting) as sufficient. Courts are also accepting Art. 49(1)(b) as a basis for routine transfers by global services, contradicting the EDPB's narrow reading. See `art49-derogations.md` for the balanced assessment.

## How the Skill Cites Schrems II

In TIA documents, reference Schrems II by:
- CJEU Case C-311/18 (the case number)
- Or: "Data Protection Commissioner v. Facebook Ireland and Schrems" (parties)
- Or: "Schrems II" (informal, widely recognised)

Always pair with the judgment date (16 July 2020) when citing in formal documents.
