# Regulatory Sources

This file lists the regulatory and judicial sources underpinning the TIA skill.

---

## GDPR — Chapter V

- **Art. 44** — General principle (level of protection must not be undermined)
- **Art. 45** — Adequacy decisions
- **Art. 46** — Appropriate safeguards (SCCs, BCRs, ad hoc, codes, certifications)
- **Art. 47** — Binding Corporate Rules
- **Art. 48** — Disclosures not authorised by Union law
- **Art. 49** — Derogations for specific situations

CELEX: `32016R0679` — Regulation (EU) 2016/679

## CJEU Case Law

- **C-311/18** — Data Protection Commissioner v. Facebook Ireland and Schrems ("Schrems II"), judgment 16 July 2020. Invalidated Privacy Shield; confirmed exporter's duty to assess third-country law.
- **C-362/14** — Schrems v. Data Protection Commissioner ("Schrems I"), judgment 6 October 2015. Invalidated Safe Harbor; established CJEU's role in adequacy review.
- **C-623/17** — Privacy International, judgment 6 October 2020. National security exemptions limited.
- **C-511/18, C-512/18, C-520/18** — La Quadrature du Net, judgment 6 October 2020. Bulk retention limits.

## EDPB Guidance

- **Recommendations 01/2020** v2.0 (adopted 18 June 2021) — Supplementary measures to ensure compliance with the EU level of protection. The TIA six-step methodology.
- **Recommendations 02/2020** (adopted 10 November 2020) — European Essential Guarantees for surveillance measures.
- **Guidelines 05/2021** v2.0 (adopted 14 February 2023) — Interplay between Article 3 and Chapter V. Three cumulative criteria for "transfer."
- **Guidelines 2/2018** (adopted 25 May 2018) — Derogations of Article 49.
- **Opinion 22/2024** — Use of (sub-)processors and Chapter V applicability.
- **Guidelines 02/2024** v2.1 (adopted 5 June 2025) — Article 48 GDPR: a third-country authority's request or judgment is not itself a lawful ground for transfer or disclosure absent an international agreement; assess case-by-case before disclosing. Reinforces the CLOUD Act / compelled-disclosure analysis.

## CNIL Guidance

- **Practical Guide — Transfer Impact Assessment** (final version, January 2025). https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/guide_tia.pdf

## Other Authoritative Materials

- **Rosenthal EU SCC TIA Toolbox** v1.10 (October 2022, patch September 2025). Statistical/probability-based TIA method widely used in Swiss and EU practice. https://www.rosenthal.ch/downloads/Rosenthal_EU-SCC-TIA.xlsx
- **Rosenthal "Lawful Access" FAQ** (May 2025). Methodology explainer.

## National Case Law

- **OLG München, Hinweisbeschluss of 11.05.2026 — 21 U 3882/25 e.** Higher Regional Court of Munich. Accepted Art. 46 SCCs + supplementary measures (encryption, minimum-necessary data, transparency reporting) for US transfers; also accepted Art. 49(1)(b) as a basis for routine transfers by inherently international services. https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2026-N-10016

## EU-US Frameworks

- **Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795** — EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision (10 July 2023). https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2023/1795/oj
- **Executive Order 14086** (US, October 2022) — Enhancing Safeguards for United States Signals Intelligence Activities. Foundation for DPF.

## UK Adequacy

- **Commission Implementing Decision** — UK adequacy under Art. 45(3) GDPR (renewed 19 December 2025, valid until 27 December 2031). Follows the UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

## Reading Order for New Practitioners

1. GDPR Chapter V (the statute itself)
2. EDPB Guidelines 05/2021 (what counts as a transfer)
3. CJEU Schrems II judgment (why TIAs exist)
4. EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 (the six steps)
5. EDPB Recommendations 02/2020 (essential guarantees)
6. CNIL TIA Guide (practical tables)
7. Specific country profile in this skill
