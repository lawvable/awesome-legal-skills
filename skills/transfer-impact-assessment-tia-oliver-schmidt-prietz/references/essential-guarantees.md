# Essential Guarantees — EDPB Recommendations 02/2020

Source: EDPB Recommendations 02/2020 on the European Essential Guarantees for surveillance measures (adopted 10 November 2020).

These four guarantees are the EDPB's framework for assessing whether a third country's surveillance laws are compatible with EU fundamental rights (Charter Arts. 7, 8, 47). Used in Step 3 Block B of the TIA pipeline.

The CJEU developed these guarantees through case law (Schrems I, Schrems II, La Quadrature du Net, Privacy International). The EDPB codified them as the assessment framework.

---

## Guarantee A — Processing based on clear, precise and accessible rules

The surveillance measure must have a legal basis that is:
- **Clear** — the scope and conditions are unambiguous
- **Precise** — the categories of persons, data, and circumstances are defined
- **Accessible** — the law is published and understandable

**Assessment questions:**
- Is the legal basis published and accessible to the public?
- Does the law specify the categories of persons whose data may be accessed?
- Does the law specify the types of data and the circumstances of access?
- Are the duration of measures and the procedures for authorisation, use, and storage defined?

**Common failure modes:**
- Broad national security exceptions with no defined scope
- Classified directives that bind providers but are not publicly accessible
- Powers defined so broadly that any data could fall within them

## Guarantee B — Necessity and proportionality

Surveillance must be limited to what is strictly necessary in a democratic society to safeguard a legitimate objective (Art. 23(1) GDPR or equivalent).

**Assessment questions:**
- Is the measure limited to specific, defined objectives?
- Is bulk/indiscriminate access ruled out, or only available under strict conditions?
- Are there safeguards against misuse for purposes beyond the stated objectives?
- Is the duration of retention limited to what is strictly necessary?

**Common failure modes:**
- Bulk acquisition powers (e.g., FISA 702 upstream/PRISM at scale)
- "National security" defined so broadly it encompasses commercial intelligence
- Retention periods that effectively allow indefinite access

## Guarantee C — Independent oversight

The surveillance must be subject to oversight by an independent body with effective powers to control compliance.

**Assessment questions:**
- Does an independent body oversee the surveillance regime?
- Is this body genuinely independent (not appointed by, reporting to, or removable by the executive that conducts the surveillance)?
- Does it have access to relevant materials (including classified ones)?
- Does it have effective powers to act on findings (suspend, sanction, refer)?

**Common failure modes:**
- "Oversight" by bodies within the same executive branch
- Bodies with access to summary information only, not underlying materials
- Bodies with advisory powers only, no binding remedy

## Guarantee D — Effective remedies for individuals

Data subjects must have effective remedies — including effective judicial remedies — against unlawful surveillance.

**Assessment questions:**
- Can data subjects challenge surveillance affecting them?
- Is there a notification mechanism, or at minimum a route to find out if one has been targeted?
- Is the remedy effective (binding, with meaningful consequences) or only advisory?
- Are remedies available to non-citizens / non-residents?

**Common failure modes:**
- Standing requirements that prevent challenge without proof of surveillance
- Notification prohibitions ("gag orders") that prevent data subjects from knowing they were targeted
- Remedies available only to citizens / nationals of the surveilling state
- Advisory ombudspersons with no binding remedy power (the Schrems II issue with the original Privacy Shield ombudsperson)

## Application in the TIA

For each surveillance law identified in the country profile's Block B:

1. Apply each guarantee individually.
2. Rate as: **adequate / concerns identified / insufficient**.
3. Document the reasoning and the specific failure modes (if any).
4. Roll up to a single Block B verdict that feeds into the Step 3 conclusion.

The skill does NOT score numerically — it documents the assessment qualitatively. The four guarantees are a checklist, not a scoring rubric.
