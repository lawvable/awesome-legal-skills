# Australia — TIA Country Profile

Last verified: 2026-05-29

## Adequacy Status

**Adequacy:** None. Art. 46 + TIA required.

## Data Protection Framework

**General law:** Privacy Act 1988 + Australian Privacy Principles (APPs). Reforms in progress — 2023/2024/2025 Privacy Act Review and Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 introduced incremental modernisation.

**Supervisory authority:** Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC). Independent. Enforcement powers strengthened by 2024 reforms.

**Data subject rights:** Access, correction, opt-out for direct marketing, anonymity option in some contexts. Narrower than GDPR (no general right to erasure or portability as of 2026-05; further reforms pending).

**Effective remedies:** OAIC complaints + civil claims. Statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy introduced in 2024.

## Surveillance & Government Access Laws

### Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979

**Scope:** Interception of communications, access to stored communications, telecommunications data access.

**Powers:** Warrants for interception (judicial); telecommunications data access via internal authorisations (with reforms tightening this).

**Guarantee A — Clear, precise, accessible rules:** **Adequate.** Statutory framework.

**Guarantee B — Necessary and proportionate:** **Concerns.** Telecommunications metadata access via internal authorisation has been controversial.

**Guarantee C — Independent oversight:** **Adequate.** Commonwealth Ombudsman; INSLM (Independent National Security Legislation Monitor).

**Guarantee D — Effective remedies:** **Concerns.** Notification not routine; standing barriers in some cases.

### Telecommunications and Other Legislation Amendment (Assistance and Access) Act 2018 ("TOLA")

**Scope:** Compels telecommunications providers to assist Australian agencies, including capability development for accessing encrypted communications.

**Powers:** Technical Assistance Requests (voluntary), Technical Assistance Notices (compulsory), Technical Capability Notices (compulsory new capability).

**Guarantee A — Clear, precise, accessible rules:** **Concerns.** Notice contents are classified.

**Guarantee B — Necessary and proportionate:** **Concerns.** Risk of "systemic weakness" remains contested.

**Guarantee C — Independent oversight:** **Concerns.** Technical Capability Notices require a double authorisation (Attorney-General *and* the Minister for Communications) and, post-2021, assessment by an independent panel (a retired judge plus a technical expert) — but this is still not full prior judicial oversight.

**Guarantee D — Effective remedies:** **Insufficient.** Recipients of notices subject to disclosure restrictions.

### Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 (SOCI Act, amended 2022)

**Scope:** Government powers over critical infrastructure providers, including data and operational controls.

**Guarantees A–D:** Powers are broad and government/ministerially directed with limited prior judicial control — treat as **Concerns** on proportionality (B) and oversight (C) where the importer is in scope; lower relevance for ordinary commercial data outside critical-infrastructure sectors.

### Five Eyes Intelligence Sharing

**Scope:** Australia is a Five Eyes member with US, UK, Canada, NZ. Signals-intelligence sharing rests on the UKUSA Agreement and its associated SIGINT arrangements.

**Implication:** Data shared with Australian agencies may be shared onward to Five Eyes partners. Schrems II concerns about US Section 702 indirectly relevant.

## Practical Risk Factors

- Five Eyes membership is the most significant factor for EU TIAs
- TOLA Act capabilities raise particular concern for encrypted-communications transfers
- Routine commercial transfers (HR, payments, B2B SaaS) lower risk
- Major providers' transparency reports show Australian government access at moderate levels

## Recommended Supplementary Measures

- **TM-1** (encryption with exporter-held keys) — important for communications/content
- **CM-1** (transparency obligation, where TOLA disclosure restrictions allow)
- **CM-2** (challenge clause)
- **OM-3** (government request handling policy)

For encrypted communications products: TOLA capability concerns may not be fully closable by supplementary measures — flag honestly.
