# South Africa — TIA Country Profile

Last verified: 2026-05-29

## Adequacy Status

**Adequacy:** None. Art. 46 + TIA required.

## Data Protection Framework

**General law:** Protection of Personal Information Act 2013 (POPIA), full enforcement from 1 July 2021. Closely GDPR-aligned (lawful processing conditions, data subject rights, controller/operator distinction).

**Supervisory authority:** Information Regulator. Independent. Active enforcement since 2021.

**Data subject rights:** Access, correction, deletion (in specific circumstances), objection. Substantively comparable to GDPR.

**Effective remedies:** Information Regulator complaints + civil claims. Administrative fines up to ZAR 10M per infraction. Civil damages available.

## Surveillance & Government Access Laws

### Regulation of Interception of Communications and Provision of Communication-related Information Act 2002 (RICA)

**Scope:** Interception of communications and access to communication-related information. Designated judge must authorise interceptions.

**Powers:** Court-authorised interception; emergency interceptions reviewable.

**Guarantee A — Clear, precise, accessible rules:** **Adequate, with reservations.** Detailed statutory framework, but AmaBhungane found the data-management/retention and post-surveillance-notification provisions deficient.

**Guarantee B — Necessary and proportionate:** **Partially adequate / reform pending.** The Constitutional Court in AmaBhungane Centre for Investigative Journalism v Minister of Justice (2021) declared RICA invalid in several respects (including bulk interception lacking an enabling law) and suspended the declaration of invalidity for 36 months — that period lapsed on **4 February 2024**. The RICA Amendment Bill (adopted Dec 2023) was referred back by the President over constitutional reservations and is **NOT yet in force**, so the defects remain uncured at the verification date.

**Guarantee C — Independent oversight:** **Partially adequate / reform pending.** AmaBhungane found the designated-judge appointment mechanism (executive discretion over appointment and renewal of term) failed to guarantee independence; the curing measures exist only in the un-enacted Bill. Inspector-General of Intelligence provides some oversight.

**Guarantee D — Effective remedies:** **Reform pending.** Post-surveillance notification reform was ordered but is not yet enacted; constitutional remedies are available, but the notification defect remains uncured.

### Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2019

**Scope:** Cybercrime offences and investigation powers (assented 2020; partially commenced Dec 2021). Court orders for evidence preservation, production.

**Profile:** Adequate process under judicial supervision.

## Practical Risk Factors

- Robust constitutional framework (Constitution 1996, Section 14 right to privacy)
- AmaBhungane decision strengthened oversight expectations
- Information Regulator becoming increasingly active
- Lower realistic risk than high-surveillance jurisdictions
- Major providers report low South African government request volumes for commercial data

## Recommended Supplementary Measures

- **TM-1** (encryption) — defence-in-depth
- **CM-1** (transparency obligation)
- **OM-3** (request handling policy)

**When NOT effective:** TM-1 is ineffective where the SA importer must decrypt to process; CM-1 transparency is ineffective where a RICA direction or non-disclosure obligation bars notifying the data subject; OM-3 has no force against a validly issued interception direction or court order.
