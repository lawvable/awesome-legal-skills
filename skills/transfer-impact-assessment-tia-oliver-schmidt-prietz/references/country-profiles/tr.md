# Turkey — TIA Country Profile

Last verified: 2026-05-29

## Adequacy Status

**Adequacy:** None. Art. 46 + TIA required.

## Data Protection Framework

**General law:** Personal Data Protection Law (KVKK) — Law 6698/2016, modelled on EU Directive 95/46 (pre-GDPR). **Law No. 7499 (in force 1 June 2024) restructured the cross-border transfer regime (Art. 9) into a three-tier hierarchy — adequacy / appropriate safeguards (Turkish standard contracts in four module types + BCRs) / derogations — with mandatory notification of signed standard contracts to the DPA within 5 business days (Regulation on Transfer of Personal Data Abroad, 10 July 2024).** Controllers must also register in **VERBIS** (the Data Controllers' Registry), an obligation enforced with cumulative fines.

**Supervisory authority:** Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu — statutorily autonomous (administrative and financial), affiliated to a Minister designated by the President; functional independence contested in EU adequacy assessments.

**Data subject rights:** Access, correction, deletion (under certain conditions), objection. Narrower than GDPR; no general data portability.

**Effective remedies:** KVKK Authority complaints + court proceedings. Administrative fines are re-indexed annually and rose materially after the 2024 amendment — cite the current revalued tier rather than a fixed figure (lira inflation makes any "real terms vs GDPR" comparison unreliable).

## Surveillance & Government Access Laws

### Law on Intelligence Services (Law 2937/1983, amended)

**Scope:** MIT (National Intelligence Organisation) powers. Broad collection authority for state security. The 2014 amendment (Law No. 6532) lets MIT compel personal data and documents from public bodies, banks, archives, and private companies, with broad personnel immunity.

**Powers:** Interception, electronic surveillance, mandated cooperation from providers.

**Guarantee A — Clear, precise, accessible rules:** **Concerns.** Broad statutory grounds; implementing rules partly classified.

**Guarantee B — Necessary and proportionate:** **Insufficient.** Limited statutory proportionality control.

**Guarantee C — Independent oversight:** **Insufficient.** Internal/executive oversight; constitutional concerns since 2016.

**Guarantee D — Effective remedies:** **Concerns.** Constitutional Court individual application possible but practical effectiveness contested.

### Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK) Articles 134-140

**Scope:** Communications interception in criminal proceedings.

**Powers:** Judicial order required. More bounded than intelligence framework.

**Guarantees A–D:** Generally adequate — interception under judicial authorisation.

### Internet Law (Law 5651/2007)

**Scope:** Internet content regulation, including data retention by access providers and hosting providers.

**Powers:** Compels providers to retain traffic data and respond to law enforcement.

**Guarantees A–D:** Retention/cooperation regime; concerns on proportionality (B) and oversight (C) where used for content control/blocking.

## Practical Risk Factors

- Political environment since 2016: post-coup-attempt measures expanded surveillance powers
- Erosion of judicial independence concerns flagged by EU institutions
- Constitutional Court remains a relevant remedy but practical effectiveness varies
- Commercial transfers to private-sector importers: moderate risk in most cases
- Political/journalistic/activist data: higher risk

## Recommended Supplementary Measures

- **TM-1** (encryption with exporter-held keys) — important
- **TM-2** (pseudonymisation) — where service permits
- **CM-1** (transparency obligation, where local law permits notification)
- **CM-2** (challenge clause)
- **OM-3** (government request handling policy)

**When NOT effective:** Transparency (CM-1) and request-handling (OM-3) measures have little force against MIT, which can compel disclosure without a court order and whose personnel have broad immunity. Where the importer needs plaintext or the data is of state-security interest, only exporter-held-key encryption (TM-1) or EEA localisation meaningfully mitigates — otherwise suspend.
