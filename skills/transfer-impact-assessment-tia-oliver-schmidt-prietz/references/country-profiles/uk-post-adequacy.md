# United Kingdom (post-adequacy) — TIA Country Profile

Last verified: 2026-05-29

## Adequacy Status

**Adequacy:** Yes — Commission Implementing Decision under Art. 45(3) GDPR, renewed 19 December 2025 (replacing the June 2025 sunset). Valid for six years, until 27 December 2031; a joint Commission/EDPB review is scheduled before any renewal.

**Trigger for renewal/review:** The UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (royal assent 19 June 2025) introduced reforms to UK GDPR. The Commission's 2025 renewal decision assessed these reforms and concluded UK protection remains essentially equivalent. Note: the Act commences in stages via commencement regulations (through ~mid-2026), so some UK GDPR amendments may not yet be in force at this profile's verification date.

## Data Protection Framework

**General law:** UK GDPR + Data Protection Act 2018 + Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Closely aligned with EU GDPR; divergence is incremental and bounded by adequacy review.

**Supervisory authority:** Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). Independent. Robust enforcement track record.

**Data subject rights:** Substantially the same as EU GDPR. Some flexibility in subject access response timelines under the 2025 reforms.

**Effective remedies:** ICO complaints + judicial review + civil claims for damages.

## Surveillance & Government Access Laws

### Investigatory Powers Act 2016 (IPA)

**Scope:** Comprehensive UK surveillance framework. Includes bulk interception, bulk acquisition of communications data, bulk equipment interference, bulk personal datasets.

**Powers:** Targeted warrants (judicial commissioner approval), bulk warrants (Secretary of State + judicial commissioner approval).

**Guarantee A — Clear, precise, accessible rules:** **Adequate.** Detailed statutory framework with codes of practice.

**Guarantee B — Necessary and proportionate:** **Adequate, with residual concerns.** Statutory necessity/proportionality tests apply with judicial-commissioner ("double-lock") review, but the IPA authorises *bulk* interception, bulk acquisition and bulk equipment interference. The CJEU (C-623/17, Privacy International, 2020) found the UK's bulk communications-data regime incompatible with EU law in its then-form; the Commission's adequacy finding reflects a political judgement that post-reform safeguards suffice, not the absence of concern.

**Guarantee C — Independent oversight:** **Adequate.** Investigatory Powers Commissioner (IPCO) with binding remedy powers; Investigatory Powers Tribunal (IPT) for individual complaints.

**Guarantee D — Effective remedies:** **Adequate, with residual concerns.** The IPT provides redress with appeals to the Court of Appeal, but its proceedings can be closed/ex parte and its practical accessibility for non-UK data subjects is limited.

The Commission's adequacy assessment concluded the IPA framework meets the essential equivalence standard.

### Investigatory Powers (Amendment) Act 2024

**Effect:** Amended the IPA 2016. Most significantly, it created a lighter-touch regime for **bulk personal datasets (BPDs)** in which individuals have a "low or no reasonable expectation of privacy" — these can be retained and examined without an individual warrant, subject only to Judicial Commissioner approval of categories. It also adjusted notification/oversight provisions and the regime for internet connection records.

**Guarantee implications:** The lower authorisation threshold for "low/no expectation" BPDs weakens **Guarantee B (necessity/proportionality)** and **Guarantee C (oversight)** relative to the 2016 baseline. Privacy advocates (Privacy International, the Internet Society) criticised the reduced safeguards. Treat as a divergence-monitoring trigger for future adequacy review.

### Data (Use and Access) Act 2025

**Effect:** Modernised UK GDPR. Adjustments include:
- Streamlined subject access response procedures
- Expanded permitted uses of personal data for research and innovation
- Adjusted legitimate interests framework

**Adequacy implication:** Commission concluded these reforms do not undermine essential equivalence. Monitor future divergence in renewal cycles.

## Practical Risk Factors

- UK is a Five Eyes member — intelligence-sharing with US, Canada, Australia, NZ is routine
- IPA framework is comprehensive but well-bounded — relatively predictable
- Brexit-era political dynamics may introduce future divergence
- The current adequacy decision runs six years (to 27 December 2031); begin re-assessment well ahead of expiry

## Recommended Supplementary Measures

Adequacy applies. No supplementary measures required for the legal basis. Defence-in-depth optional:
- **TM-1** for high-sensitivity transfers
- **Standard contractual obligations** (security, breach notification) regardless of adequacy

## TIA Output

For UK transfers, the formal TIA document is light:
- Section 2: document UK adequacy decision
- Section 3: confirm adequacy; note the IPA framework broadly satisfies the essential guarantees with residual concerns on bulk powers (and the 2024 Amendment's lighter-touch BPD regime); note 27 December 2031 expiry
- Section 4: no measures required (adequacy)
- Section 6: monitor: adequacy expiry 27 December 2031 (begin re-assessment well in advance), UK GDPR divergence under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, the Investigatory Powers (Amendment) Act 2024 BPD regime, and the Five Eyes intelligence-sharing context
