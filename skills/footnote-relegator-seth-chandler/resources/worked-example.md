# Worked Example

A single paragraph from a (fictitious) immigration-detention article, with one
existing citation-only footnote, relegated at a fraction that takes two of its
five sentences.

## Before

> Detention under § 1226(c) is mandatory for the covered classes.[^1] The
> mandate is the statute's point: Congress in 1996 deliberately removed the
> discretionary-release clause that had governed since 1952. To be sure, a
> handful of district courts have read a durational limit into the provision,
> though no circuit has adopted one. Scholars have likewise divided, with the
> historical literature largely ignoring the doctrinal debate and vice versa.
> The removal of discretion is why the canon of constitutional avoidance
> cannot do the work the government asks of it.

[^1]: 8 U.S.C. § 1226(c) (2018).

## Ledger

| # | Unit (first words) | Words | Category | Destination | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "To be sure, a handful of district courts…" | 24 | qualification/caveat | merge into note 1 | sentence 1 |
| 2 | "Scholars have likewise divided…" | 18 | literature placement | new note | sentence 2 |

Sentence 2 ("The mandate is the statute's point…") is compressed history by
surface type but a **protected chain link**: the final sentence's avoidance
argument depends on it. It stays.

## After

> Detention under § 1226(c) is mandatory for the covered classes.[^1] The
> mandate is the statute's point: Congress in 1996 deliberately removed the
> discretionary-release clause that had governed since 1952.[^r1] The removal
> of discretion is why the canon of constitutional avoidance cannot do the
> work the government asks of it.

[^1]: 8 U.S.C. § 1226(c) (2018). To be sure, a handful of district courts
    have read a durational limit into the provision, though no circuit has
    adopted one.

[^r1]: Scholars have divided over the 1996 removal, with the historical
    literature largely ignoring the doctrinal debate and vice versa.

## What to notice

- **Move 1 merged** into the existing citation-only note (one anchor, one
  note): cite first and untouched, demoted prose following. The caveat still
  qualifies the mandatory-detention claim — from below.
- **Move 2** became a new note anchored on the sentence it enriches, not on
  the sentence it happened to follow. Its standing-alone edit: "have likewise
  divided" → "have divided over the 1996 removal" — the "likewise" pointed at
  body text no longer adjacent, and the referent of the dividing had to be
  named. That is the *permitted* kind of edit: minimal, referential, not
  stylistic.
- The body now runs claim → basis → consequence with no seam: the surviving
  three sentences were the argument all along.
- Memo entries for this paragraph would record both moves with their
  categories, and nothing else — no rewriting happened, so nothing else needs
  confessing.
