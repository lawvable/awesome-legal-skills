# Index analytique de la doctrine — skill gouvernance-emetteurs-cotes

## Document indexé : AMF — Corporate Sustainability Reporting: The Way Forward (octobre 2025)

**Référence complète** : Autorité des marchés financiers, *Corporate Sustainability Reporting: The Way Forward — Insight on the first sustainability statements published by French listed companies under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting directive (CSRD)*, octobre 2025, 66 pages.

**Langue du document** : anglais. Format slide deck (pagination 16/9).

**Périmètre temporel** : premiers états de durabilité 2024 publiés par des sociétés françaises sous la CSRD.

**Échantillon** (p. 3) :
- **20 non-financial and financial issuers** au-dessus du seuil de 1 000 salariés, divers secteurs : Industrial Goods & Services, Healthcare, Energy, Automobile and Parts, Travel and Leisure, Food & Beverage, Technology, Drug and Grocery Stores, Banks and insurance companies.
- **Ad hoc study on assurance reports** : 91 émetteurs CAC40/SBF-120 (33 du CAC 40, 58 du SBF 120).

**Nature du document** (p. 3) — ce que cette étude **n'est pas** (citations littérales du document, encadré « What this study is not ») :
- *« Does not reflect AMF supervisory actions »*
- *« Does not imply AMF's validation of the quality of information published by issuers. »*
- *« No statistical study (sample is not representative) »*
- *« No technical evaluation of companies' sustainability assessment: focus is on transparency »*
- *« No new doctrine on CSRD: relying on existing EU regulations and FAQs. »*

**Reporting areas covered** (p. 4) :
- **1. General information** : Basis for preparation (BP) ; Materiality assessment: description of process and results (IRO-1, SBM-3) ; Minimum disclosure requirements (MDR-A).
- **2. Other general aspects** : Structure of the sustainability statement ; Interoperability ; Inclusion of EU datapoints.
- **3. Climate** : Transition plans (ESRS E1).
- **4. Social** : Own employees (ESRS S1).

**Structure du document** (p. 2 — sommaire) :
- Objective & methodology of the study : **p. 3**
- Key findings and suggestions as regards the ESRS simplification : **p. 5-13**
- Findings from the first ESRS sustainability reports : **p. 14-63**
- Ad hoc study on assurance reports : **p. 64-66**

**Vérification pagination** : pagination physique = pagination logique confirmée (sondages p. 2, 20, 40, 66). Pagination de slide deck (bas-droit de chaque page). **p. 2 = sommaire**.

**Référentiel comparé** : suggestions de simplification AMF comparées au **EFRAG exposure draft on the revised ESRS published in July 2025**, avec code couleur (aligned / partially aligned / not aligned).

## Cartographie thématique par bloc de la grille

### Bloc D — Comités du conseil

#### Inclusion par référence et articulation avec le rapport sur le gouvernement d'entreprise (BP-2)

- **Cartographie de section** : Structure of the sustainability statement, p. 36-44.
- **Constat AMF** : p. 42. Large utilisation de l'incorporation par référence par les émetteurs, en particulier pour les datapoints GOV et SBM-1.
- **Pratiques observées** : p. 42. La plupart des émetteurs fournissent la liste de toutes les incorporations par référence (requis par ESRS 2 BP-2), aidant à guider le lecteur.
- **Sources référencées** : p. 42. Rapport de gestion (facteurs de risque), URD, rapport sur le gouvernement d'entreprise (composition du conseil et rémunération), notes des états financiers.
- **Règle ESRS 1 §120a** : p. 42. Exige une référence bidirectionnelle. *« Very few issuers from the sample apply this double-reference rule. »*

### Bloc E — Rémunérations des dirigeants

#### Ratio de rémunération (S1-16) — articulation entre état de durabilité et rapport sur le gouvernement d'entreprise

- **Cartographie de section** : Own employees, p. 56-63.
- **Constat** : p. 61. *« All issuers disclose information on the pay gap, typically covering both the gender pay gap and the ratio between the highest remuneration and the median remuneration. »*
- **Hétérogénéité — séparation entre les rapports** : p. 61. *« These indicators may be located in separate parts of the report, the gender pay gap in the sustainability section, the pay ratio in the corporate governance report (linked to the French legal requirement to publish the "equity ratio"), without cross-reference or narrative link between the two reports. »*
- **Manque de transparence méthodologique** : p. 61. *« The components of remuneration included in the calculation are not specified; the scope is often missing. »*
- **Périmètre non consolidé** : p. 61. *« The pay ratio is not always provided on a consolidated basis: issuers calculate the ratio at the level of the French group, on a reduced scope, or country by country. »*

##### Métriques sur les non-salariés (S1-7, p. 62)

- **Constat** : p. 62. *« Several companies provide some disclosure on non-employees (reporting on non-employees is subject to phasing-in for the first reporting year). »* Le périmètre et la méthodologie manquent de précision.
- **Définition imprécise** : p. 62. *« Very few define precisely who is included in this category – whether it refers to temporary workers, or independent service providers. »* Incertitude sur le classement des stagiaires (*interns*), apprentis et franchisés (*franchisee*) : non-salariés, salariés ou travailleurs de la chaîne de valeur ?
- **Granularité** : p. 62. La majorité fournit des chiffres agrégés ou de brèves mentions sans contexte, sans justifier l'absence de désagrégation.

##### Métriques sur la diversité (S1-9, p. 63)

- **Constat** : p. 63. Plusieurs émetteurs publient la répartition par âge de leur effectif. *« The prevailing approach remains largely descriptive and non-analytical, often limited to high-level figures without commentary or interpretation. »*
- **Bonne pratique** : p. 63. Certains émetteurs ajoutent une ventilation par genre (lecture croisée âge/genre) ; très peu fournissent une ventilation par fonction (*job function*) permettant d'identifier des déséquilibres (concentration d'âge aux postes subalternes, manque de diversité d'âge aux postes de direction).

### Bloc H — Comply or explain (niveau d'assurance)

#### Bilan rapports d'assurance — étude ad hoc

- **Cartographie de section** : Ad hoc study on assurance reports, p. 64-66.
- **Échantillon** : p. 65. **91 issuers** (33 CAC 40 + 58 SBF 120) having issued a sustainability statement.
- **Niveau d'assurance** : p. 65.
  - *« Some issuers opted for reasonable assurance on certain datapoints, on a voluntary basis (the CSRD imposes for the first year only limited assurance). »*
  - **84 % Big 4 audit** (KPMG, Deloitte, PWC, EY).
  - **96 % adding Mazars and Grant Thornton**.
  - Honoraires d'assurance : moyenne **8 % du total des honoraires d'audit** (range : 1 % à 25 %).
- **Conclusions des rapports d'assurance** : p. 65.
  - **DMA process** : 99 % unmodified conclusions, 1 % qualified.
  - **Compliance with ESRS** : 98 % unmodified, 2 % qualified.
  - **Only 3 qualified conclusions in total**.
- **Observations d'emphase (« emphasis of matters »)** : p. 65-66.
  - DMA process : 20 % with emphasis of matters (3 % specific / 17 % generic) ; 80 % without.
  - Compliance with ESRS : 55 % with emphasis of matters (11 % specific / 44 % generic) ; 45 % without.
- **Position AMF** : p. 66. *« In most cases, emphasis of matters relating to the DMA process were rather generic. Many emphasis of matters concerning compliance with ESRS were also high-level. »*
- **Exemples d'observations spécifiques** : p. 66. *« Comments on the scope of the DMA, on input parameters (DMA), on specific thematics and sub-thematics. »*

### Bloc J — Information sur la durabilité (CSRD) — *avec sous-rubriques J.1 à J.7*

#### J.1 — Cadre normatif (CSRD, ESRS, taxonomie, vérification)

##### Constats généraux sur la première année d'application

- **Cartographie de section** : Key findings, p. 5-13.
- **Constat majeur** : p. 6. *« Differences in format and content under CSRD compared to previous reports under NFRD (Non financial Reporting Directive) show significant efforts of all issuers to prepare their first sustainability statements. Very few auditors have published a qualified opinion »*
- **Amélioration de la qualité globale** : p. 6. *« Overall, sustainability reporting is of better quality with the CSRD compared to NFRD (accessibility, comparability of data), for instance regarding the double materiality assessment and climate-related reporting (use of denominations, enhanced consistency of transition plans…) »*
- **Difficultés significatives observées pour la 1re année** : p. 6-7.
  - *« Overall readability and consistency affected by the volume of datapoints. »*
  - *« Lack of time to link new sustainability assessments and existing strategy. »*
  - *« Difficulties related to complex datapoints (data collection or reliability, lack of guidance) and scope coverage (consolidation for all KPIs, value chain impacts assessment). »*
  - *« Quantification of financial aspects (financial effects, investments related to action plans). »*
- **Hétérogénéité résiduelle** : p. 7.
  - Méthodologies pour certains datapoints.
  - Granularité (de très détaillée à boilerplate).
  - *« Significant disparities in double materiality assessment results within the same sector. »*
- **Format** : p. 7. *« Heterogeneity in structure and presentation. The numerous redundancies and interconnections between ESRS datapoints lead to extensive use of cross-references and incorporation by reference. »*
- **Phases transitoires et allègements** : p. 6. Les émetteurs utilisent les phase-ins et reliefs pour les grandes entreprises, sauf cas particuliers. *« Few companies communicate on omissions of sensitive information »*, et *« many disclose information on the value chain »* malgré la mesure transitoire de la CSRD.

##### Omissions of sensitive information (BP-1) — section consacrée

- **Cartographie de section** : Basis for preparation, p. 17-21. **Omissions of sensitive information** : **p. 21** (titre exact dans le rapport).
- **Constat sur intellectual property** : p. 21. *« Few issuers state that they use the omission related to intellectual property, know-how or result of innovation (BP-1, par. d). Some issuers state explicitly that they do not use this option. »*
- **Constat sur impending developments** : p. 21. *« Few issuers explain that the exemption to disclose information related to impending developments or matters in the course of negotiation is used (BP-1, par. e). Very few explicitly explain that they do not use it. »*
- **Imprécision juridique relevée** : p. 21. *« Some issuers mention the omission of "confidential" or "sensitive information", without clear legal basis (do not refer to BP-1, par. 5 categories). »*
- **Lien avec la simplification proposée** : p. 10. L'AMF propose de nouveaux reliefs sur l'omission d'informations sensibles (voir J.7 ci-dessous).

#### J.2 — Analyse de matérialité (impact / financière / double matérialité)

##### Méthodologies variées (ESRS 1)

- **Cartographie de section** : Materiality assessment, p. 22-33.
- **Double materiality assessment process** : p. 22.
- **Approches différentes dans la DMA** : p. 22.
  - **Top-down approach** : *« Some issuers start their DMA from a list of sustainability topics, leading to a near-exhaustive evaluation of IROs on all ESRS topics. Others assess IROs only for a subset of potentially material topics. »*
  - **Bottom-up approach** : *« Others start from a list of potentially material IROs (e.g. list from NFRD reports), then assess their materiality with CSRD criteria, then structure/group IROs by topic. »*
- **Évaluation finale** : p. 22. *« In all cases, issuers ultimately assess materiality at the granular level of IROs or groups of IROs (vs. at the higher level of topics). »*

##### Usage de la liste ESRS (AR 16)

- **Pratiques** : p. 22. *« Some issuers use the list of topics/sub-topics from the ESRS, others use their own existing classification. Some explain using a mix between their own classification and that of AR 16. »*

##### Engagement avec les parties prenantes

- **Constats** : p. 22. *« A large majority consult both internal and external stakeholders to inform the DMA. Few consult only internal experts or indicate partial engagement. Stakeholders are not always consulted at the same stage of assessment. »*
- **Pratique observée** : p. 22. *« Some issuers designed a dedicated consultation process for the 2024 DMA, others relied on existing information about stakeholder views. »*

##### Référence aux DMA antérieures

- **Constat** : p. 22. *« Several issuers explicitly refer to their previous year's DMA as the basis for their ESRS-compliant analysis. Many mention having extended the previous methodology with complementary analyses (e.g. to assess the value chain). Some indicate that the assessment process will evolve over time. »*

##### Reporting on materiality assessment process (IRO-1)

- **Cartographie** : p. 26-27.
- **Hétérogénéité** : p. 26. *« Heterogeneity observed on the level of detail and the degree of compliance with the datapoints required on the presentation of the DMA process. »*
- **Reporting partiel** : p. 26. *« Compliance with all IRO-1 datapoints is generally partial. Some issuers do not publish the IRO-1 datapoints defined in the thematic standards (or only for some sustainability topics). »*
- **Constat majeur** : p. 26. *« A large number of issuers do not publish the IRO-1 thematic datapoints associated with non-material topics, although these are still mandatory datapoints. »*
- **Présentation** : p. 26. *« A large majority publish the information about the materiality process in the thematic sections (vs. general section only). Many provide both a general overview in the general section and detailed elements in the thematic sections, sometimes with redundancies. »*

##### Reporting sur les paramètres d'entrée (IRO-1, par. 53g)

- **Constats généraux** : p. 27. *« Several issuers publish information on input parameters (data sources used, assumptions made). Information varies greatly in granularity. »*
- **Bonnes pratiques identifiées** : p. 27.
  - *« Information particularly useful when issuers specify which inputs (sources, tools) have been used for a particular sustainability topic. »*
  - Bases de données mentionnées : *« UNEP's ENCORE, WWF's Water Risk Filter »*.
  - *« Many issuers cited the Aqueduct water risk tool from the World Resources Institute to assess water-related risks. »*
- **Manque sur le social** : p. 27. *« Information on input parameters is often unspecified for social topics, in contrast to environmental topics. »*

##### Méthodologie séparée matérialité d'impact / matérialité financière

- **Constats** : p. 30. *« Issuers report on impacts AND risks/opportunities when presenting the DMA process under IRO-1, in line with the CSRD's double materiality perspective. »*
- **Difficultés terminologiques** : p. 30. *« In some cases, the distinction is not clear due to incorrect use of terminology (e.g. use of the term "risk" associated with the description of an impact). »*

##### Reporting on materiality assessment results (SBM-3)

- **Cartographie** : p. 30-33.

##### Suggestions AMF de simplification (référence aux ESRS amendés EFRAG)

- **Cartographie de section** : Key suggestions, p. 8-13.
- **Réduction des datapoints IRO-1** : p. 9. *« The AMF suggests reducing the datapoints in ESRS 2 and in the thematic ESRS to avoid boilerplate disclosures and reduce redundancies. »*
- **Datapoints les plus utiles** : p. 9. *« Information on input parameters and their link with materiality thresholds specific to sustainability topics, as well as information on the nature of financial thresholds (several good practices identified in 2025). »*
- **Datapoints à streamliner** : p. 9. *« Information on criteria for determining the materiality of information and information on stakeholder engagement (could be harmonised with SBM-2). The thematic IRO-1 datapoints could be converted into guidance. »*

#### J.3 — Données prospectives, plan de transition, scénarios

##### Plans de transition climatique (ESRS E1) — vue d'ensemble

- **Cartographie de section** : Climate transition plan, p. 48-54.
- **Constats globaux** : p. 48.
  - *« Overall improvement as compared to previous non-financial statements in terms of quality and comparability (use of denominations, consistency, etc.) which highlight the added value of the ESRS. »*
  - *« None of the transition plans (TPs) covered in our sample can be considered compliant with all ESRS datapoints. »*
  - *« Beyond the mere "tick-the-box" exercise, we have assessed whether TPs manage to convey a sense of the broader undertaking's strategy which is consistent throughout the report. In this respect major difficulties are still populating TPs' building blocks: i) target-setting; ii) decarbonisation levers; iii) financial resources. »*
- **Référence à un guide AMF antérieur** : p. 48. *« Mostly correspond to challenges anticipated last year (cf "AMF's 2024 user guide for undertakings – Reporting on climate transition plan in ESRS format"). »*
- **Bonne pratique observée** : p. 48-49. *« Use of the "AR 48 table" which puts in perspective the GHG inventory (including Scope 3 categories) with the entity's targets. »*

##### Inventaire GHG (E1-6)

- **Constats** : p. 50. *« For financial institutions, significant differences remain in the scope of reporting from one financial institution to another. »*

##### Leviers de décarbonation

- **Hétérogénéité** : p. 52. *« Some companies present a simple list of items, others strive to be more granular (e.g. by separating actions taken from future actions) and compliant with MDR-A requirements. Overall, the latter often miss (time horizons, clear scope, expected results). »*
- **Contributions à la trajectoire de décarbonation** : p. 52. *« Heterogeneous practices. When reported, the contribution of levers to targets is not always complete (by lever and by scope) nor easy to understand. »*
- **Bonne pratique** : p. 52. *« Using a graph to present decarbonisation levers (cf. AR-31 guidance), in addition to the description. »*
- **Locked-in emissions** : p. 52. *« Although rarely granular, reporting on this aspect has progressed compared to previous years (low adoption of the concept in the past). »*

##### Ressources financières allouées au plan de transition

- **Diversité des pratiques** : p. 53. *« Identified as the most difficult step for companies to take on transition planning. »* Conduit à différents types de reporting :
  - *« Detailed reporting by lever, by type of expenditure (Human, CapEx, OpEx), for planned and future resources over different time horizons. »*
  - *« Detailed reporting for part of the requirements (already planned resources). »*
  - *« Aggregated reporting: a few global figures (X billion low-carbon actions). »*
  - *« No quantitative reporting but with justifications: non-materiality or challenges in distinguishing low-carbon investments from general CapEx. »*
  - *« No reporting at all on financial resources. »*
- **Réconciliation manquante** : p. 53. *« Very few elements of reconciliation between resources associated with the transition plan (if any) and the financial statements. »*

##### Crédits carbone et absorptions (E1-7)

- **Confusion** : p. 54. *« Some issuers do not make a clear distinction between GHG emission reductions in their value chain (reported under E1-4) and emissions associated with carbon credits or removals (to be reported under E1-7) — netting of these different types of emissions. »*
- **Position AMF** : p. 54. *« This goes against the E1-4 requirements to set targets in gross value (excluding emissions associated with removals, carbon credits, avoided emissions). »*
- **Distinction des outils** : p. 54. *« E1-4 for very specific types of removal (transfer of CO2 to geological storage) vs. E1-7 for other removal mechanisms and carbon credits. »*
- **Reporting E1-7** : p. 54. *« Issuers seem to rely more on carbon credits than on removals. The level of transparency on carbon credits appears overall compliant with requirements (type of projects, location, amount of associated emissions, quality of credits, cancelled carbon credits). »*

#### J.4 — Chaîne de valeur et reporting groupe

##### Périmètre de consolidation (BP-1, par. 5a et b)

- **Cartographie de section** : Basis for preparation, p. 17-21. **Scope of consolidation** : **p. 17**.
- **Constat général** : p. 17. *« In line with ESRS requirements, issuers disclose whether the sustainability statement has been prepared on a consolidated or individual basis, and confirm that the scope of consolidation is the same as for financial statements. »*
- **Niveau de précision variable** : p. 17. *« Some issuers provide detailed information on limitations and different scopes of consolidation for certain indicators; others remain boilerplate. »*
- **Redondances** : p. 17. *« The BP-1 datapoint on scope coverage is sometimes redundant with other ESRS 2 datapoints published by companies in their thematic sections (e.g. in MDR-M: minimum reporting on metrics methodology). »*

##### Couverture de la chaîne de valeur (BP-1, par. 5c) et estimations (BP-2)

- **Cartographie** : p. 18.
- **Constats** : p. 18.
  - *« Some issuers provide a clear overview of value chain coverage (or not) by their policies/actions/targets and DMA. »*
  - *« Others remain boilerplate (they do not always explain to what extent the value chain is covered, nor their reasoning, nor the consequences of limitations). »*
- **Renvois croisés** : p. 18. *« Many refer to other sections of the report for further details. These references are sometimes too general to identify the specific BP-1 datapoint. »*
- **Estimations chaîne de valeur** : p. 18. *« Several issuers provide only boilerplate disclosures on the use of estimates for the value chain. »*

##### Métriques sujettes à incertitude (BP-2)

- **Cartographie** : p. 19.
- **Constats** : p. 19. *« Some issuers provide a boilerplate statement on the existence of uncertainty in relation to their metrics in their general section related to BP-2 without mentioning which metrics it relates to or only provide the list of metrics without explaining the sources of uncertainty, the judgements made or the consequences of such limitations. Some issuers provide detailed explanations. »*
- **Métriques typiquement sujettes à incertitude** : p. 19.
  - **Climat** : *« GHG emissions (certain scope 3 categories in particular), investments related to action plans (in particular to the transition plan). »*
  - **Social** : *« Adequate wage (choice of benchmarks and standards, difficulties in covering the full scope), gender pay gap, annual total remuneration ratios, number of work-related fatalities. »*
  - **Other environmental** : *« Water, air and soil emissions by pollutant, resource inflow, waste. »*

##### Datapoints non publiés en raison de difficultés (BP-2)

- **Cartographie** : p. 20.
- **Contexte** : p. 20. *« In the context of the first year of CSRD implementation, several issuers explained that they could not publish certain datapoints, in particular due to the lack of data and difficulties to provide reliable estimates. »*
- **Datapoints non publiés cités** : p. 20.
  - **Climate transition plan (E1-1)**.
  - **Pollution (E2)** : *« Water and soil emissions by pollutant, microplastics manufactured and used. »*
  - **Biodiversity (E4)** : *« Data collection and measurement challenges; financial effects (direct and indirect costs) of biodiversity offsets. »*
  - **Circular economy (E5)** : *« Resource inflow (e.g. sustainable organic sourcing, lack of definition). »*
  - **Own employees (S1)** : *« Total amount of fines, penalties, compensation; number of work-related fatalities. »*
  - **Business conduct (G1)** : *« Supplier payment terms. »*
  - **Anticipated financial effects** associated with risks and opportunities.

#### J.7 — Confrontation des positions AMF / ESMA / EFRAG

##### Contribution à la simplification ESRS — comparaison avec EFRAG

- **Cartographie de section** : Key suggestions, p. 8-13.
- **Objectif de l'étude** : p. 3. *« Contribute to ongoing simplification efforts by highlighting potential areas for clarification or revision in the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS). »*
- **Méthode de comparaison** : p. 8. Les suggestions de l'AMF sont comparées au **EFRAG exposure draft on the revised ESRS published in July 2025** avec un code couleur :
  - Aligned with the EFRAG draft of revised ESRS.
  - Partially aligned.
  - Not aligned, no similar proposal in the EFRAG draft.
- **Cinq sections de simplification** présentées : p. 8.
  - General aspects (ESRS 1, ESRS 2).
  - Architecture of the ESRS.
  - Topical ESRS.
  - Focus on climate reporting.
  - Focus on social reporting.

##### Autres simplifications générales (ESRS 1)

- **Nouveaux reliefs proposés** : p. 10.
  - Reliefs sur l'omission d'informations sensibles.
  - Principe « undue cost or effort » limité dans le temps, accompagné d'une transparence suffisante sur les datapoints manquants.
- **Méthodologie DMA elle-même** : p. 10.
  - *« ESRS should clarify that companies are not expected to conduct a very detailed assessment and scoring for each IRO and each sub-topic of sustainability. »*
  - *« EFRAG should find the right balance in the expected level of aggregation of information on impacts, risks and opportunities. »*
  - *« Conducting an assessment at the level of IROs (vs. at the higher level of topics only) is important as companies must present their IROs and how they manage them. »*
  - *« Maintaining the assessment of current and potential impacts and risks before taking into account mitigation efforts (i.e. not a net assessment). »*
  - *« No obligation to conduct the DMA every year unless necessary. »*

##### Structure de l'état de durabilité

- **Position AMF** : p. 10. Besoin de trouver le bon équilibre entre flexibilité accrue pour la présentation (demande des préparateurs) et comparabilité/accessibilité pour les lecteurs.
- **Recommandation pratique** : p. 10. *« Companies should be allowed to present the DMA of topics in the corresponding thematic sections. More flexibility will not be problematic if companies indicate the DR number associated with their chapter/sub-chapter ("SBM-3", "E1-4", etc.), as already seen in many reports. »*

## Volume et hétérogénéité de l'état de durabilité

- **Cartographie de section** : Structure of statements, p. 36-44.
- **Length** : p. 36. *« Sustainability statements range from 77 to 295 pages. Average: 142.1 pages. »*

## Structure de l'état de durabilité (p. 36-44)

### Convergence forte sur le format « 4 blocs » ESRS 1

- **Constat** : p. 37. *« Almost all issuers follow the required structure of 4 chapters from ESRS 1 - Appendix D (General disclosures, thematic sections E, S and G). »*
- **Adoption de l'Appendix F** : p. 37. *« A large majority adopt the detailed structure of Appendix F (on a voluntary basis). »*
- **Au-delà des 4 blocs** : p. 37. *« Several issuers add other chapters: methodological note in annex, correspondence tables. »*

### Hétérogénéité dans la structuration des sous-sections

- **Constats** : p. 37.
  - *« Some strictly follow the order of disclosure requirements (DR) in a thematic standard, or even of datapoints in a DR (facilitates identification but increases redundancies and cross-references). »*
  - *« Others adopt a more flexible approach to limit redundancies (e.g. by grouping policies related to similar topics). »*

### Bonnes pratiques de lisibilité

- **Résumé exécutif** : p. 39-40. *« Several issuers voluntarily provide an executive summary (at the beginning of the report or in each thematic section) with an overview of material IROs, policies, actions, targets and key indicators. »*
- **Liste exhaustive des indicateurs** : p. 39. *« Some issuers provide at the end of the report an exhaustive list of all published sustainability indicators, with comparative information for 2023. »*
- **Utilisation de « tags » DR** : p. 39. *« Several issuers use ESRS DR or even DP references in the name of their sections and sub-sections, facilitating identification (good practice). »*
- **Tableau récapitulatif des KPI** : p. 41. *« Good practice observed: provide a table with all KPIs presented in the sustainability report, including taxonomy KPIs (along with comparatives, not required in 2024). »*

### Information non matérielle

- **Présentation hétérogène** : p. 43. *« Some issuers present non-material information within their sustainability statement (different from material entity-specific information under ESRS). »*
- **Ambiguïté** : p. 43. *« It is not always clear whether information presented as "additional" to the ESRS statement is material or not. »*
- **Question d'assurance** : p. 43. *« It is not always clear whether this additional information is covered by the auditor's assurance (especially when the information is presented in a dedicated "other sustainability information" section at the end of the report or even after the auditor's opinion). »*

## Recommandations AMF nouvelles ou positions exprimées en 2025

L'étude AMF formule **suggestions of simplification** (et non recommandations doctrinales) :

1. **Reduce datapoints in ESRS 2 and thematic standards** pour la matérialité (IRO-1).
2. **Convert thematic IRO-1 datapoints into guidance**.
3. **Streamline stakeholder engagement** (harmonise IRO-1 stakeholder engagement with SBM-2).
4. **Remove GOV-4 [renamed GOV-3]** (due diligence mapping) car valeur ajoutée faible.
5. **Introduce reliefs on sensitive information** et un principe *« undue cost or effort »* limité dans le temps.
6. **Strengthen interoperability with ISSB** (financial ranges, *« undue cost or effort »* principle).
7. **Allow presentation of DMA in thematic sections**.
8. **Maintain assessment of current and potential impacts and risks** before mitigation efforts.
9. **Standardise use of AR 48 table** pour articuler inventaire GHG et cibles climatiques.
