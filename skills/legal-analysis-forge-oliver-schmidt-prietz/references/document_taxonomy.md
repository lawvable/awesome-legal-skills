## 1. Binding primary instruments

### EU Regulation

Examples: GDPR (2016/679), AI Act (2024/1689), Data Act (2023/2854), DGA (2022/868), DSA (2022/2065), DMA (2022/1925), NIS2 (in Directive form, see §2), CRA (2024/2847), DORA (2022/2554), eIDAS 2.0 (2024/1183), ePrivacy Regulation (pending), PLD (2024/2853 — Directive, see §2).

- **Binding force**: directly applicable in all Member States from date of application; no transposition required
- **Issuer**: European Parliament and Council; legal basis usually Art. 16 or 114 TFEU
- **Citation format**: Regulation (EU) YYYY/NNNN; cite by Article and Recital
- **Signals**: published in OJ L series; CELEX prefix 3...R...; numbered articles; recitals as preamble; final provisions on entry into force and date of application
- **Scrutinise for**: legal basis under TFEU, Charter compatibility, proportionality, allocation between EU and Member State competence, interaction with sectoral acts, delegated and implementing act empowerments, review clauses

### EU Directive

Examples: NIS2 (2022/2555), PLD (2024/2853), AI Liability Directive (pending).

- **Binding force**: binding as to the result to be achieved; requires transposition by Member States within stated deadline
- **Citation format**: Directive (EU) YYYY/NNNN; cite by Article and Recital
- **Signals**: published in OJ L series; CELEX prefix 3...L...; transposition deadline in final provisions
- **Scrutinise for**: same as Regulations plus transposition margin (where Member States have discretion), goldplating risk, direct effect of unimplemented provisions, vertical vs horizontal direct effect

### EU Decision

- **Binding force**: binding on addressees identified in the act
- **Citation format**: Decision (EU) YYYY/NNNN
- **Signals**: addressed to specific Member States, undertakings, or institutions
- **Scrutinise for**: scope of binding effect, legal basis, procedural compliance

## 2. Binding secondary instruments

### Delegated Act

- **Binding force**: binding
- **Issuer**: Commission, under empowerment in primary instrument (Art. 290 TFEU)
- **Citation format**: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) YYYY/NNNN
- **Signals**: cites the empowering article of the primary act in its preamble; Parliament and Council have objection rights
- **Scrutinise for**: whether the act stays within the empowerment (intra vires), whether it modifies non-essential elements only, whether objection period has run

### Implementing Act

- **Binding force**: binding
- **Issuer**: Commission, under Art. 291 TFEU, usually with comitology
- **Citation format**: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) YYYY/NNNN
- **Signals**: cites Art. 291 TFEU and the implementing power in the primary act; comitology procedure named in preamble
- **Scrutinise for**: whether the act executes rather than modifies the primary instrument, comitology compliance

### EDPB Binding Decision

- **Binding force**: binding on the supervisory authorities concerned
- **Issuer**: EDPB under Art. 65 GDPR
- **Citation format**: EDPB Binding Decision NN/YYYY
- **Signals**: identifies the cross-border case; resolves disagreement between lead and concerned SAs
- **Scrutinise for**: scope of bindingness (between authorities, then translated into national decisions), judicial review path

## 3. Non-binding interpretive instruments

### Commission Guidelines

Examples: Guidelines on the definition of an AI system (C(2025) 5053); draft Guidelines on Art. 6 high-risk classification (2026).

- **Binding force**: non-binding; authoritative interpretation that shapes enforcement
- **Issuer**: Commission, usually under express empowerment in primary act (e.g. Art. 6(5) AI Act, Art. 96 AI Act)
- **Citation format**: Commission Guidelines C(YYYY) NNNN; cite by paragraph number
- **Signals**: published as Commission Communication or annex thereto; disclaimer that they are non-binding; reference to Court of Justice as ultimate interpreter
- **Scrutinise for**: textual fidelity to the primary act, interpretive moves not supported by the text, gaps in coverage, operability, consistency with adjacent guidelines, whether the procedural conditions for issuance were met (e.g. AI Board consultation)

### Commission Recommendation

- **Binding force**: non-binding (Art. 288 TFEU)
- **Issuer**: Commission
- **Citation format**: Commission Recommendation (EU) YYYY/NNNN
- **Signals**: "Recommendation" in title; addressed to Member States or other actors
- **Scrutinise for**: alignment with EU primary law, persuasive force in subsequent legislation or case law

### EDPB Guidelines

Examples: Guidelines 1/2024 on processing of personal data based on legitimate interest; Guidelines 01/2020 on transfers.

- **Binding force**: non-binding; authoritative interpretation of GDPR shaping SA enforcement
- **Issuer**: EDPB under Art. 70(1)(e) GDPR
- **Citation format**: EDPB Guidelines NN/YYYY on [topic], version X.Y, adopted [date]; cite by paragraph number
- **Signals**: published on edpb.europa.eu; versioned; usually preceded by a public consultation version
- **Scrutinise for**: textual fidelity to GDPR, departures from prior WP29 guidance, internal consistency, treatment of CJEU case law, gaps

### EDPB Opinion

- **Binding force**: non-binding; specific procedural roles under Art. 64 GDPR (consistency mechanism)
- **Citation format**: EDPB Opinion NN/YYYY
- **Signals**: addresses a specific national draft measure or list (e.g. DPIA blacklists, BCRs)
- **Scrutinise for**: scope of the opinion, alignment with GDPR text, whether SAs followed it

### AI Office outputs

Examples: GPAI Code of Practice (Art. 56 AI Act).

- **Binding force**: varies; the AI Office can adopt non-binding outputs, and codes of practice it endorses can have presumption-of-compliance effect
- **Issuer**: AI Office (within the Commission)
- **Citation format**: cite by version, date, and section
- **Scrutinise for**: legal basis, alignment with AI Act text, scope of presumption of compliance, signatory effect

### ENISA guidance

- **Binding force**: non-binding; technical authority
- **Issuer**: ENISA under empowerment in NIS2, CRA, etc.
- **Scrutinise for**: technical accuracy, alignment with legal text, treatment in supervisory practice

## 4. Adjudicative instruments

### CJEU judgment

- **Binding force**: binding interpretation of EU law; erga omnes in preliminary references
- **Citation format**: Case C-NN/YY, [Party] v [Party], ECLI:EU:C:YYYY:NNN; cite by paragraph number
- **Signals**: rendered by Court of Justice or General Court; chamber composition; preliminary reference or direct action; published on curia.europa.eu
- **Scrutinise for**: scope of the ruling (operative part vs reasoning), preliminary reference question vs answer, whether the Court restricts or extends prior case law, treatment of AG Opinion

### Court of Justice Order

- **Binding force**: binding but typically procedural or where the answer is clear from existing case law (Art. 99 Rules of Procedure)
- **Citation format**: as for judgments
- **Scrutinise for**: same as judgments, with awareness that the reasoning is usually compressed

### AG Opinion

- **Binding force**: non-binding; persuasive
- **Citation format**: Opinion of Advocate General [Name] in Case C-NN/YY, ECLI:EU:C:YYYY:NNN; cite by paragraph number
- **Signals**: precedes the judgment by roughly 3–6 months
- **Scrutinise for**: argumentation that the Court may adopt or reject; alternative readings; comparative law references

### National DPA decision

- **Binding force**: binding on addressee within the Member State; subject to judicial review
- **Citation format**: vary by authority; include authority name, date, case reference
- **Signals**: published on the DPA's website, often in national language only
- **Scrutinise for**: legal basis under national and EU law, treatment of CJEU and EDPB guidance, fine calculation methodology, scope of binding effect

### National court decision

- **Binding force**: binding within the Member State; persuasive elsewhere
- **Citation format**: court name, date, docket; for German courts, BVerfG / BGH / OLG / VG with file number; for ECLI-enabled courts, ECLI:[MS]:[Court]:YYYY:NNN
- **Scrutinise for**: alignment with CJEU case law, transposition-law interpretation, whether the court referred or should have referred

## 5. Voluntary instruments with regulatory weight

### Code of conduct

- **Legal basis**: Art. 40 GDPR; Art. 56 AI Act; Art. 45 DSA; equivalents under other acts
- **Binding force**: voluntary; once approved, binding on signatories
- **Scrutinise for**: scope of binding effect on signatories, monitoring mechanism, approval procedure compliance, presumption of compliance with the underlying act

### Harmonised standard

- **Legal basis**: Art. 40 AI Act; equivalents under CRA, etc.; under Regulation (EU) 1025/2012
- **Binding force**: voluntary; creates presumption of conformity where reference is published in OJ
- **Citation format**: EN NNNNN:YYYY; reference also OJ publication
- **Scrutinise for**: scope of the presumption, gaps where the standard does not cover the legal requirement, fitness-for-purpose, drafting body legitimacy

## 6. Member State transposition instruments

### Examples

- **BDSG** (Bundesdatenschutzgesetz) — Germany's GDPR transposition and supplementation
- **BSIG-neu** (Gesetz über das Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der Informationstechnik, as amended by NIS2UmsuCG)
- **NIS2UmsuCG** (German NIS2 transposition)
- Equivalents in other Member States (Austrian NISG, Italian D.Lgs. 138/2024, Dutch Cyberbeveiligingswet, French Loi Résilience, Spanish national NIS2 transposition)

### Scrutinise for

- Conformity with the underlying Directive (and any Regulation it supplements)
- Goldplating: where the national act adds obligations beyond the Directive
- Goldcutting: where the national act omits or weakens obligations from the Directive
- Direct effect of unimplemented Directive provisions (vertical only)
- Sectoral allocation (which authority supervises which obligation)
- Interaction with other national law (constitutional, administrative, civil)

## 7. Draft and consultation instruments

### Examples

- Commission draft guidelines for stakeholder consultation
- EDPB draft guidelines for public consultation
- Parliament or Council draft positions
- Trilogue compromise texts (where leaked or released)

### Binding force

None. Signals direction; sometimes anticipates the final text closely, sometimes diverges substantially.

### Scrutinise for

- Status: which stage of the procedure
- Consultation parameters: deadline, submission process, public or stakeholder-only
- Anticipated changes from draft to final
- Strategic implications for the stakeholder response

## 8. Quick characterisation checklist

For any incoming document, run through:

1. **Title and preamble** — what does it call itself?
2. **OJ publication or CELEX number** — confirms instrument type and date
3. **Citation of legal basis** — confirms competence and procedural track
4. **Recitals** — interpretive framing; do not confuse with operative provisions
5. **Substantive articles** — the binding content
6. **Annexes** — usually substantive; never assume they are decorative
7. **Final provisions** — entry into force, date of application, transition, sunset, review
8. **Disclaimers** — particularly in non-binding instruments
9. **Signatures and adoption date** — confirms status (adopted vs draft)
10. **Cross-references** — which other instruments the document engages with

If after this checklist the instrument type is still unclear, ask the user before proceeding.
