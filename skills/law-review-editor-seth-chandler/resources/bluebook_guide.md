# Bluebook Citation Reference

Essential citation rules for law review articles (21st Edition). This is a condensed guide focusing on the most common citations.

## General Rules

### Typeface Conventions
- **Law review articles**: Use *italics* for case names, book titles, and article titles
- **Court documents/legal memoranda**: Use underlining instead of italics

### Signals
**No signal**: Cited authority directly states the proposition, identifies source of quotation, or identifies authority referred to in text
- *See* - Authority supports but doesn't directly state the proposition
- *See also* - Additional source material supporting the proposition
- *Cf.* - Authority supports proposition by analogy
- *Contra* - Authority directly contradicts the proposition
- *But see* - Authority contradicts proposition (less direct than contra)
- *See generally* - Background/general support

**Order of signals**: (1) no signal, (2) *see*, (3) *see also*, (4) *cf.*, (5) contra, (6) *but see*, (7) *see generally*

### Short Forms
Use short forms only after full citation appears. Never use short form for first citation.

## Cases

### Full Citation Format
**Federal Cases**: *Case Name*, Volume Reporter Page, Pinpoint (Court Year).

**Example**: *Brown v. Board of Educ.*, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954).

**State Cases**: *Case Name*, Volume Reporter Page, Pinpoint (Court Year).

**Example**: *People v. Anderson*, 6 Cal. 3d 628, 640 (1972).

### Case Name Rules
- Abbreviate parties per Table T6 (Corp., Inc., Ltd., Ass'n, etc.)
- Use first party name on each side only
- Omit "The" as first word
- *United States v. Smith* (not *U.S. v. Smith* in citations)
- *In re Smith* (not *In the Matter of*)

### Court Abbreviations (Table T1)
- **U.S. Supreme Court**: No court abbrev. needed - just year
- **Federal Courts of Appeals**: (9th Cir. 2020), (D.C. Cir. 2019)
- **Federal District Courts**: (S.D.N.Y. 2021), (N.D. Cal. 2020)
- **State Supreme Courts**: Usually just (Cal. 2020), except (N.Y. App. Div. 2020)

### Reporter Abbreviations
- **Supreme Court**: U.S. (official), S. Ct., or L. Ed.
- **Federal Courts of Appeals**: F., F.2d, F.3d, F.4th
- **Federal District Courts**: F. Supp., F. Supp. 2d, F. Supp. 3d
- **State reporters**: See Table T1

### Short Forms for Cases
After full cite, use:
- *Id.* if immediately preceding citation (same footnote or consecutive footnotes)
- *Brown*, 347 U.S. at 495 (if non-consecutive)
- *Id.* at 500 (if different page of same source)

## Statutes

### Federal Statutes
**U.S. Code**: Title U.S.C. § Section (Year).

**Example**: 42 U.S.C. § 1983 (2018).

**Multiple sections**: Use §§
- 42 U.S.C. §§ 1981-1983 (2018).

### State Statutes
Follow Table T1 for state-specific formats.

**Example (California)**: Cal. Civ. Code § 1750 (West 2020).

### Short Forms
- § 1983 (if clear from context)
- *Id.* (if immediately preceding)

---

## Constitutions

**Format**: [Jurisdiction] Const. art. [article], § [section].

**Examples**:
- U.S. Const. art. I, § 8.
- U.S. Const. amend. XIV, § 1.
- Cal. Const. art. III, § 3.

**Short form**: Art. I, § 8 (if clear from context)

---

## Law Review Articles & Journals

**Format**: Author, *Title*, Volume Journal Abbreviation Page, Pinpoint (Year).

**Example**: Richard A. Posner, *Economic Analysis of Law*, 53 U. Chi. L. Rev. 1051, 1065 (1986).

### Author Names
- First & middle initials + full surname for up to two authors
- Three or more authors: First author *et al.*
- Student notes: Use *Note* or *Comment* instead of author name

### Journal Abbreviations (Table T13)
Common examples:
- Harvard Law Review: Harv. L. Rev.
- Yale Law Journal: Yale L.J.
- Stanford Law Review: Stan. L. Rev.
- Columbia Law Review: Colum. L. Rev.
- University of Chicago Law Review: U. Chi. L. Rev.

### Short Forms
- Posner, *supra* note 12, at 1070.
- *Id.* at 1072 (if immediately preceding)

---

## Books

**Format**: Author, *Title* Page (Edition/Publisher Year).

**Example**: Ronald Dworkin, *Taking Rights Seriously* 81 (1977).

**Multiple authors**: List up to two; three or more use *et al.*

**Edited volumes**: Editor Name ed., *Title* (Year).

**Short form**: Dworkin, *supra* note 5, at 90.

---

## Newspapers & Magazines

**Format**: Author, *Article Title*, Newspaper, Date, at Page.

**Examples**:
- Adam Liptak, *Supreme Court Rules on Presidential Immunity*, N.Y. Times, July 1, 2024, at A1.
- *The Court's Immunity Decision*, Wall St. J., July 2, 2024, at A14 (editorial).

**Short form**: Liptak, *supra* note 18.

---

## Online Sources

### General Websites
**Format**: Author, *Title*, Website, URL (last visited Date).

**Example**: Jane Doe, *Understanding Constitutional Law*, Cornell Law School, https://www.law.cornell.edu/const (last visited Oct. 15, 2024).

### Online Journals/Databases
Cite as print version when available. If only online:

**Example**: John Smith, *The Future of Privacy Law*, 10 J. Online L. 1, 15 (2023), https://www.journalonline.com/article.

---

## Parentheticals

Add explanatory parentheticals to provide context:

**Examples**:
- *Brown v. Board of Educ.*, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954) (holding that segregation in public schools violates Equal Protection Clause).
- Richard Posner, *Economic Analysis of Law*, 53 U. Chi. L. Rev. 1051, 1065 (1986) (arguing that efficiency should guide legal rules).

**Weight of authority**: (plurality opinion), (per curiam), (mem.), (en banc)

**Quoting/citing**: (quoting *Case Name*, Volume Reporter Page, Pinpoint), (citing *Source*)

---

## Footnote Structure

### Order Within Footnotes
1. No signal authorities
2. Authorities with signals (in signal order)
3. Different signals separated by semicolons
4. Multiple authorities under same signal separated by semicolons
5. Explanatory parentheticals at end

**Example**:
```
15. Brown v. Board of Educ., 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954); see also Loving v. Virginia, 
388 U.S. 1, 12 (1967) (extending equal protection analysis to marriage); cf. Korematsu 
v. United States, 323 U.S. 214 (1944) (applying different standard during wartime).
```

### Subsequent References
- **First reference**: Full citation
- **Immediately following**: *Id.* at [page]
- **Later reference**: Short form with "*supra* note [#]"

## Common Errors to Check

1. **Spacing**: Proper spacing around abbreviations (U.S., F.3d, etc.)
2. **Pinpoint cites**: Always include specific page reference when citing for proposition
3. **Periods**: Every cite ends with period
4. **Italics vs. Roman**: Case names, book titles, article titles in italics; "supra," "infra," "et al." in roman
5. **Volume/Page format**: No "p." or "pp." - just numbers (347 U.S. 483)
6. **Year**: Always include; in parentheses at end
7. **Small caps**: Use for author names in books and articles (Dworkin)
8. **Commas**: After case name, after page cite, before parenthetical
9. **"Id."**: Capitalize and italicize with period
10. **Court designation**: Include unless obvious (always omit for U.S. Supreme Court)

## Quick Checklist for Citation Review

- [ ] Case names properly italicized and abbreviated?
- [ ] All citations have pinpoint pages?
- [ ] Signals used correctly and in proper order?
- [ ] Short forms used appropriately (*Id.*, *supra*)?
- [ ] Court and year properly formatted?
- [ ] Spacing and punctuation correct?
- [ ] Reporter abbreviations match Bluebook tables?
- [ ] All citations end with periods?
- [ ] *Supra* references point to correct footnote numbers?
- [ ] Parentheticals explain relevance when needed?
