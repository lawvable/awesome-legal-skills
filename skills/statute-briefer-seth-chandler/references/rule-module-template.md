# Rule-Module Template

Use this template for each major operative provision in the RULES section.

## Rule-Module: [Provision cite]

### 1. Provision
Full pinpoint citation (e.g., "42 U.S.C. § 1983" or "Tex. Health & Safety Code § 161.0901(a)")

### 2. Trigger conditions (elements)
List each element that must be satisfied to activate the provision. Number them.

**Example (42 U.S.C. § 1983):**
1. Defendant acted "under color of" state law
2. Defendant deprived plaintiff of a right
3. The right was secured by the Constitution or federal laws

**For complex provisions:** Break elements into sub-elements when the statute uses nested conditions (e.g., "A applies if (1) X and (2) either Y or Z").

### 3. Legal effect
Identify the operative command:
- **Command** – "shall," "must" (mandatory duty)
- **Permission** – "may" (discretionary authority)
- **Prohibition** – "shall not," "may not," "no person shall"
- **Entitlement** – Creates a right, benefit, or cause of action

State the effect concisely.

**Example:**
> **Entitlement:** Creates a private cause of action for damages against the person who caused the deprivation.

### 4. Exceptions / defenses / safe harbors
List carve-outs from the rule. Include:
- Explicit statutory exceptions ("except that...")
- Affirmative defenses created by the statute
- Safe harbors ("shall not be liable if...")
- Immunity provisions

If none exist in the statute, state: "None specified in statute."

**Example:**
> - Absolute immunity for legislators acting in legislative capacity
> - Qualified immunity for officials performing discretionary functions (judicially created, not statutory)

### 5. Procedure / decision-maker
Identify procedural requirements:
- **Deadlines** – Filing periods, notice periods, cure periods
- **Notice requirements** – Who must be notified, form, timing
- **Standards** – Burden of proof, evidentiary standards
- **Required steps** – Exhaustion, administrative prerequisites
- **Decision-maker** – Who decides (agency, court, official)

If not specified, state: "Not specified; default procedural rules apply."

**Example:**
> - No exhaustion of state remedies required (Patsy v. Board of Regents)
> - Standard: preponderance of the evidence
> - Decision-maker: federal or state court

### 6. Consequences
Identify what happens when the provision is triggered:
- **Penalties** – Civil/criminal fines, imprisonment
- **Remedies** – Damages, injunctions, declaratory relief
- **Invalidity** – Contract void, action nullified
- **Loss of benefits/funds** – Forfeiture, disqualification
- **Other** – Reporting requirements, debarment

**Example:**
> - Compensatory damages for actual injury
> - Punitive damages (where reckless or callous indifference)
> - Reasonable attorney's fees to prevailing plaintiff (42 U.S.C. § 1988)
> - Injunctive and declaratory relief available

### 7. Forum / enforcer
Identify who can invoke the provision and where:
- **Enforcer** – Agency (which?), private plaintiff, prosecutor
- **Forum** – Federal court, state court, administrative tribunal
- **Jurisdiction** – Original, exclusive, concurrent
- **Venue** – Where suit may be brought

**Example:**
> - **Enforcer:** Private plaintiff (individual whose rights were violated)
> - **Forum:** Federal district court (28 U.S.C. § 1343) or state court (concurrent jurisdiction)
> - **Venue:** District where violation occurred or defendant resides

### 8. Interaction rules
Identify how this provision relates to other law:
- **Preemption** – Does it displace state law? Express or implied?
- **Savings clause** – Does it preserve other remedies?
- **Severability** – If part invalidated, does rest survive?
- **Effective date** – When does it apply? Retroactivity?
- **Cross-references** – Which other provisions condition or modify this one?

**Example:**
> - **Savings:** Does not preempt state tort remedies (concurrent remedies available)
> - **Cross-references:** 42 U.S.C. § 1988 (attorney's fees); 28 U.S.C. § 1343 (jurisdiction)
> - **Effective date:** Not applicable (long-standing provision)

## Condensed Format (for brevity requests)

When user requests "short" output, use this condensed format:

**Rule-Module: [cite]**
- **Trigger:** [numbered elements]
- **Effect:** [command/permission/prohibition/entitlement + brief description]
- **Exceptions:** [list or "none"]
- **Consequences:** [penalties/remedies]
- **Forum:** [enforcer + court/agency]
- **Interactions:** [preemption/savings/key cross-refs]
