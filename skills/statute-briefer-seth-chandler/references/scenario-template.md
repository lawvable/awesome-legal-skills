# Scenario Module Template

Use this template when briefing a statute in Mode B (scenario-driven). The Scenario Module appears before MAPS.

## Scenario synopsis

State legally salient facts only. Strip narrative; focus on:
- Who are the parties/actors?
- What conduct or transaction occurred?
- When and where?
- What decision or dispute is at issue?

**Example:**
> Employer X terminated Employee Y after Y reported suspected Medicare fraud to the company's compliance hotline. Y seeks to recover damages under the False Claims Act's anti-retaliation provision.

## Issues framed as statutory questions

Convert the scenario's disputes into precise statutory interpretation questions. Reference specific provisions.

**Example:**
> 1. Does Y qualify as an "employee" under 31 U.S.C. § 3730(h)(1)?
> 2. Did Y's report to the internal hotline constitute "lawful acts done ... in furtherance of" an FCA action under § 3730(h)(1)?
> 3. Was Y's termination "because of" the protected activity?

## Relevant excerpts (verbatim)

For each provision implicated by the issues, provide:

### [Provision cite]

**Quoted Text:**
> "[exact statutory language]"

**Adjacent definitions/exceptions (if applicable):**
> "[related definitional or exception language]"

**Guidelines:**
- Use block quotes or clear "Quoted Text:" markers
- Include pinpoint citation
- Quote only what is necessary to preserve meaning
- Include adjacent definition/exception language when it changes the provision's application
- If relevant language is not in the provided text, state: "Text not included in provided materials"

## Application map (statute-to-facts)

For each issue, create a structured analysis:

### Issue 1: [restate the statutory question]

| Element | Fact Status | Analysis |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Element A | Met / Unclear / Disputed / Fact needed | Brief explanation |
| Element B | Met / Unclear / Disputed / Fact needed | Brief explanation |

**Missing facts (if any):** List specific facts that would resolve unclear elements.

**Example:**
| Element | Fact Status | Analysis |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| "employee" | Met | Y was on X's payroll; not a contractor |
| "lawful acts" | Unclear | Internal report may or may not qualify |
| "in furtherance of" FCA action | Unclear | No evidence Y contemplated filing suit |
| "because of" | Disputed | X claims performance-based termination |

**Missing facts:** Whether Y mentioned potential litigation when reporting; X's documentation of performance issues prior to report.

## Context map (where excerpts live)

Show the structural location of each quoted excerpt within the Act.

**Format:**
```
Title/Chapter/Subchapter/Part/Section path
```

**Example:**
```
31 U.S.C. § 3730(h)(1) - Anti-retaliation provision
  └─ Chapter 37: Claims
      └─ Subchapter III: Claims Against the United States Government
          └─ § 3730: Civil actions for false claims
              └─ (h): Relief from retaliatory actions
                  └─ (1): Protected conduct and remedies
```

Identify adjacent sections that typically travel with the excerpt:
- Related definitions (e.g., § 3729(b) defines "knowing" and "knowingly")
- Enforcement mechanisms (e.g., § 3730(b)-(d) qui tam procedures)
- Exceptions or limitations

## Not used but structurally important

List major components of the Act that are NOT triggered by this scenario. This preserves whole-act awareness.

**Example:**
> The following provisions are not implicated by this scenario but are significant components of the False Claims Act:
> - § 3729: Liability provisions and penalty amounts
> - § 3730(b)-(e): Qui tam procedures for private enforcement
> - § 3731: Procedural rules and statute of limitations
> - § 3732: Jurisdiction and venue
> - § 3733: Civil investigative demands
