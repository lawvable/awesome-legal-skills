# Mediation Problem Design Grammar

## Design the Public Dispute

Build a dispute that is understandable without specialized law but not legally predetermined. Prefer commercial uncertainty involving performance, valuation, governance, IP, supply, distribution, licensing, financing, reputation, control, or implementation.

Give the parties a credible history and a precipitating event. Make public positions genuinely conflict. Avoid villains, fraud-dependent plots, or a party whose conduct is irrational unless the irrationality itself is a deliberate teaching objective.

## Create Hidden Value

Use at least three negotiable dimensions, ordinarily drawn from different categories:

- money, price, installments, revenue share, or valuation;
- timing, duration, renewal, option, or staged performance;
- volume, exclusivity, territory, customer access, or distribution;
- IP ownership, licensing, branding, recipes, data, or know-how;
- governance, control, reporting, audit, or approval rights;
- marketing, service levels, quality, investment, or operational commitments;
- reputation, apology, confidentiality, public statements, or transition support;
- risk allocation through contingencies, insurance, escrow, earn-outs, pilots, or triggers;
- adjacent ventures, substitute products, new markets, or future business.

Every valuable opportunity must connect to a resource or authority a party actually controls.

## Calibrate the Narrow Window

Aim for a `narrow_sound` corridor:

- **Impossible:** no package meets both sides' hard constraints or outperforms both BATNAs.
- **Brittle:** exactly one package works, especially if it requires guessing a single hidden number.
- **Narrow sound:** two to four materially different packages work, each requiring linked concessions and careful information exchange.
- **Too easy:** the confidential packets reveal matching numbers or an obvious trade that resolves every issue without meaningful risk or sacrifice.

Create overlap through differently valued terms, not arbitrary numerical coincidence. Make at least one seemingly attractive proposal fail because of financing, authority, implementation, legal, or protected-interest constraints.

Do not make every interest tradeable. Each party should have one or more protected interests. Do not make protected interests mirror images that make settlement impossible.

## Build Confidential Information

Each packet should reveal:

- why the public position is incomplete;
- what the party must protect;
- where the party can move;
- what creates internal or board-level difficulty;
- what it privately fears about litigation or termination;
- what resources or opportunities it can offer;
- what information it needs from the other side;
- what it believes or suspects, clearly distinguished from fact.

Do not disclose the other side's reservation value, full business plan, or intended package. Avoid telling the party exactly which offer to make. Give negotiators enough to reason, not a script.

Do not mirror option menus across the two packets. A packet may mention a partial concept its own party has considered, but it must not reproduce the author-only package taxonomy. If both packets list the same restoration, exit, pilot, licensing, or other routes in parallel, the exercise is probably too easy. Distribute complementary resources and constraints instead, so negotiators must assemble the routes themselves.

## Preserve Commercial Soundness

Define a sound agreement as one that is:

- within the representatives' authority or expressly conditional on approval;
- financeable under the known cash, revenue, asset, and timing constraints;
- legally and contractually implementable on the assumed facts;
- operationally specific enough to perform;
- durable against known business risks;
- preferable to each party's realistic BATNA;
- attentive to affected third parties, rights, and dependencies.

Require implementation terms when the bargain depends on future conduct: milestones, metrics, reporting, audits, cure periods, review dates, decision rules, and consequences of failure.

## Control Difficulty

Increase difficulty through value differences, uncertainty, sequencing, and credible mistrust. Do not increase difficulty through missing definitions, accidental arithmetic ambiguity, obscure law, contradictory dates, or facts no participant could know.

Use clarification forecasting before finalization. Distinguish:

- accidental ambiguity that must be repaired;
- legal uncertainty participants should argue about;
- factual uncertainty a contingent agreement can manage;
- information one party must elicit during mediation.

## Ensure Originality

Use examples only to learn structure. Do not reuse distinctive combinations of industry, parties, events, products, amounts, timelines, titles, or settlement opportunities. Change the economic engine, not merely names.

Record in `validation.originality_notes`:

- which structural archetype was used;
- at least three material differences from any reference example;
- a short phrase-overlap check;
- confirmation that all prose and numerical patterns are original.

Do not bundle or reproduce source examples in the skill unless redistribution is authorized.
