# Packet Templates

## General Information Packet

Use this order unless a supplied packet establishes another style:

1. Original title and neutral subtitle.
2. Commercial setting, market, and relevant entities.
3. Relationship history and governing agreements.
4. Objective chronology.
5. Precipitating event and consequences.
6. Each side's public account and requested relief.
7. Negotiation history and present impasse.
8. Mediation setting, representatives, and authority.

Write neutrally. Use approximate parity in the treatment of the parties. Include numbers necessary to understand the dispute but exclude hidden targets, internal losses, financing limits, unannounced alternatives, board pressures, and intended trades.

## Confidential Packet

Label the document prominently:

`CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION - [PARTY NAME / ROLE]`

Use this order:

1. Internal assessment of legal and commercial strength.
2. True interests and protected interests.
3. Financial or operational constraints.
4. Targets and reservation conditions, including uncertainty.
5. Private risks, BATNA, and WATNA.
6. Resources, rights, or opportunities available to trade.
7. Beliefs and suspicions, labeled as such.
8. Authority and approvals.
9. Adjacent opportunities and implementation concerns.

Use second person by default. Do not number the intended settlement packages or state that a specific package is the answer. Do not include the other side's secrets.

Do not end both packets with matching lists of possible deal structures. A party may have one incomplete internal idea, but the packet should primarily disclose ingredients, boundaries, authority, and questions. Require participants to construct packages through information exchange.

## Author-Only Validation Report

Include:

- entry route and source material;
- iteration count;
- hard validation results;
- information-firewall results;
- semantic scorecard;
- candidate package count and short author-only descriptions;
- high-, medium-, and low-severity defects by iteration;
- unresolved judgment calls;
- final pass/fail conclusion.

## Clarification-Risk Report

List likely participant questions. For each, classify the response:

- `answer_in_packet`: cite the location.
- `repair_before_release`: identify the accidental ambiguity.
- `facts_sufficient`: explain why the uncertainty is deliberately legal or inferential.
- `information_to_elicit`: identify which party may disclose it in mediation.
- `contingency_candidate`: explain how a settlement could manage the uncertainty.

Do not use `facts_sufficient` to conceal a broken calculation or missing commercial premise.

## Style

- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.
- Keep invented jurisdictions and laws simple unless the legal framework is the teaching objective.
- Use consistent currencies, units, dates, defined terms, and pronouns.
- Make the documents readable in one sitting.
- Preserve professional realism without unnecessary boilerplate.
- Use clear paragraphing rather than dense lists in participant packets.
