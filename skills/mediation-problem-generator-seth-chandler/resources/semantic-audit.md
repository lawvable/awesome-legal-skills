# Semantic Audit and Exit Rubric

Run every section on every revision. Record findings in `validation-report.md` and `validation.audit_history`.

## Severity

- **High:** makes a sound settlement impossible or obvious; leaks confidential information; contradicts an objective fact; breaks the math; gives a party impossible authority; makes a candidate package unlawful, unaffordable, or unavailable.
- **Medium:** creates material confusion, imbalance, unrealistic behavior, weak implementation, or an avoidable clarification request.
- **Low:** style, emphasis, minor redundancy, or nonmaterial ambiguity.

## 1. Objective Consistency

- Do all names, roles, dates, locations, assets, contracts, and events match?
- Does every objective fact have one meaning across all packets?
- Are differing accounts labeled as beliefs, allegations, or legal uncertainty?
- Does the chronology allow every event to occur in the stated order?
- Does every party know only what it could reasonably know?

## 2. Numerical Integrity

- Recompute every total, percentage, valuation, installment, loss, and ownership share.
- Test cash timing, not merely nominal totals.
- Distinguish revenue, profit, cost, cash, valuation, damages, and turnover.
- Confirm that every candidate payment is financeable or properly contingent.
- Confirm that non-cash consideration is not double-counted.

## 3. Information Firewall

- Does the public packet reveal any target, reservation condition, private distress, hidden alternative, or intended trade?
- Does either confidential packet reveal the other party's secret as fact?
- Does a belief accidentally become an objective assertion?
- Can a party infer the exact intended package without eliciting information?
- Do the two packets contain mirrored option menus, parallel package labels, or the same sequence of proposed routes?

## 4. Incentives and Alternatives

- Is each BATNA realistic rather than artificially catastrophic?
- Is each WATNA meaningfully worse but plausible?
- Does each party have a reason not to capitulate immediately?
- Is at least one sound settlement preferable to each BATNA?
- Does either party dominate on legal rights, money, time, information, and alternatives simultaneously?

## 5. Narrow-Window Calibration

Classify the corridor:

1. `impossible` - no sound package.
2. `brittle` - only one package or one hidden number works.
3. `narrow_sound` - two to four materially different sound packages require linked concessions and information exchange.
4. `too_easy` - obvious matching interests or generous overlap makes agreement nearly automatic.

Require `narrow_sound` to pass.

For each candidate package, test:

- authority;
- legal availability;
- financing and cash timing;
- operational feasibility;
- third-party rights;
- implementation and monitoring;
- performance under downside scenarios;
- comparison with both BATNAs;
- use of at least two linked concessions;
- material difference from other packages.

## 6. Integrative Quality

- Are at least three bargaining dimensions available?
- Are at least two cross-trades grounded in different valuations?
- Is at least one contingency available to manage uncertainty?
- Does each side control something the other values?
- Are protected interests respected rather than simply ignored?
- Does the exercise reward diagnosis, option generation, and package bargaining?
- Must participants assemble the package themselves, rather than select from matching menus embedded in both packets?

## 7. Legal and Commercial Plausibility

- Is the legal uncertainty genuine but not dispositive?
- Does the problem avoid requiring specialized law that participants were not given?
- Are contracts, governance, IP, financing, and remedies described plausibly?
- Would rational organizations behave roughly as described?
- Can representatives implement what they may agree?

## 8. Pedagogical Balance

- Does each side have strengths, vulnerabilities, and room to move?
- Is neither side cast as the obvious villain?
- Does each packet offer roughly comparable strategic depth?
- Can skilled negotiators outperform unskilled negotiators without possessing outside knowledge?
- Is there room for creative packages without unlimited improvisation?

## 9. Clarification Forecast

Simulate at least ten likely participant questions. Repair accidental ambiguity. Retain deliberate legal or factual uncertainty only when it serves negotiation and the intended response can fairly be `facts sufficient` or `information to elicit`.

## 10. Originality

- Search for distinctive phrase overlap against supplied examples when available.
- Compare industry, relationship, precipitating event, legal issue, economic engine, and settlement opportunities.
- Require at least three material structural differences from every reference case.
- Reject renamed or cosmetically altered copies.

## Role-Limited Audit Questions

Ask each party-limited reviewer:

1. What are your five most important interests?
2. What are your hard constraints and uncertainties?
3. What information must you obtain?
4. What is your realistic BATNA?
5. Propose at least two sound packages and explain why you could authorize them.
6. Which apparently attractive agreement would be unsound?
7. What questions would you seek to clarify?

Ask the mediator-limited reviewer:

1. Is the public dispute coherent and balanced?
2. Which issues appear distributive, integrative, or contingent?
3. Does the public packet prematurely reveal the solution?
4. What process would surface the hidden value?

Ask the full-package reviewer:

1. Are all candidate packages sound under both packets?
2. Are two to four packages materially different?
3. Is the corridor narrow but real?
4. Are any secrets leaked or impossible knowledge assigned?
5. Which single defect most threatens the exercise?

## Exit Rule

Pass only when:

- deterministic validation has no errors;
- high-severity findings are zero;
- the last two iterations report zero new high-severity findings;
- the corridor is `narrow_sound`;
- both party-limited reviews find at least two plausible packages;
- originality and clarification checks pass.

After five unsuccessful iterations, report the unresolved design conflict and request an author decision.
