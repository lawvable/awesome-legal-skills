# Foundational texts — Special Panels Timor-Leste

## 1. UNTAET Regulation No. 1999/1 (27 November 1999)

**On the Authority of the Transitional Administration in East Timor.**

**Function:** establishes UNTAET's plenary authority. **Section 3** provides for **continuity of pre-existing law** — the laws applicable in East Timor as of 25 October 1999 (largely Indonesian law) remain in force except where inconsistent with UNTAET's mandate or with international human rights standards.

This is the **constitutional foundation** of UNTAET's legal authority — UNTAET acts as the sovereign authority of East Timor between 1999 and the formal independence of Timor-Leste on 20 May 2002.

## 2. UNTAET Regulation No. 2000/11 (6 March 2000)

**On the Organization of Courts in East Timor.**

**Function:** establishes the **District Court of Dili** and other District Courts in East Timor. **Section 10** specifically provides for the establishment of **panels with exclusive jurisdiction over serious criminal offences** within the District Court of Dili.

**Section 10.4** clarifies: "*The establishment of panels with exclusive jurisdiction over serious criminal offences shall not preclude the jurisdiction of an international tribunal for East Timor over these crimes.*" — reflecting the parallel debate over whether an international tribunal should be established (which never happened).

## 3. UNTAET Regulation No. 2000/15 (6 June 2000)

**On the Establishment of Panels with Exclusive Jurisdiction over Serious Criminal Offences.**

**The operative substantive and jurisdictional instrument of the SPSC.** Key sections:

- **Section 1 — Definitions.**
- **Section 2 — Jurisdiction.**
  - 2.1: Subject-matter jurisdiction = serious criminal offences = genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity, murder, sexual offences, torture
  - 2.2-2.5: Temporal, territorial, and personal jurisdiction
- **Section 3 — Composition of Panels.**
  - "*The Panels shall be composed of two international judges and one East Timorese judge.*"
- **Section 4 — Genocide.** Definition tracking the Genocide Convention 1948.
- **Section 5 — Crimes against humanity.** Definition tracking Rome Statute Article 7 (knowingly part of widespread or systematic attack against civilian population) — including murder, extermination, enslavement, deportation, imprisonment, torture, rape and sexual violence, persecution, enforced disappearance, apartheid, other inhumane acts.
- **Section 6 — War crimes.** Defined in relation to Geneva Conventions 1949 and Additional Protocols and customary international law.
- **Section 7 — Torture.** Defined per Convention against Torture 1984.
- **Section 8 — Murder.** As an underlying SPSC offence (distinct from murder as crime against humanity or war crime).
- **Section 9 — Sexual offences.** Including rape, sexual slavery, enforced prostitution, forced pregnancy, enforced sterilization, other sexual violence.
- **Section 10 — Universal jurisdiction provisions.**
- **Section 12 — Statute of limitations.** No statute of limitations for international crimes within the SPSC's jurisdiction.
- **Section 14 — Modes of individual criminal responsibility.** Commission (including joint commission and JCE), planning, ordering, instigating, aiding and abetting, attempting.
- **Section 15 — Command/superior responsibility.** Classical formulation — military and non-military superiors held responsible for crimes committed by subordinates where the superior knew or should have known and failed to prevent or punish.
- **Section 19 — *Ne bis in idem*.**

## 4. UNTAET Regulation No. 2000/16 (6 June 2000)

**On the Organization of the Public Prosecution Service in East Timor.**

**Function:** establishes the **Serious Crimes Unit (SCU)** under the **Deputy Prosecutor General for Serious Crimes (DPGSC)** within the Office of the Prosecutor General. The SCU is the prosecuting authority for SPSC cases.

## 5. UNTAET Regulation No. 2000/30 (25 September 2000)

**On the Transitional Rules of Criminal Procedure.**

**Function:** defines proceedings before the Special Panels — investigation, indictment, trial, appeal, plea negotiation.

Key sections:
- **Section 29A** — plea negotiation provisions (significant in SPSC practice — many defendants entered plea-based dispositions)
- Witness protection provisions
- Defense rights provisions

## 6. UNTAET Regulation No. 2001/10 (13 July 2001)

**On the Establishment of a Commission for Reception, Truth and Reconciliation in East Timor.**

Establishes the **CAVR (Comissão de Acolhimento, Verdade e Reconciliação de Timor-Leste)** — the truth and reconciliation commission.

**Section 23** establishes **Community Reconciliation Procedures (CRPs)** for lesser crimes — procedurally articulated with the SPSC: cases involving "less serious" crimes (not crimes against humanity, genocide, sexual offences, or murder requiring SPSC prosecution) could be referred from the SCU to CAVR for community-based reconciliation hearings.

## 7. Memorandum of Understanding between UNTAET and the Government of the Republic of Indonesia

**Signed:** 5-6 April 2000.

**Function:** on cooperation in legal, judicial, and human rights-related matters — provides for enforcement of warrants of arrest and transfer of accused persons across the Indonesia-Timor-Leste border.

**Practical effect:** **largely inoperative**. Indonesia claimed the MOU required Indonesian government ratification (which was never given), and that the agreement was limited to the period of UN administration in East Timor. **Indonesia did not effectively cooperate** with the SPSC. Most senior indictees — including General Wiranto, indicted in 2003 — remained in Indonesia and never stood trial.

This **non-cooperation by Indonesia** is the **structural fact** that produced the impunity at the senior commander level which defines the SPSC's institutional legacy.

## 8. Constitution of the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste (20 May 2002)

The constitution of independent Timor-Leste — adopted on the date of independence. **Article 165 (Reception of previous law)** provides for continuity of UNTAET-era law to the extent compatible with the new Constitution. This is the legal basis on which the SPSC continued to operate post-independence (2002-2006).

## 9. Indonesian Penal Code (KUHP) — applied subsidiarily

As applicable through Section 3 of UNTAET Regulation 1999/1 (continuity of pre-1999 law). Used for ordinary criminal offences not displaced by UNTAET Regulations.

## Working with foundational texts in project knowledge

Recommended workflow:
1. Add **UNTAET Regulation 2000/15** for substantive law questions.
2. Add **UNTAET Regulation 2000/30** for procedural questions.
3. Add **UNTAET Regulation 2000/11** for institutional questions about the Dili District Court.
4. Add **UN Security Council resolutions** establishing UNTAET (notably UNSC Res 1272 of 25 October 1999) for the broader institutional context.
5. Add **CAVR Final Report ("Chega!")** for historical and contextual reference.

If not in project knowledge, retrieve from legal-tools.org or UN archives.
