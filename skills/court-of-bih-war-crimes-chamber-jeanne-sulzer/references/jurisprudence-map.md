# Jurisprudence map — Special Panels Timor-Leste

A topic-by-topic map of SPSC's principal holdings.

## 1. Crimes against humanity — 1999 post-referendum violence

The SPSC's principal substantive jurisprudence concerns **crimes against humanity** committed during the 1999 East Timor violence. Major cases:

- **Joni Marques et al. (Los Palos case)** — Judgment Case No. 09/2000, 11 December 2001. Conviction of 10 defendants for crimes against humanity (murder, torture, persecution) for the September 1999 violence in the Lautém district. **The first major SPSC judgment establishing crimes against humanity in the 1999 context.**

- **Joseph Leki** — Judgment Case No. 05/2000, 11 June 2001. Murder during the 1999 violence.

- **Lolotoe case** (Cardoso, Mau Bere, Sufa) — Multiple judgments. Detention, torture, sexual violence in Lolotoe (Bobonaro district) during the 1999 violence.

- **José Cardoso Fereira** — Judgment Case No. 04/2001, 5 April 2003. Sexual offences and torture (Lolotoe case context).

- **Joseph Marcal Tilman** — multiple judgments on Bobonaro district crimes.

- **Aparicio Guterres et al.** — Liquiçá Church massacre proceedings (April 1999).

- **Eurico Guterres** — proceedings concerning the Aitarak militia leader. Note: Eurico Guterres was tried separately at the Indonesian Ad Hoc Tribunal in Jakarta (where he was convicted, the conviction was reversed in 2006, then partially restored in 2008). The SPSC did NOT try Eurico Guterres at full trial — he was indicted but remained in Indonesia.

## 2. Sexual violence as crime against humanity and war crime

Significant body of case-law including:
- **José Cardoso Fereira** — sexual offences in Lolotoe (5 April 2003)
- **Joao Sarmento Domingos Mendoca** — sexual offences and persecution
- Multiple cases involving sexual violence by Indonesian military and pro-Indonesia militias against Timorese women in 1999

The SPSC jurisprudence on sexual violence is consistent with ICTY *Kunarac et al.* and ICTR *Akayesu* — recognising rape, sexual slavery, and enforced sexual violence as crimes against humanity and war crimes.

## 3. Command/superior responsibility

The SPSC applied **command/superior responsibility under Section 15 of UNTAET Regulation 2000/15** in classical formulation. Applied to:
- **Militia commanders** (Timorese pro-Indonesia militia leaders — tried defendants)
- **Indonesian military commanders** (named in SCU indictments — never tried due to Indonesia's non-cooperation)

The most institutionally significant **indictment** under command responsibility was:

- **Wiranto et al. Indictment** — SCU Indictment of 24 February 2003. Indicted General Wiranto (former Indonesian Minister of Defense in 1999), Major General Zacky Anwar Makarim, and other senior Indonesian military commanders for crimes against humanity under command/superior responsibility. **Wiranto was never tried** — he remained in Indonesia. The indictment is significant for its **doctrinal articulation of command responsibility** in the specific factual configuration of the 1999 violence, but it produced no judgment.

## 4. Joint criminal enterprise (JCE)

Applied in the **Los Palos** judgment and subsequent cases. SPSC's JCE construction followed ICTY *Tadić* (Appeals Judgment 15 July 1999), with adjustments for the specific context of Indonesian military and pro-Indonesia militia coordination.

## 5. Plea negotiation under Section 29A

UNTAET Regulation 2000/30 Section 29A provided for **plea negotiation**. Many SPSC cases were resolved through plea-based dispositions — the defendant admitted facts, the prosecution presented evidence, the court evaluated and entered conviction.

This is a **distinctive procedural feature** of the SPSC compared to other internationalised tribunals (ECCC, ICTY, ICTR — which had no comparable plea-bargaining framework or more limited applications). The plea-negotiation provisions reflected **practical necessity** given the SPSC's massive caseload and limited resources.

## 6. Murder as ordinary crime alongside international crimes

UNTAET Regulation 2000/15 Section 8 included **murder** within the SPSC's jurisdiction as an ordinary crime alongside international crimes (genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity).

This is **unusual** in internationalised criminal justice. Most other internationalised tribunals limited their jurisdiction to international crimes only. The SPSC's inclusion of murder reflected **practical necessity** — the need to prosecute 1999 violence even where the **chapeau elements of crimes against humanity** (widespread or systematic attack against civilian population) might not be established in a specific isolated incident.

## 7. The Indonesian Penal Code (KUHP) — subsidiary application

For offences not covered by UNTAET Regulation 2000/15, the SPSC applied the **Indonesian Penal Code (KUHP)** as continued under UNTAET Regulation 1999/1 (continuity of pre-1999 law). This produced a **hybrid substantive law regime** — international crimes under UNTAET 2000/15, ordinary crimes under KUHP.

## 8. Sentencing — maximum 25 years

UNTAET Regulation 2000/15 set the **maximum sentence at 25 years' imprisonment** for most offences. **No death penalty** — UNTAET excluded the death penalty as inconsistent with international human rights standards (UNTAET Reg. 1999/1 Section 3 preservation of pre-existing law was qualified by international human rights compatibility).

This is distinct from the Indonesian Penal Code's available sentencing range and reflects UNTAET's transitional administration prerogative.

## 9. The Wiranto Indictment of 24 February 2003 — a notable non-trial

**SCU Indictment of 24 February 2003** against General Wiranto and six others for crimes against humanity under command/superior responsibility. The indictment is the **most prominent unrealized prosecution** in the Serious Crimes process — Wiranto, former Indonesian Minister of Defense, never stood trial. The indictment is institutionally significant as a documented record of the senior-level command structure responsible for the 1999 violence, even though it produced no judicial finding.

In 2004, the SPSC reportedly issued **arrest warrants** for Wiranto and others, but Indonesia did not execute them. Wiranto subsequently ran in Indonesian presidential elections and held senior Indonesian government positions.

This case is the **iconic example of the SPSC's structural impunity at the senior commander level**.

## 10. Relationship with CAVR Community Reconciliation Procedures

UNTAET Regulation 2001/10 Section 23 provided that the **CAVR Community Reconciliation Procedures (CRPs)** could address "less serious" crimes — crimes not constituting genocide, crimes against humanity, sexual offences, or murder requiring SPSC prosecution. CRPs involved:
- Community-based hearings with the perpetrator, victims, and community
- Symbolic and material reparations
- Reintegration of perpetrators into the community

Approximately **1,371 cases** were resolved through CRPs between 2002 and 2004. This **complementary mechanism** for lesser crimes is one of the SPSC system's distinctive features — and a model later considered for other transitional justice contexts.

## How to use this map

1. Identify the user's substantive question and locate the relevant section above.
2. For a precise legal finding, identify the specific case and judgment.
3. Verify the cited passage through `verification-workflow.md` — locate the actual judgment text via JSMP, Berkeley, legal-tools.org.
4. Cite using the conventions in `citation-format.md`.
5. Acknowledge the **structural impunity** context where the case touches on senior commander responsibility — Indonesia's non-cooperation is part of the institutional record.

The UNTAET Regulations, the actual SPSC and SCU records, and the CAVR Final Report are the territory. This file is a map of them.
