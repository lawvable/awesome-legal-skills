# Verification workflow — Special Panels Timor-Leste

## The principle

No case-specific citation appears in an output until verified against an authoritative source in the current conversation. Foundational instruments (UNTAET Regulations 1999/1, 2000/11, 2000/15, 2000/16, 2000/30; CAVR Final Report) may be cited from project knowledge.

## The fallback ladder

1. **JSMP archive** (jsmp.tl) — Judicial System Monitoring Programme; first point of entry for procedural records and trial reports
2. **UC Berkeley Human Rights Center** digital archive — most comprehensive single repository of SPSC judgments
3. **UN UNTAET archives** — for UNTAET Regulations and UN Secretary-General reports
4. **legal-tools.org** — ICC Legal Tools Database mirror
5. **ETAN** (etan.org) — comprehensive NGO archive
6. **ICTJ reports** — Caitlin Reiger 2006, Megan Hirst and Howard Varney 2005
7. **Academic literature** — Linton, de Bertodano, Strohmeyer, Cohen — for context only, never authority for what the SPSC has held
8. **CAVR Final Report ("Chega!")** — for historical context
9. **Ask the user** if nothing verifies

## Anticipate longer verification times

Unlike active institutions with comprehensive official websites, the SPSC archive is **distributed across multiple repositories** and **partly fragile**. Common verification challenges:
- A judgment may exist on legal-tools.org but with limited paragraph numbering visibility
- A judgment may be referenced in JSMP trial reports but the full text may be at Berkeley
- An SCU indictment may be in ETAN's archives but the original SCU files are at Berkeley
- Older judgments (2001-2003) may have less digital indexing than later ones (2004-2006)

**Allow more verification time than for active institutions.** When a Tier 1 source cannot be located, **document the search attempt** in your response and consider whether partial verification (existence + Tier 2 description) suffices for the proposition being supported.

## Capture these fields

For every verified citation:
- **Case name** — formal *Prosecutor v. [Defendant(s)]* designation
- **Case number** — SPSC convention (typically XX/YYYY)
- **Document type** — Indictment (SCU), Judgment, Appeal Judgment, Decision
- **Panel** — Special Panels for Serious Crimes, Dili District Court (or Court of Appeal of Timor-Leste for appeals)
- **Date**
- **Paragraph or page**
- **Language and translation chain** — SPSC documents may exist in English, Indonesian, Portuguese, or Tetum versions; identify which version you are citing

## SPSC-specific traps

### Trap 1 — SPSC is NOT the Indonesian Ad Hoc Tribunal

There were **two parallel judicial processes** addressing the 1999 East Timor violence:

1. **SPSC** — at the Dili District Court, established by UNTAET. Tried lower-rank Timorese militia members (mostly) and a small number of Indonesian military personnel who happened to be in Timor-Leste. **Hybrid composition (2 international + 1 Timorese judges).**

2. **Indonesian Ad Hoc Human Rights Court on East Timor** — at the Central Jakarta District Court, established by Indonesian Presidential Decree No. 53/2001. Tried senior Indonesian military and police commanders (notably General Wiranto's subordinates). **Indonesian national court** with all-Indonesian judges. Most convictions reversed on appeal; the proceedings were widely criticised as inadequate (see David Cohen, *Intended to Fail*, ICTJ 2003).

**Common error:** conflating these two distinct judicial processes. A "Wiranto trial" reference is almost certainly the **Indonesian Ad Hoc Tribunal** (the SPSC indicted Wiranto in 2003 but never tried him).

### Trap 2 — SPSC is NOT a "proposed international tribunal"

A proposed international tribunal for East Timor was discussed at the UN in 1999-2000 but **never established**. The SPSC was the **practical solution** in lieu of an international tribunal. **Section 10.4 of UNTAET Regulation 2000/11** explicitly preserved the possibility of a future international tribunal — but none was ever created.

**Common error:** referring to "the international tribunal for East Timor" or "the East Timor international tribunal" as if it existed. The accurate designation is **the Special Panels for Serious Crimes (SPSC) at the Dili District Court** — a hybrid jurisdiction integrated into the national judicial system, not an international tribunal.

### Trap 3 — Structural impunity at senior commander level

The SPSC tried approximately **88 defendants out of 391 persons indicted** (across 95 indictments). The vast majority of indictees — particularly senior Indonesian military commanders, some 339 in all — remained in Indonesia and were not extradited. The Indonesia-UNTAET MOU of April 2000 was **never effectively operationalised**.

**Common error:** characterising the SPSC's case law as "the comprehensive judicial response to the 1999 violence". It is not. The SPSC's record is one of **partial accountability primarily at the lower ranks**, with **structural impunity at the senior commander level**. Any substantive analysis of SPSC jurisprudence should acknowledge this institutional reality.

### Trap 4 — SPSC closed in 2006; the SCIT successor mechanism (2008-2012) had limited mandate

The SPSC was **discontinued on 20 May 2006** when the UN decided not to extend the Serious Crimes process under the new UNMIT mandate. From **2008 to 2012**, the **Serious Crimes Investigation Team (SCIT)** operated under UNMIT to **complete investigations** of pending cases — but the SCIT had **no prosecutorial authority**. SCIT findings could only be acted upon through Timor-Leste's national prosecutorial system, which was under-resourced and politically constrained.

**Common error:** treating the SCIT as a continuation of the SPSC's prosecutorial function. It was not. The SCIT was an investigative completion mechanism, not a continuing prosecutorial body.

### Trap 5 — CAVR is distinct from SPSC

The **CAVR (Comissão de Acolhimento, Verdade e Reconciliação)** is a **truth and reconciliation commission**, not a criminal jurisdiction. Established by UNTAET Regulation 2001/10 of 13 July 2001. Its **Community Reconciliation Procedures (CRPs)** addressed "less serious" crimes through community-based hearings, in articulation with — but distinct from — SPSC criminal prosecutions.

**Common error:** conflating CAVR findings (a truth commission's historical record) with SPSC judgments (a court's legal determinations). The CAVR Final Report ("Chega!" of October 2005) is **foundational historical documentation** but not authoritative as legal precedent.

### Trap 6 — UNTAET vs UNMIT vs UNMISET vs UNAMET — UN mission acronyms

Multiple UN missions in East Timor / Timor-Leste over time. Distinguish:
- **UNAMET** — UN Mission in East Timor (May-October 1999) — the referendum mission
- **INTERFET** — International Force for East Timor (September 1999 - February 2000) — Australian-led military force
- **UNTAET** — UN Transitional Administration in East Timor (October 1999 - May 2002) — the **founding administration of the SPSC**
- **UNMISET** — UN Mission of Support in East Timor (May 2002 - May 2005) — post-independence transition support
- **UNOTIL** — UN Office in Timor-Leste (May 2005 - August 2006) — succeeded UNMISET; under UNOTIL the SCU was wound down
- **UNMIT** — UN Integrated Mission in Timor-Leste (August 2006 - December 2012) — under which the SCIT operated 2008-2012

**Common error:** misattributing institutional acts to the wrong UN mission. **The SPSC was created by UNTAET** (the founding administration). Subsequent UN missions inherited and gradually wound down the Serious Crimes process.

### Trap 7 — "East Timor" vs "Timor-Leste" — naming and dates

- **Before 20 May 2002**: technically still "East Timor" (the territory under UNTAET administration, not yet an independent state)
- **From 20 May 2002**: **Timor-Leste** (the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste)

The SPSC operated from 2000 through 2006 — straddling the transition. Documents from the UNTAET period (2000-2002) typically refer to "East Timor"; documents from the post-independence period (2002-2006) typically refer to "Timor-Leste". In contemporary scholarship, **Timor-Leste is the preferred designation**; "East Timor" is historical.

## Translation discipline

SPSC proceedings used multiple languages. **The court typically issued judgments in English**, with translation to Tetum, Indonesian or Portuguese for service on parties. **Where judgment text is cited, identify the language version cited and any translation chain.** The English versions on legal-tools.org and Berkeley are typically the court's own English text — but verify on a case-by-case basis.

## Partial verification is acceptable if disclosed

If you can confirm a case existed and was tried but cannot locate the specific judgment text, **disclose this**. The SPSC's archival fragility means partial verification with transparent disclosure is sometimes the best achievable; **silent overstatement is not acceptable**.
