# Evidence — MBE High-Yield & Traps

> The MBE tests the **Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE)**. This file lists the most-tested rules, classic distractor traps, and the FRE-vs-California (CEC) splits — critical because CA essays test the CEC. For full black-letter rules see `references/essays/evidence.md` and `references/standard-paragraphs/evidence.md`.

---

## Most-tested MBE topics

- **Relevance — FRE 401/403:** Evidence is relevant if it has any tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable; court may exclude if probative value is substantially outweighed by danger of unfair prejudice.
- **Character evidence — FRE 404(a):** Propensity character evidence is generally inadmissible; criminal defendant may open the door; victim's character admissible in limited circumstances; civil cases bar character-for-conduct almost entirely.
- **MIMIC — FRE 404(b):** Prior bad acts admissible for non-propensity purposes: Motive, Intent, Mistake (absence of), Identity, Common scheme/plan (mnemonic: MIMIC).
- **Methods of proving character — FRE 405:** When character is admissible, prove by reputation or opinion; specific acts allowed only when character is an element of a claim or defense.
- **Hearsay definition — FRE 801:** Out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted; non-verbal assertive conduct is a statement; non-assertive conduct is not hearsay.
- **Non-hearsay party-opponent admissions — FRE 801(d)(2):** Statement by opposing party in their individual capacity, adoptive, authorized, agent/employee, or co-conspirator — these are defined as non-hearsay, not an exception.
- **Prior statements of witnesses — FRE 801(d)(1):** Prior inconsistent statement made under oath in a prior proceeding = non-hearsay; prior consistent statement offered to rebut charge of fabrication = non-hearsay; prior identification = non-hearsay.
- **Present sense impression — FRE 803(1):** Statement describing an event made while or immediately after perceiving it; declarant need not be unavailable.
- **Excited utterance — FRE 803(2):** Statement relating to a startling event made while under the stress of excitement; can be minutes or even hours later if stress persists.
- **State of mind — FRE 803(3):** Statement of then-existing mental, emotional, or physical condition; Hillmon doctrine: forward-looking intent admissible to show subsequent conduct.
- **Medical diagnosis/treatment — FRE 803(4):** Statements made for diagnosis or treatment, including cause if pertinent to treatment; need not be made to treating physician.
- **Business records — FRE 803(6):** Records of regularly conducted activity made at or near the time by someone with knowledge, pursuant to regular practice; foundation via custodian or certification.
- **Public records — FRE 803(8):** Records of public agencies setting out activities, matters observed (law enforcement records limited against criminal defendants), or factual findings from investigations.
- **Unavailability exceptions — FRE 804:** Declarant unavailable (refuses, privilege, death, illness, absent): former testimony, dying declaration, statement against interest, personal/family history, forfeiture by wrongdoing.
- **Dying declaration — FRE 804(b)(2):** Belief of impending death; in FRE, limited to homicide and civil cases; declarant need not actually die.
- **Statement against interest — FRE 804(b)(3):** Statement so contrary to declarant's pecuniary, proprietary, or penal interest that a reasonable person would not have made it unless believing it true; penal interest offered to exculpate accused requires corroboration.
- **Confrontation Clause:** Crawford v. Washington — testimonial out-of-court statements by unavailable declarant are barred unless defendant had prior opportunity to cross-examine; primary purpose test distinguishes testimonial (formal police interrogation) from non-testimonial (emergency 911 call).
- **Impeachment — FRE 607/608/609:** Any party may impeach; prior convictions (felony or crime of dishonesty); bad acts without conviction via cross only (no extrinsic evidence); bias, prior inconsistent statements, contradiction.
- **Expert opinion — FRE 702 (Daubert):** Testimony based on sufficient facts, reliable principles/methods, reliably applied; judge is gatekeeper; experts may rely on inadmissible evidence if reasonably relied on by experts in the field.
- **Lay opinion — FRE 701:** Rationally based on perception, helpful to understanding, not based on specialized knowledge; common examples: speed, intoxication, handwriting, identity.
- **Authentication — FRE 901:** Condition precedent to admissibility; sufficient evidence to support finding that item is what proponent claims; self-authenticating documents under FRE 902.
- **Best evidence / original writing rule — FRE 1002:** To prove content of a writing, recording, or photograph, original required; duplicates generally admissible (FRE 1003); rule only triggered when content itself is at issue.
- **Privileges:** FRE does not codify specific privileges — federal common law applies (attorney-client, spousal testimonial, marital communications); in federal question cases, federal privilege law governs; in diversity cases, state privilege law governs.

## Classic distractor traps

- **"It's hearsay!" when it isn't:** Watch for statements offered not for their truth but for effect on listener, verbal acts (operative words of a contract/gift/defamation), legally operative language, or circumstantial evidence of declarant's state of mind — none are hearsay.
- **Missing a party-opponent admission:** FRE 801(d)(2) admissions are labeled non-hearsay, not an exception — a distractor will say the statement is inadmissible hearsay when it is actually a party admission coming in without any unavailability requirement.
- **Present sense impression vs. excited utterance:** PSI requires contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous description; EU requires stress of excitement but tolerates a longer time gap and need not describe the event precisely. Mixing the two elements is a common wrong answer.
- **Prior inconsistent statement:** Only non-hearsay under FRE 801(d)(1)(A) if made under oath in a prior proceeding; if not under oath, it comes in only to impeach (not as substantive evidence) — a frequent trap switches substantive vs. impeachment use.
- **Prior consistent statement timing trap:** FRE 801(d)(1)(B) requires the consistent statement to predate the alleged motive to fabricate or improper influence; if made after the motive arose, it is inadmissible as substantive evidence.
- **Character propensity vs. MIMIC:** Wrong answers will classify a 404(b) prior-act offering as inadmissible propensity; correct answer identifies the specific permissible non-propensity purpose and notes the court may still apply 403 balancing.
- **Dying declaration scope:** FRE limits dying declarations to homicide prosecutions and civil cases — a wrong answer admits one in a robbery prosecution; CA goes further and allows them in any criminal case.
- **Statement against interest vs. party admission:** A non-party declarant's self-inculpatory statement requires unavailability (FRE 804); a party's own admission does not — mixing these is a high-frequency trap.
- **Lay vs. expert opinion:** A witness testifying from personal observation (speed of car, apparent intoxication) is lay under FRE 701 even if the witness is a doctor; a distractor will wrongly demand expert foundation.
- **Best evidence rule overreach:** The rule only applies when the content of the document is directly at issue; a witness testifying from memory about an event that was also recorded is NOT barred by FRE 1002.
- **Daubert vs. Frye on the MBE:** The MBE follows Daubert (FRE 702 reliability/gatekeeping); a distractor will apply a Frye general-acceptance standard — wrong on the MBE.
- **Spousal privileges:** Two distinct privileges — spousal testimonial (spouse refuses to testify against other spouse; holder is witness-spouse in federal courts) vs. marital communications (either spouse holds, covers confidential communications, survives divorce); conflating them is a trap.

## FRE vs. California (CEC) — keep two answers straight

- **Balancing test:** FRE 403 excludes evidence when probative value is *substantially outweighed* by unfair prejudice. CEC §352 uses the same language but California courts apply it more frequently in practice. **MBE = FRE 403; CA essay = CEC §352.**
- **Proposition 8 / Truth-in-Evidence (Cal. Const. Art. I §28(f)(2)):** In criminal cases, all relevant evidence is admissible in California unless excluded by the U.S. Constitution, a federal statute, or specific CEC provisions. This abolishes many common-law exclusions for criminal defendants. No FRE equivalent.
- **Physician-patient privilege:** California recognizes a broad physician-patient privilege (CEC §990 et seq.) covering confidential communications for the purpose of diagnosis or treatment. The FRE has no physician-patient privilege — federal common law does not recognize it.
- **Psychotherapist-patient privilege:** Both FRE (federal common law post-Jaffee v. Redmond) and CEC §1010 recognize it, but the California version is broader and more codified.
- **Expert reliability standard:** FRE 702 = Daubert (judge as gatekeeper; reliability of methodology). California = Kelly/Frye (general acceptance in the relevant scientific community) for new scientific techniques, though California has partially narrowed Kelly's scope. **MBE answer: Daubert. CA essay answer: Kelly/Frye for novel science.**
- **Hearsay numbering:** FRE and CEC organize exceptions differently; CEC exceptions start at §1200. Do not cite CEC section numbers on the MBE.
- **Propensity in domestic violence/sexual assault cases:** CEC §1109 allows propensity evidence of prior domestic violence or elder abuse acts in DV/elder-abuse cases; CEC §1108 allows propensity evidence of prior sexual offenses in sexual offense cases. FRE 413–415 are the federal analogues but apply more narrowly. California's CEC §§1108–1109 are broader and more frequently tested on CA essays.
- **Character evidence for victims — rape shield:** FRE 412 bars victim's sexual behavior/predisposition in civil and criminal cases with limited exceptions. CEC §782/1103(c) provides California's rape shield. Substance is similar; cite FRE on MBE, CEC on essay.
- **Business records:** FRE 803(6) vs. CEC §1271 — substantively similar; California requires the custodian or qualified witness to testify (or certify) that the record was made in the ordinary course of business.
- **Adoptive admissions:** FRE 801(d)(2)(B) — party's adoption of another's statement. California treats adoptive admissions as an exception (not a non-hearsay exclusion), but the substantive result is the same.
- **Dying declarations:** FRE 804(b)(2) limits to homicide prosecutions and civil cases. CEC §1242 allows dying declarations in **any** criminal or civil proceeding — broader California rule tested on essays.

## Sample original MBE questions

### Question 1

During Paulina's civil fraud trial, her attorney calls a witness, Malik, who testifies that he overheard the defendant's CFO say to a colleague, "We knew the projections were inflated when we sent them to investors." The defendant objects: "Hearsay."

The court should rule the statement:

- (A) Inadmissible, because it is an out-of-court statement offered for its truth.
- (B) Inadmissible, because the CFO is not a party to the action.
- (C) Admissible, because it is a statement by an agent of a party-opponent made within the scope of employment.
- (D) Admissible, because it qualifies as an excited utterance.

**Correct answer: (C)**

**Rule:** FRE 801(d)(2)(D) — a statement is not hearsay if offered against an opposing party and was made by the party's agent or employee on a matter within the scope of the agency or employment relationship while that relationship existed.

**Application:** The CFO is an agent/employee of the defendant corporation; the statement about the financial projections falls squarely within the CFO's scope of employment; no unavailability required.

**Why each distractor fails:**
- **(A)** Wrong category — this is not hearsay at all under FRE 801(d)(2); it is defined as non-hearsay.
- **(B)** Incorrect — the CFO's party-employer is the defendant; agency attribution applies to the entity party.
- **(D)** No facts support the stress-of-excitement requirement; this is a business conversation, not a response to a startling event.

### Question 2

In a murder prosecution, the government calls Officer Reyes, who testifies that when she arrived at the scene, the victim — lying on the ground with a gunshot wound — grabbed her arm and said, "Dante shot me. I know I'm dying." The victim died two hours later. Defendant objects on hearsay grounds.

The statement is most likely:

- (A) Inadmissible hearsay because the declarant's belief of imminent death is unverified.
- (B) Inadmissible hearsay because the victim did not die immediately after making the statement.
- (C) Admissible as a dying declaration, provided the victim believed death was imminent.
- (D) Admissible as an excited utterance only, because the dying-declaration exception does not apply in homicide cases.

**Correct answer: (C)**

**Rule:** FRE 804(b)(2) — a statement that the declarant made while believing death to be imminent, concerning its cause or circumstances, is admissible in a homicide prosecution or civil case if the declarant is unavailable.

**Application:** The victim's words "I know I'm dying" establish the subjective belief of imminent death; the statement identifies the cause; the victim subsequently died (unavailability satisfied); this is a homicide prosecution — all elements met.

**Why each distractor fails:**
- **(A)** The test is the declarant's subjective belief, not objective medical certainty; "I know I'm dying" is sufficient evidence of that belief.
- **(B)** FRE 804(b)(2) does not require immediate death — only unavailability at time of trial and belief of imminent death at the time of the statement.
- **(D)** Backwards — dying declarations are specifically admissible in homicide prosecutions under FRE 804(b)(2); excited utterance is a separate, available-declarant exception under FRE 803(2).
