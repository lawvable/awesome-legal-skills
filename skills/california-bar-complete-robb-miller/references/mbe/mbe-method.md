# MBE Method — Multiple-Choice Bar Prep

> The MBE is the 200-question multiple-choice half of the bar (California weights the
> written and MBE components 50/50). It tests **seven** subjects with ~national/majority
> law: Civil Procedure, Constitutional Law, Contracts (incl. UCC Art. 2), Criminal Law &
> Procedure, Evidence, Real Property, and Torts. The substance overlaps the essay banks —
> this layer adds MBE-specific **study modes, an original-question protocol, and the
> MBE-vs-California divergences** that trip examinees who studied CA law for the essays.

## Subjects & where the substance lives

The MBE subjects reuse the essay material. For black-letter rules, pull from the issue
banks in `references/essays/<subject>.md` and the memorizable paragraphs in
`references/standard-paragraphs/<subject>.md`. The MBE-specific high-yield rules, classic
distractor traps, and MBE-vs-CA splits are in `references/mbe/<subject>.md`:

`civil-procedure.md` · `constitutional-law.md` · `contracts.md` ·
`criminal-law-procedure.md` · `evidence.md` · `real-property.md` · `torts.md`

> **Critical:** the MBE tests **national/majority** law. California essay answers sometimes
> differ (CEC vs FRE, CA community property, CA criminal statutes, CA civil-procedure rules).
> Each MBE subject file flags these so the student keeps two answers straight: the **MBE
> answer** (majority/federal) and the **CA essay answer**.

## The four study modes

Detect which the student wants from how they ask, then run it. One concept or one question
at a time — keep it conversational, never dump a whole file.

### 1. Teach
"Teach me [topic]" / "walk me through [doctrine]." Give a tight black-letter walkthrough from
the bank, surface the 2–3 most-tested rules, and name the classic traps for that topic. End
by checking understanding with one quick question.

### 2. Quiz (single MCQ)
"Quiz me on [subject]." Generate **one original** NCBE-style question (see protocol below),
wait for the answer, then reveal the correct choice with a full explanation of why it's right
and why each distractor is wrong. Move to the next.

### 3. Timed Drill
"Drill me / timed set of N." Deliver N original questions at exam pace (the MBE allots ~1.8
minutes per question). Hold explanations until the end, then review missed questions first.

### 4. Weak-Area Focus
Track which sub-topics the student misses and preferentially re-test those. Periodically
report a simple accuracy breakdown by subject/sub-topic so they know where to spend time.

## Original-question protocol (IP-safe — never reproduce real exam questions)

All practice questions must be **original**. Never reproduce, paraphrase, or lightly edit
actual NCBE/released MBE questions — that is copyright infringement. Generate fresh fact
patterns instead, following NCBE *style*:

1. **Stem:** a short, novel fact pattern (2–6 sentences) with a single clear call ("The
   plaintiff will most likely…", "The defendant's best defense is…").
2. **Four options (A–D):** one correct answer and three plausible distractors. Good
   distractors embody specific errors — the right rule misapplied, a wrong element, a tempting
   but irrelevant doctrine, or the **California rule where the MBE wants the majority rule**.
3. **One best answer:** MBE questions ask for the *best* answer, not merely a correct one.
4. **Explanation:** after the student answers, state the correct choice, the governing rule
   (from the bank, in original words), why it controls on these facts, and a one-line reason
   each wrong option fails.
5. **Difficulty:** mix straightforward recall with two-step application; flag any question
   that turns on an MBE-vs-CA divergence.

**Question template**
```
Q. [original fact pattern]. [Single call of the question]?
(A) …
(B) …
(C) …
(D) …
```
After the answer:
```
Correct: (X). Rule: [black-letter rule]. Why: [application to these facts].
(A)…wrong because… (B)…wrong because… (C)…wrong because… (D)…wrong because…
[⚠️ MBE vs CA note if relevant]
```

## Scoring & pacing reality

The MBE is scaled; raw accuracy of roughly 60–67% is competitive. Coach the student to (1)
read the call first, (2) eliminate two distractors fast, (3) pick the *best* of the
remaining two, and (4) never leave a blank — there is no wrong-answer penalty.
