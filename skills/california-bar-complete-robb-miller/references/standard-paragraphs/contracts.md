## A. Applicable Law

### Common Law vs UCC 🔴
The common law governs contracts for services and real property, while UCC Article 2 governs
contracts for the sale of goods — movable, tangible property. For a mixed contract, courts
apply the predominant-purpose test, applying a single body of law to the whole contract based
on its primary thrust.
*Apply: classify the subject matter and pick the governing law before anything else.*

---

## B. Formation

### Offer 🟢
An offer is an outward manifestation of present contractual intent, communicated to the
offeree, with terms sufficiently definite and certain that acceptance will form a contract.
Under the UCC, an offer may leave terms open so long as the parties intend to contract and
there is a reasonably certain basis for granting a remedy.
*Apply: identify a communicated manifestation of intent with definite terms.*

### Termination of the Offer 🟢
An offer terminates by revocation communicated to the offeree before acceptance, by the
offeree's rejection or counteroffer, by lapse of a stated or reasonable time, or by operation
of law — the death or incapacity of either party, destruction of the subject matter, or
supervening illegality.
*Apply: ask whether the offer was still open when the offeree purported to accept.*

### Irrevocable Offers 🟡
An offer cannot be revoked where it is held open by an option supported by consideration; by
a UCC firm offer, in which a merchant promises in a signed writing to hold the offer open
(irrevocable for the stated time, not to exceed 90 days); by the offeree's beginning
performance of a unilateral contract; or by the offeree's foreseeable and detrimental
reliance on the offer.
*Apply: identify the option, firm offer, part performance, or reliance.*

### Acceptance 🟢
Acceptance is an objective manifestation of assent to the terms of the offer by the person to
whom it was addressed. At common law, the acceptance must mirror the offer's terms; a varying
response is a counteroffer. A bilateral offer is accepted by a return promise, while a
unilateral offer is accepted only by completing the requested performance.
*Apply: confirm assent by the proper offeree through the required mode.*

### Battle of the Forms 🔴
Under UCC § 2-207, a definite and timely expression of acceptance or a written confirmation
operates as an acceptance even though it states terms additional to or different from the
offer, unless acceptance is expressly conditioned on assent to the new terms. Between
merchants, additional terms become part of the contract unless they materially alter it, the
offer expressly limited acceptance to its terms, or the offeror objects within a reasonable
time.
*Apply: ask whether both parties are merchants and whether the added term materially alters
the deal.*
> **Divergence:** At common law the mirror-image rule controls — additional terms make the
> response a counteroffer, and the last form sent governs. Section 2-207 rejects that for the
> sale of goods.

### Mailbox Rule 🟡
An acceptance is effective upon dispatch, provided it is properly addressed and sent by a
reasonable means, whereas a rejection is effective only on receipt. If the offeree sends a
rejection and then an acceptance, whichever arrives first controls. The mailbox rule does not
apply to option contracts, which require receipt of the acceptance by the deadline.
*Apply: pin down whether acceptance was dispatched before any revocation or rejection.*

### Consideration 🟢
Consideration is a bargained-for exchange in which each party incurs legal detriment by doing,
or promising to do, something it is not legally obligated to do, or by forbearing something it
has a right to do. A promise to perform a pre-existing legal duty is not consideration at
common law.
*Apply: identify the bargained-for detriment on each side.*
> **Divergence:** A contract modification needs new consideration at common law (the
> pre-existing-duty rule), but under the UCC a modification sought in good faith is binding
> without any new consideration.

### Promissory Estoppel 🟡
A promise is enforceable without consideration where the promisor should reasonably expect it
to induce action or forbearance, the promisee does foreseeably and detrimentally rely on it,
and injustice can be avoided only by enforcement; recovery may be limited to the reliance
interest.
*Apply: show a promise, foreseeable reliance, detriment, and injustice absent enforcement.*

## C. Defenses to Formation and Enforcement

### Statute of Frauds 🟢
Certain contracts are unenforceable unless evidenced by a writing signed by the party to be
charged: contracts in consideration of marriage, those that cannot be performed within one
year, those involving an interest in land, those by an executor to answer for estate debts
personally, those for the sale of goods of $500 or more, and suretyship contracts. Exceptions
include part performance, full performance, judicial admission, a merchant's confirmatory
memorandum, and specially manufactured goods.
*Apply: place the contract within a Statute-of-Frauds category, then look for a writing or
exception.*

### Mistake 🟡
A mutual mistake — where both parties err about a basic assumption that materially affects the
exchange — makes the contract voidable by the adversely affected party, unless that party bore
the risk of the mistake. A unilateral mistake generally does not avoid the contract unless the
other party knew or should have known of the mistake or enforcement would be unconscionable.
*Apply: determine whether one or both parties were mistaken about a basic assumption.*

### Misrepresentation, Duress, and Undue Influence 🟡
A contract is voidable where assent was induced by a misrepresentation (a false assertion of
fact, material or fraudulent, justifiably relied upon), by duress (an improper threat that
leaves the victim no reasonable alternative), or by undue influence (unfair persuasion of a
party who is under another's domination or in a relationship of trust).
*Apply: identify the improper inducement that produced assent.*

### Unconscionability 🟡
A court may refuse to enforce a contract or a clause that is unconscionable at the time of
formation, which requires both procedural unconscionability (unfair surprise or absence of
meaningful choice, as in an adhesion contract) and substantive unconscionability (oppressive
or grossly one-sided terms).
*Apply: show both an unfair process and an unfair term.*

### Capacity 🟡
A contract made by a person lacking capacity — a minor, a person who is mentally incompetent, or
one so intoxicated that the other party has reason to know it — is voidable by the incapacitated
party. A minor may disaffirm before or shortly after reaching majority but must return any benefit
still in its possession, and may be liable in restitution for necessaries.
*Apply: identify the incapacity and the party's right to disaffirm.*

### Misunderstanding (Ambiguity) 🟡
Where the parties attach materially different meanings to an ambiguous term and neither knows nor
has reason to know of the other's meaning, there is no mutual assent and no contract; if one party
knows or should know of the ambiguity, the contract is enforced according to the meaning of the
innocent party.
*Apply: ask whether each party reasonably held a different meaning of the ambiguous term.*

## D. Terms

### Parol Evidence Rule 🔴
Where the parties intend a writing to be a complete and final integration of their agreement,
the parol evidence rule bars evidence of prior or contemporaneous agreements that contradict
or supplement the writing. It does not bar evidence offered to show a defense to formation, to
explain an ambiguity, to prove a condition precedent to effectiveness, or to supply consistent
additional terms to a partial integration; under the UCC, course of dealing, usage of trade,
and course of performance may always explain or supplement.
*Apply: ask whether the writing is integrated and whether the evidence contradicts or merely
explains it.*

### Warranties 🟡
Under the UCC, a seller may make an express warranty through an affirmation of fact, promise,
description, or sample that becomes part of the basis of the bargain; an implied warranty of
merchantability, applicable when the seller is a merchant of such goods, that the goods are fit
for their ordinary purpose; and an implied warranty of fitness for a particular purpose where
the seller knows the buyer's purpose and that the buyer is relying on the seller's skill.
Implied warranties may be disclaimed by conspicuous language or by selling the goods "as is."
*Apply: identify the warranty made and whether it was effectively disclaimed and breached.*

### Gap-Fillers and Course of Dealing (UCC) 🟡
Where a sale-of-goods contract leaves terms open, the UCC supplies gap-fillers — a reasonable price,
a reasonable time for performance, delivery at the seller's place of business — so long as the
parties intended to contract. The parties' course of performance, course of dealing, and usage of
trade are used to interpret and supplement the agreement, in that order of priority.
*Apply: fill missing terms with the UCC default and interpret by the parties' practice.*

## E. Conditions

### Conditions 🟡
A condition is an event, not certain to occur, that must occur before a duty to perform arises
(a condition precedent) or that discharges a duty (a condition subsequent). Express conditions
require strict compliance, while constructive conditions require only substantial compliance.
A condition may be excused by waiver, by estoppel, by the benefited party's wrongful prevention
or hindrance, or to avoid a forfeiture.
*Apply: identify the condition and ask whether it occurred or was excused.*

## F. Performance and Breach

### Substantial Performance vs Perfect Tender 🔴
At common law, a party who substantially performs — committing no material breach — may recover
on the contract less damages for the deficiency, while a material breach excuses the other
party's performance and permits suit. Under the UCC, the perfect-tender rule allows a buyer to
reject goods that fail in any respect to conform to the contract, subject to the seller's right
to cure within the contract time and the special rule for installment contracts, which allows
rejection only for a substantial impairment.
*Apply: measure the performance against the governing standard and check for cure.*

### Anticipatory Repudiation 🟡
An unequivocal statement or act by a party indicating that it will not perform, made before
performance is due, is an anticipatory repudiation that allows the non-repudiating party to sue
immediately, suspend its own performance, or await performance. A party with reasonable grounds
for insecurity may demand adequate assurance and suspend performance until it is received;
failure to provide assurance within a reasonable time is itself a repudiation. A repudiation may
be retracted before the other party relies on it.
*Apply: identify the clear refusal to perform before performance was due.*

### Excuse of Performance 🟡
A duty to perform is discharged by impossibility, where performance has become objectively
impossible (such as the death of a person essential to performance or destruction of the
specific subject matter); by impracticability, where an unforeseen event whose non-occurrence
was a basic assumption makes performance excessively and unreasonably burdensome; or by
frustration of purpose, where an unforeseen event destroys the value of the performance to a
party, a purpose known to both as the foundation of the contract.
*Apply: tie the unforeseen event to impossibility, impracticability, or frustration.*

### UCC Risk of Loss 🟡
Where the contract allocates risk of loss, that controls; otherwise, under a shipment contract the
risk passes to the buyer when the seller delivers conforming goods to the carrier, while under a
destination contract it passes only when the goods are tendered at the destination. Absent a
carrier, a merchant seller bears the risk until the buyer takes physical possession, and a
non-merchant seller bears it until the seller tenders the goods. A breach by either party shifts the
risk of loss to the breaching party.
*Apply: classify the contract, then locate where the risk had passed when the loss occurred.*

### UCC Buyer's Acceptance, Rejection, and Revocation 🟡
A buyer has a right to inspect goods before acceptance, and payment without an opportunity to inspect
is not acceptance. Under the perfect-tender rule, the buyer may reject goods that fail in any respect
to conform, within a reasonable time and with notice, subject to the seller's right to cure. Once the
buyer accepts, it may no longer reject, but may revoke acceptance if a nonconformity substantially
impairs the value of the goods and acceptance was based on a reasonable but mistaken assumption of
conformity or the seller's assurances.
*Apply: trace the buyer's inspection, acceptance, and any rejection or revocation.*

## G. Third Parties

### Third-Party Beneficiaries 🟡
A third party may enforce a contract only if it is an intended beneficiary — one the contracting
parties intended to benefit, typically a named creditor or donee beneficiary — whereas an
incidental beneficiary has no rights. An intended beneficiary's rights vest when it learns of and
relies on, assents to, or sues on the contract, after which the original parties may not modify or
rescind without its consent.
*Apply: classify the beneficiary as intended or incidental, and check vesting.*

### Assignment and Delegation 🟡
A party may assign its contract rights unless the assignment would materially change the obligor's
duty or risk, is barred by the contract, or is prohibited by law; an assignment for value
extinguishes the assignor's rights. A party may delegate its duties unless the duty is personal,
involving special skill or trust, or delegation is barred; the delegating party remains liable
unless there is a novation.
*Apply: test the assignment or delegation against its limits.*

## H. Remedies (overview)

### Contract Remedies 🟢
The default remedy for breach is expectation damages — the benefit of the bargain, putting the
non-breaching party where full performance would have. Consequential damages are recoverable only
if they were foreseeable at the time of contracting, and incidental damages cover the costs of
dealing with the breach; reliance and restitution are alternatives. Liquidated damages are
enforceable where damages were difficult to estimate at formation and the sum is a reasonable
forecast rather than a penalty, and equitable specific performance is available for unique goods
or land when damages are inadequate. All damages must be reasonably certain, foreseeable, and
unavoidable through reasonable mitigation.
*Apply: compute the expectation interest, add foreseeable consequential losses, and subtract
avoidable losses — then see the Remedies bank for the full analysis.*
