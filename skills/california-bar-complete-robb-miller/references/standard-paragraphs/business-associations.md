## A. Agency

### Formation of Agency 🟢
An agency relationship arises when one person (the principal) manifests assent that another (the
agent) act on the principal's behalf and subject to the principal's control, and the agent
consents. No consideration is required, and capacity is required only of the principal.
*Apply: identify the manifestation of assent and the principal's right of control.*

### Actual Authority (Express and Implied) 🟢
An agent has actual authority where the principal's words or conduct lead the agent reasonably to
believe it is authorized to act. Actual authority is express when conferred in words, and implied
when reasonably necessary to carry out the express authority or arising from custom, position, or
prior dealings.
*Apply: ask whether the principal's communications gave the agent a reasonable belief of
authority.*

### Apparent Authority 🔴
Apparent authority exists when the principal's words or conduct cause a third party reasonably to
believe the agent is authorized, even though the agent lacks actual authority; the focus is on the
principal's manifestations to the third party, not on what the principal told the agent.
*Apply: trace the third party's reasonable belief back to the principal's own conduct.*

### Ratification 🟡
A principal may ratify an unauthorized act, making it binding as if originally authorized, where the
principal has knowledge of the material facts, affirms the act or accepts its benefits, and the act
was one the principal could have authorized when it occurred.
*Apply: show the principal's informed affirmance.*

### Agent's Fiduciary Duties 🟡
An agent owes the principal fiduciary duties of loyalty, obedience, and reasonable care — including
duties to account, to avoid undisclosed self-dealing and conflicts, and not to usurp the
principal's opportunities or secretly profit from the agency.
*Apply: identify the agent's disloyal act and the principal's remedy of disgorgement.*

### Principal's Liability for the Agent's Torts 🟢
Under respondeat superior, a principal is vicariously liable for the torts of an employee committed
within the scope of employment, but generally not for those of an independent contractor. Conduct
is within scope if it is of the kind the agent was hired to perform and is actuated at least in
part to serve the principal; a minor detour stays within scope, but a substantial frolic does not.
*Apply: classify the actor as employee or contractor, then test scope of employment.*

### Agent's Contract Liability — Disclosed vs Undisclosed Principal 🟡
An authorized agent for a disclosed principal is not personally liable on the contract; where the
principal is partially disclosed (identity unknown) or undisclosed, the agent may be held
personally liable, and the third party may elect to hold either the agent or the principal once
revealed.
*Apply: ask whether the principal's existence and identity were disclosed at contracting.*

## B. Partnership

### Formation of a General Partnership 🟢
A general partnership is the association of two or more persons to carry on as co-owners a business
for profit, whether or not they intend to form one. The sharing of profits raises a presumption of
partnership unless the share was received as repayment of a debt, wages, rent, or similar payment.
No writing or filing is required.
*Apply: look for co-ownership and profit-sharing.*

### Partnership by Estoppel 🟡
One who is not a partner may be liable as if a partner where that person represents, or consents to
a representation, that it is a partner, and a third party reasonably relies on the representation in
dealing with the partnership.
*Apply: identify the holding-out and the third party's reliance.*

### Partnership Property and a Partner's Rights in It 🟡
Property is partnership property if acquired in the partnership's name or with partnership funds; a
partner is a co-owner of partnership property only as a tenant in partnership, may use it solely for
partnership purposes, and may not unilaterally transfer it. A partner's transferable interest is
limited to its share of profits and surplus, which it may assign without dissolving the partnership.
*Apply: characterize the asset as partnership or personal property, then test the partner's rights.*

### Partners' Fiduciary Duties 🟢
Partners owe the partnership and one another fiduciary duties of loyalty and care. Loyalty requires
a partner to account for profits, refrain from self-dealing adverse to the partnership, and not
compete with it; care bars grossly negligent, reckless, or intentional misconduct.
*Apply: pinpoint the partner's self-dealing, competition, or careless act.*

### Partner Authority and Liability to Third Parties 🟡
Each partner is an agent of the partnership for carrying on its business in the ordinary course, and
the partnership is bound by such acts. Partners are jointly and severally liable for all partnership
obligations, though a plaintiff ordinarily must first exhaust partnership assets; an incoming partner
is not personally liable for obligations arising before its admission.
*Apply: ask whether the act was in the ordinary course, then assess each partner's exposure.*

### Profit and Loss Sharing 🟡
Absent an agreement otherwise, partners share profits equally regardless of differing capital
contributions, and losses are shared in the same proportion as profits.
*Apply: divide profits and losses equally unless the agreement provides otherwise.*

### Admission of a New Partner 🟢
Unless the partnership agreement provides otherwise, the admission of a new partner requires the
unanimous consent of the existing partners.
*Apply: confirm unanimous consent to the new partner.*

### Dissociation, Dissolution, and Distribution 🟡
A partner's dissociation is its ceasing to be associated in carrying on the business; depending on
the cause, the partnership either buys out the dissociated partner and continues, or dissolves and
winds up. On winding up, assets are applied first to creditors (including partners who are
creditors), then to partners' capital accounts, and finally to partners in proportion to their
profit shares.
*Apply: characterize the dissociation, then run the distribution priorities.*

### Limited Partnership 🟡
A limited partnership, which requires a state filing, has at least one general partner who manages
and bears unlimited personal liability and at least one limited partner whose liability is limited to
its capital contribution, so long as the limited partner does not exercise control beyond the
statutory safe harbors.
*Apply: identify the general and limited partners and each one's liability.*

### Limited Liability Partnership 🟡
A limited liability partnership is a general partnership that, by making the required state filing,
shields each partner from personal liability for the partnership's obligations, leaving the partner
liable only for its own wrongful conduct.
*Apply: confirm the LLP filing and the resulting liability shield.*

## C. Corporate Formation

### Promoter Liability and Pre-Incorporation Contracts 🟡
A promoter who contracts on behalf of a corporation not yet formed remains personally liable on the
contract, and continues to be liable even after incorporation unless there is a novation
substituting the corporation; the corporation becomes liable only when it adopts the contract
expressly or by accepting its benefits. Promoters are fiduciaries who may not make a secret profit
in their dealings with the corporation.
*Apply: ask whether a novation released the promoter and whether the corporation adopted the
contract.*

### Subscribers 🟡
A subscriber is one who makes a written offer to buy stock from a corporation not yet formed. A
pre-incorporation subscription is irrevocable for six months unless it provides otherwise or all
subscribers consent to revocation.
*Apply: check whether the subscription was revoked within the irrevocable period.*

### De Jure, De Facto, and Corporation by Estoppel 🟢
A de jure corporation is formed by filing articles of incorporation that substantially comply with
the incorporation statute, and the filing is conclusive proof of valid formation. Where formation
fails, a de facto corporation may exist if there was a good-faith, colorable attempt to comply and
the business operated as a corporation without knowledge of the defect; corporation by estoppel may
bar a party who dealt with the business as a corporation from later denying its existence.
*Apply: ask whether the articles were properly filed, and if not, whether de facto or estoppel
applies.*

### Ultra Vires 🟡
Under the modern rule, a corporation's acts beyond its stated purpose (ultra vires acts) are
generally valid; a shareholder may seek an injunction, the state may dissolve the corporation, and
the corporation may hold responsible directors and officers liable for losses caused by the ultra
vires act.
*Apply: identify the act outside the corporate purpose and the available remedy.*

### Foreign Corporations 🟡
A corporation incorporated in another state must, to transact regular intrastate business, qualify
by filing a certificate of authority with the Secretary of State, providing the same information
required of a domestic corporation; failure to qualify may bar it from suing in the state's courts.
*Apply: ask whether the out-of-state corporation transacts intrastate business and has qualified.*

### Piercing the Corporate Veil 🔴
Shareholders are generally not personally liable for corporate obligations, but a court may pierce
the veil where shareholders have abused the corporate form — treating corporate assets as their own
or ignoring formalities (alter ego), grossly undercapitalizing the corporation, or using it to
perpetrate a fraud — and refusing to pierce would sanction injustice. Courts pierce more readily for
tort creditors than contract creditors.
*Apply: identify the abuse of the corporate form and the resulting injustice.*

## D. Directors, Officers, and Duties

### Board Action and Meetings 🟡
The board of directors manages the corporation and must act either by unanimous written consent or
at a duly held meeting with notice and a quorum (a majority of all directors) present; a resolution
passes by a majority of those present. Directors may not vote by proxy or pursuant to voting
agreements, and each director is presumed to concur in board action unless a dissent is recorded.
*Apply: confirm valid board action by consent or proper meeting, and note any unrecorded dissent.*

### Officers' Authority 🟡
Officers are agents of the corporation and bind it through their authorized acts. Their authority may
be actual (conferred by the articles, bylaws, or board), apparent (where the corporation holds the
officer out as authorized and third parties rely), or inherent in the office held.
*Apply: identify the source of the officer's authority to bind the corporation.*

### Duty of Care and the Business Judgment Rule 🟢
A director must discharge her duties in good faith, with the care an ordinarily prudent person would
exercise, and in a manner she reasonably believes to be in the corporation's best interests. Under
the business judgment rule, a court presumes the director acted on an informed basis, in good faith,
and in the honest belief the action served the corporation, and will not second-guess such a
decision absent fraud, illegality, or a conflict of interest.
*Apply: ask whether the decision was informed and disinterested, invoking the rule's protection.*

### Duty of Loyalty — Self-Dealing 🔴
A director must act in the corporation's interest, not her own. An interested or self-dealing
transaction is voidable unless the director shows it was fair to the corporation when entered, or it
was approved after full disclosure of all material facts by a majority of disinterested directors or
shareholders. The remedy is rescission of the transaction or disgorgement of the director's profit.
*Apply: identify the conflicted transaction and test for fairness or disinterested approval.*

### Duty of Loyalty — Corporate Opportunity 🔴
A director or officer may not divert to herself a business opportunity in the corporation's line of
business or in which it has an interest or expectancy without first disclosing it and giving the
corporation a chance to act; the corporation's lack of financial ability to take the opportunity is
no defense. The remedy is recovery of profits or imposition of a constructive trust.
*Apply: ask whether the opportunity belonged to the corporation and was taken without offer.*

### Indemnification of Directors and Officers 🟡
A corporation may indemnify a director or officer for litigation costs incurred by reason of the
position; indemnification is mandatory where the director prevails, permissive where she acted in
good faith and in the corporation's best interests, and prohibited where she is held liable to the
corporation itself.
*Apply: classify the outcome to determine whether indemnification is required, permitted, or barred.*

### Controlling Shareholder Duties 🟡
A controlling shareholder owes a fiduciary duty not to use its control to benefit itself at the
minority's expense — including in a sale of control, a freeze-out merger, or parent-subsidiary
dealings — and such transactions are tested for fairness to the minority.
*Apply: identify the controller's self-benefiting use of control and test its fairness.*

## E. Shareholders

### Shareholder Voting 🟡
Shareholders of record on the record date may vote at a meeting in person or by proxy (revocable
unless coupled with an interest), and may pool their votes through binding voting agreements or
voting trusts. A quorum is a majority of the shares entitled to vote, and cumulative voting, where
authorized, lets shareholders concentrate votes to elect directors.
*Apply: confirm record ownership, quorum, and the proper voting mechanism.*

### Shareholder Derivative Suit 🔴
In a derivative suit, a shareholder sues to enforce a right belonging to the corporation, and any
recovery runs to the corporation. The shareholder must have owned stock at the time of the wrong and
throughout the suit, fairly represent the corporation's interests, and make a demand on the board to
act (or show demand would be futile).
*Apply: distinguish corporate harm from personal harm, then check standing and demand.*
> **CA divergence:** California permits the corporation to require a plaintiff holding a small stake
> to post security for the corporation's expenses, and applies its own demand framework —
> `> ⚠️ VERIFY` the current Corporations Code provisions before relying on specifics.

### Shareholder Direct Suit 🟡
A shareholder may sue directly where it suffered a harm distinct from that suffered by the
corporation generally — such as denial of voting rights, denial of dividends, or oppression — with
recovery running to the shareholder, in contrast to a derivative claim for injury to the corporation
itself.
*Apply: ask whether the injury is uniquely the shareholder's or shared through the corporation.*

### Dividends 🟡
Whether to declare a dividend lies in the board's discretion, protected by the business judgment
rule, and a shareholder cannot compel one absent bad faith. Dividends may be paid only from legally
available funds and not when the corporation is insolvent or the payment would render it insolvent;
directors who approve an unlawful distribution are personally liable.
*Apply: ask whether funds were legally available and whether withholding was in good faith.*

### Preemptive Rights 🟡
A preemptive right is a shareholder's right to maintain its proportional ownership by purchasing a
pro-rata share of a new issuance of stock; under modern statutes it exists only if the articles
expressly provide for it.
*Apply: check the articles for an express grant of preemptive rights.*

### Shareholder Inspection Rights 🟡
A shareholder has a right to inspect the corporation's books and records upon proper demand for a
purpose reasonably related to its interest as a shareholder.
*Apply: confirm a proper purpose for the inspection.*

## F. Federal Securities Liability

### Rule 10b-5 — Securities Fraud 🟡
Federal Rule 10b-5 makes it unlawful to use any manipulative or deceptive device in connection with
the purchase or sale of any security. A private plaintiff must prove (1) scienter — an intent to
deceive or reckless disregard, (2) deception or material misrepresentation or omission, and (3) a
connection with an actual purchase or sale; for damages, the plaintiff must also prove reliance and
loss causation. Insider trading violates the rule where an insider, or a tippee who knows the tipper
breached a duty for personal benefit, trades on material nonpublic information.
*Apply: run the elements, and for trading cases test the insider/tipper-tippee duty.*
> ⚠️ VERIFY scope of insider-trading and tipper-tippee liability before relying on specifics.

### Section 16(b) — Short-Swing Profits 🟡
Section 16(b) imposes strict liability on a corporate insider — a director, officer, or more-than-
ten-percent shareholder of a reporting corporation — requiring the insider to disgorge to the
corporation any profit realized from a purchase and sale (or sale and purchase) of the company's
equity securities within a six-month period, regardless of intent or use of inside information.
*Apply: identify a covered insider and a matching buy-sell pair within six months.*

## G. Fundamental Corporate Changes

### Fundamental Changes and Appraisal Rights 🟡
A fundamental corporate change — a merger or consolidation, a sale of substantially all assets
outside the ordinary course, an amendment of the articles, or dissolution — requires the board to
adopt a resolution and approval by a majority of the shares entitled to vote. A shareholder who
dissents from certain fundamental changes may invoke appraisal (dissenters') rights to be paid the
fair value of its shares.
*Apply: confirm board plus shareholder approval, and note any dissenter's appraisal remedy.*

## H. Limited Liability Companies

### LLC — Formation and Limited Liability 🟡
A limited liability company is an unincorporated entity formed by filing articles of organization
that combines the limited liability of a corporation with the pass-through taxation and flexibility
of a partnership; its members are not personally liable for the LLC's obligations beyond their
contributions.
*Apply: confirm the filing and the members' limited liability.*

### LLC — Management 🟡
An LLC is member-managed by default, with each member having authority to bind the LLC in the
ordinary course, unless the articles or operating agreement designate it as manager-managed, in
which case only the managers have such authority.
*Apply: identify whether the LLC is member- or manager-managed and who may bind it.*

### LLC — Fiduciary Duties 🟡
Those who manage an LLC — the members in a member-managed LLC, or the managers in a manager-managed
LLC — owe the LLC fiduciary duties of loyalty and care comparable to those of partners and directors.
*Apply: identify the managing parties and the duties they owe.*
