## A. Justiciability (always screen first)

### Case or Controversy 🟢
Article III, Section 2 limits the federal judicial power to actual "cases or
controversies." A federal court therefore may not issue advisory opinions and may decide
only disputes that are justiciable — meaning the plaintiff has standing and the matter is
ripe, not moot, and not a political question.
*Apply: confirm a live, concrete dispute before reaching the merits.*

### Standing 🔴
To have standing, a plaintiff must establish (1) an **injury in fact** — a concrete and
particularized invasion of a legally protected interest that is actual or imminent, (2)
**causation** — the injury is fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct, and
(3) **redressability** — a favorable decision is likely to remedy the injury.
*Apply: walk each of the three elements against this plaintiff's alleged harm.*

### Injury in Fact 🟢
The injury must be concrete and particularized and actual or imminent, not conjectural or
hypothetical. A generalized grievance shared by all citizens, or a speculative future
harm, is insufficient.
*Apply: identify the specific personal harm to this plaintiff.*

### Causation and Redressability 🟢
Causation requires that the injury be fairly traceable to the challenged action of the
defendant and not the result of an independent third party not before the court.
Redressability requires that a favorable judicial decision would likely redress the
injury.
*Apply: link the defendant's conduct to the harm, and the requested relief to a cure.*

### Taxpayer Standing 🟡
A taxpayer generally lacks standing to challenge government expenditures because the
interest is too remote and shared with all taxpayers. A narrow exception allows a
taxpayer to challenge a specific congressional appropriation enacted under the taxing and
spending power as a violation of the Establishment Clause.
*Apply: if a taxpayer plaintiff, deny standing unless it is an Establishment Clause
spending challenge.*

### Third-Party Standing 🟡
A plaintiff generally cannot assert the constitutional rights of others. Exceptions exist
where the plaintiff suffers its own injury and (1) has a close relationship with the
third party, or (2) the third party is unlikely to be able to assert its own rights.
*Apply: identify the special relationship or obstacle permitting jus tertii.*

### Associational Standing 🟡
An organization has standing to sue on behalf of its members when (1) the members would
have standing to sue in their own right, (2) the interests at stake are germane to the
organization's purpose, and (3) neither the claim nor the relief requires participation
of individual members.
*Apply: run the three prongs against the organization.*

### Ripeness 🟢
A claim is not ripe if the alleged injury is too remote or speculative. Courts evaluate
the fitness of the issue for judicial decision and the hardship to the parties of
withholding review; pre-enforcement review is available where the threat of enforcement
is genuine.
*Apply: ask whether the harm has matured enough to decide now.*

### Mootness 🟢
A case is moot if the controversy has been resolved so that the plaintiff no longer has a
concrete stake. Exceptions exist for wrongs capable of repetition yet evading review,
voluntary cessation that could resume, and class actions where a named representative's
claim becomes moot.
*Apply: if the dispute resolved after filing, check the exceptions.*

### Political Question 🟡
Federal courts will not decide political questions — issues constitutionally committed to
another branch or lacking judicially manageable standards, such as challenges to the
conduct of foreign affairs, impeachment procedures, or partisan gerrymandering.
*Apply: identify the textual commitment to a coordinate branch.*

### Eleventh Amendment / Sovereign Immunity 🟡
The Eleventh Amendment bars private suits against a state in federal court, whether by its
own citizens or those of another state. Suit is permitted where (1) the state consents,
(2) Congress validly abrogates immunity under its Section 5 power, or (3) the plaintiff
seeks prospective injunctive relief against a state officer under Ex parte Young.
*Apply: identify which exception, if any, applies.*

### Congressional Conferral of Standing / Zone of Interests 🟡
Congress may by statute confer standing on a plaintiff who would otherwise lack it, but it can
never eliminate the Article III case-or-controversy requirement of an actual injury. A plaintiff
suing under a statute must also fall within the zone of interests Congress meant to protect.
*Apply: check for a statutory grant of standing and that the plaintiff is within the protected
class — but confirm a concrete injury remains.*

### Adequate and Independent State Grounds 🟡
The Supreme Court will not review a state-court judgment if it rests on a state-law ground that is
both adequate to support the judgment and independent of federal law, because any opinion on the
federal question would be advisory.
*Apply: ask whether the state judgment stands on its own state-law footing.*

### Abstention (Younger) 🟡
A federal court will abstain from enjoining a pending state criminal (and certain civil)
proceeding, leaving the federal claims to be raised in the state forum, unless the state
prosecution was brought in bad faith or to harass.
*Apply: identify the pending state proceeding and the absence of a bad-faith exception.*

## B. State Action 🟢
The Constitution restrains only governmental actors; purely private conduct is not
subject to most constitutional limits. Private action will be treated as state action
where the private actor (1) performs a function traditionally and exclusively reserved to
the government (public function), or (2) is so entangled with the government — through
significant encouragement, authorization, or joint participation — that its conduct is
fairly attributable to the state (entanglement/nexus).
*Apply: identify the public function or the governmental entanglement.*

## C. Congressional Power

### No General Police Power 🟢
Congress is a government of limited, enumerated powers and possesses no general police
power; every federal statute must be traceable to a specific constitutional grant, such
as the Commerce, Taxing, or Spending Clauses, together with the Necessary and Proper
Clause.
*Apply: name the enumerated power the statute rests on.*

### Commerce Clause 🔴
Under the Commerce Clause, Congress may regulate (1) the channels of interstate commerce,
(2) the instrumentalities of interstate commerce and persons or things in interstate
commerce, and (3) activities that have a substantial effect on interstate commerce. Where
the activity is economic, courts aggregate its effects across the nation; non-economic
activity generally may not be regulated on a substantial-effects theory.
*Apply: classify the regulated activity and, if economic, aggregate its effect.*

### Taxing Power 🟡
Congress may lay and collect taxes to provide for the general welfare. An exaction that
raises revenue is a valid tax even if it also regulates behavior, and a measure labeled a
penalty may be sustained as a tax if it operates like one.
*Apply: ask whether the charge functions as a tax.*

### Spending Power 🟡
Congress may spend for the general welfare and may place conditions on the receipt of
federal funds if the conditions are (1) clearly stated, (2) related to the federal
interest in the program, (3) not unconstitutional in themselves, and (4) not so coercive
as to compel state participation.
*Apply: test each condition against the four limits.*

### Necessary and Proper Clause 🟢
The Necessary and Proper Clause is not an independent source of power but allows Congress
to enact any law that is a rationally related and appropriate means of executing one of
its enumerated powers.
*Apply: tie the means to an enumerated end.*

### Tenth Amendment / Anti-Commandeering 🔴
The Tenth Amendment reserves to the states powers not delegated to the federal government.
Congress may not commandeer the states by compelling state legislatures to enact laws or
ordering state executive officials to administer a federal program; it may, however,
regulate private conduct directly or attach conditions to federal funds.
*Apply: ask whether the law conscripts the state rather than regulating private parties.*

### Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment 🟡
Under Section 5, Congress may enact legislation to enforce the Fourteenth Amendment's
guarantees against the states, but only remedial or prophylactic measures that are
congruent and proportional to a documented pattern of constitutional violations; Congress
may not create new rights or substantively redefine them.
*Apply: check for a record of violations and proportional remedy.*

## D. Separation of Powers

### Executive Power — Youngstown 🔴
The scope of presidential power is assessed under the Youngstown framework: the
President's authority is at its maximum when acting with congressional authorization, in a
"zone of twilight" when Congress is silent, and at its lowest ebb when acting contrary to
the express or implied will of Congress.
*Apply: locate the President's action in one of the three categories.*

### Appointment and Removal 🟡
The President appoints principal officers with the advice and consent of the Senate;
Congress may vest the appointment of inferior officers in the President, the courts, or
department heads. The President may generally remove executive officers at will, though
Congress may impose for-cause limits on removal of independent agency heads so long as the
restriction does not impede the President's core executive functions.
*Apply: classify the officer and test any removal restriction.*

### Legislative Veto and Nondelegation 🟡
A legislative veto — an attempt by Congress to control executive action without
bicameralism and presentment — is unconstitutional, because the exercise of legislative
power requires passage by both houses and presentment to the President. Congress may
delegate rulemaking authority to the executive so long as it articulates an intelligible
principle to guide the exercise of that power.
*Apply: look for a one-house veto or a standardless delegation.*

### Presidential Immunity 🔴
> ⚠️ VERIFY scope before exam — Trump v. United States (2024).
A former President enjoys absolute immunity from criminal prosecution for acts within his
core constitutional powers, at least presumptive immunity for other official acts, and no
immunity for unofficial acts.
*Apply: characterize the act as core, official, or unofficial.*

### Impeachment 🟡
The House of Representatives has the sole power to impeach a federal officer (the President, Vice
President, and civil officers) by a majority vote for treason, bribery, or other high crimes and
misdemeanors; the Senate has the sole power to try the impeachment, and conviction and removal
require a two-thirds vote.
*Apply: confirm a House majority to impeach and a two-thirds Senate vote to convict.*

### Pardon Power 🟡
The President may grant reprieves and pardons for federal offenses, except in cases of impeachment;
the power extends to federal crimes only and not to state offenses or civil liability.
*Apply: confirm the offense is federal and not an impeachment.*

### Veto and Pocket Veto 🟡
A bill presented to the President becomes law if signed, or if not returned within ten days while
Congress is in session; if the President takes no action and Congress adjourns within that period,
the bill is pocket-vetoed and does not become law. A returned veto may be overridden by a
two-thirds vote of each house.
*Apply: track the ten-day window and whether Congress was in session.*

### Ex Post Facto Laws and Bills of Attainder 🟡
Neither Congress nor the states may pass an ex post facto law — one that retroactively criminalizes
conduct, increases punishment, or reduces the proof required for a past act — or a bill of attainder,
a legislative act that inflicts punishment on named individuals without a trial.
*Apply: ask whether the law punishes past conduct retroactively or targets named persons.*

## E. Federalism and Supremacy

### Supremacy and Preemption 🟢
Under the Supremacy Clause, valid federal law is supreme and preempts conflicting state
law. Preemption may be **express** (the statute says so), **field** (the federal scheme is
so pervasive that it occupies the field), or **conflict** (compliance with both is
impossible, or state law stands as an obstacle to the full federal purpose).
*Apply: identify the form of preemption in play.*

### Intergovernmental Immunity 🟡
States may not directly regulate or tax the federal government or its instrumentalities,
nor may they discriminate against the federal government or those it deals with; the
federal government is likewise generally immune from discriminatory state regulation.
*Apply: ask whether the state singles out or directly burdens the federal government.*

### Full Faith and Credit 🟡
The courts of one state must give full faith and credit to the judgments of another state's courts
where the rendering court had jurisdiction, the judgment was on the merits, and it is final.
*Apply: confirm jurisdiction, a merits judgment, and finality before enforcing the sister-state
judgment.*

## F. Dormant Commerce Clause and Privileges & Immunities

### Dormant Commerce Clause 🔴
Even absent congressional action, the Commerce Clause implicitly restricts state
regulation that burdens interstate commerce. A state law that **discriminates** against
interstate commerce — facially, in purpose, or in effect — is virtually per se invalid and
will be upheld only if it serves a legitimate local purpose that cannot be achieved by
reasonable nondiscriminatory means. A **nondiscriminatory** law that only incidentally
burdens interstate commerce is upheld unless the burden is clearly excessive in relation
to the local benefits (Pike balancing).
*Apply: first ask discriminatory or not, then apply the matching test.*

### Market-Participant Exception 🟡
When a state acts not as a regulator but as a participant in the market — buying, selling,
or hiring — it may favor its own citizens without violating the dormant Commerce Clause.
*Apply: check whether the state is a proprietor rather than a regulator.*

### Article IV Privileges and Immunities 🟡
The Privileges and Immunities Clause of Article IV bars a state from discriminating against
nonresidents with respect to fundamental rights, such as the pursuit of a livelihood,
unless the state shows a substantial justification and that nonresidents are a peculiar
source of the evil. Corporations and aliens are not protected, and there is no
market-participant exception.
*Apply: identify discrimination against out-of-state individuals on a fundamental right.*

### State Taxation of Commerce 🟡
A state tax on interstate commerce is valid under the Complete Auto test if (1) there is a
substantial nexus between the activity and the taxing state, (2) the tax is fairly
apportioned, (3) it does not discriminate against interstate commerce, and (4) it is
fairly related to services the state provides.
*Apply: run the four prongs.*

### Import-Export Clause and State Taxation 🟡
A state may not, without the consent of Congress, lay imposts or duties on imports or exports
except what is absolutely necessary for executing its inspection laws. A nondiscriminatory state
tax on goods that are part of the general property in the state, however, is permissible.
*Apply: ask whether the state tax singles out imports/exports as such.*

### Fourteenth Amendment Privileges or Immunities (Right to Travel) 🟡
The Privileges or Immunities Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment protects the rights of national
citizenship, most notably the right to interstate travel — including the right of a newly arrived
citizen to be treated the same as longer-term residents — so durational-residency requirements for
important benefits receive heightened scrutiny.
*Apply: identify a law penalizing newly arrived residents or burdening interstate migration.*

## G. Levels of Scrutiny (the master key) 🔴
**Rational basis** applies to most economic and social legislation: the law is upheld if
it is rationally related to any legitimate government interest, and the challenger bears
the burden. **Intermediate scrutiny** applies to classifications based on sex or
legitimacy and to content-neutral speech restrictions: the law must be substantially
related to an important government interest, and the government bears the burden. **Strict
scrutiny** applies to suspect classifications (race, national origin, alienage) and
burdens on fundamental rights: the law must be necessary to achieve a compelling
government interest, and the government bears the burden.
*Apply: identify the classification or right, then state and apply the correct tier — this
choice drives the whole rights analysis.*

## H. Equal Protection 🔴
Equal protection — applied to the states through the Fourteenth Amendment and to the
federal government through the Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause — forbids the
government from treating similarly situated persons differently without adequate
justification. The level of scrutiny turns on the classification: strict scrutiny for
race, national origin, and alienage; intermediate scrutiny for sex and legitimacy; and
rational basis for all others. Where a law is facially neutral, the challenger must show
both a discriminatory purpose and a discriminatory effect to trigger heightened review.
*Apply: identify the classification, set the tier, then apply it.*

## I. Due Process

### Procedural Due Process 🟢
Procedural due process requires that the government provide fair process before depriving a
person of life, liberty, or property. The court first asks whether a protected liberty or
property interest is at stake, and then determines what process is due by balancing (1) the
private interest affected, (2) the risk of erroneous deprivation and the value of
additional safeguards, and (3) the government's interest, including administrative burdens
(Mathews v. Eldridge).
*Apply: identify the protected interest, then run the Mathews balance.*

### Substantive Due Process 🔴
> ⚠️ VERIFY current scope — Dobbs v. Jackson (2022) narrowed the doctrine.
Substantive due process protects against government infringement of fundamental rights. A
law burdening a fundamental right — such as marriage, procreation, the care and custody of
children, and interstate travel — is reviewed under strict scrutiny, while laws affecting
non-fundamental interests receive rational basis review.
*Apply: classify the right as fundamental or not, then set the tier.*

## J. Takings 🟡
The Fifth Amendment, applied to the states through the Fourteenth, permits the government
to take private property only for a public use and upon payment of just compensation. A
**per se taking** occurs when the government permanently physically occupies property or
when a regulation denies an owner all economically viable use (Lucas). A partial
regulatory burden is analyzed under Penn Central by weighing the regulation's economic
impact, its interference with distinct investment-backed expectations, and the character of
the government action. **Public use** is satisfied by any conceivable public benefit, and
**just compensation** is measured by fair market value at the time of the taking.
*Apply: classify the taking, then apply the matching test and compensation rule.*

## K. Contracts Clause 🟡
Article I, Section 10 bars states from passing laws that substantially impair existing
contractual obligations. A law substantially impairing a **private** contract must serve a
significant and legitimate public purpose by reasonable and appropriate means; impairment
of the state's **own** contracts receives stricter scrutiny. The Clause applies only to
state action affecting existing contracts, not to the federal government.
*Apply: ask whether a state law substantially impairs an existing contract, then test the
justification.*

## L. First Amendment — Free Speech

### Free Speech Framework 🔴
The First Amendment, applied to the states through the Fourteenth, protects freedom of
speech. The threshold question is whether the regulation is **content-based** — targeting
speech because of its subject matter or viewpoint, which is presumptively invalid and
subject to strict scrutiny — or **content-neutral**, which is subject to intermediate
scrutiny. Special doctrines govern prior restraints, vagueness and overbreadth,
unprotected categories, public forums, and commercial and symbolic speech.
*Apply: first classify the regulation as content-based or content-neutral.*

### Content-Based Restrictions 🔴
A law that restricts speech based on its content or viewpoint is presumptively
unconstitutional and will be upheld only if it is necessary to serve a compelling
government interest and is narrowly tailored to that end. Viewpoint discrimination is
especially disfavored and almost never survives.
*Apply: identify the content distinction, then apply strict scrutiny.*

### Content-Neutral / Time, Place, and Manner 🟢
A content-neutral restriction on the time, place, or manner of speech in a public forum is
valid if it (1) is content-neutral, (2) is narrowly tailored to serve a significant
government interest, and (3) leaves open ample alternative channels of communication.
*Apply: run the three prongs.*

### Prior Restraint 🟡
A prior restraint — a government order suppressing speech before it occurs — bears a heavy
presumption against its validity and is permitted only on a showing of a special societal
harm; any licensing scheme must contain narrow, definite standards and procedural
safeguards.
*Apply: identify the pre-publication suppression and demand justification.*

### Vagueness and Overbreadth 🟡
A speech regulation is void for **vagueness** if it fails to give a reasonable person fair
notice of what is prohibited, and void for **overbreadth** if it restricts substantially
more protected speech than the Constitution permits.
*Apply: test the statute's clarity and reach.*

### Unprotected Categories 🟡
Certain categories of speech receive little or no protection and may be regulated: speech
that is directed to inciting imminent lawless action and likely to produce it
(Brandenburg), fighting words, true threats, obscenity (Miller), and defamation. Outside
these narrow categories, speech is presumptively protected.
*Apply: ask whether the speech falls within a recognized unprotected category.*

### Commercial Speech 🟡
Commercial speech that is truthful and concerns lawful activity receives intermediate
protection: a restriction is valid only if it serves a substantial government interest,
directly advances that interest, and is no more extensive than necessary (Central Hudson).
False, misleading, or unlawful commercial speech is not protected.
*Apply: confirm the speech is lawful and truthful, then run Central Hudson.*

### Public Forum Doctrine 🟡
In a traditional or designated public forum, content-based restrictions face strict
scrutiny and content-neutral time, place, and manner rules must be narrowly tailored to a
significant interest with ample alternatives. In a nonpublic forum, the government may
restrict speech if the regulation is viewpoint-neutral and reasonably related to a
legitimate purpose.
*Apply: classify the forum, then apply the matching standard.*

### Symbolic Speech 🟡
The government may regulate expressive conduct if the regulation (1) is within its
constitutional power, (2) furthers an important interest, (3) that interest is unrelated to
the suppression of expression, and (4) the incidental burden on speech is no greater than
necessary (United States v. O'Brien).
*Apply: run the O'Brien factors for conduct with a communicative element.*

## M. First Amendment — Religion

### Free Exercise Clause 🟡
The Free Exercise Clause forbids government from punishing religious belief. A neutral law
of general applicability that only incidentally burdens religious practice is valid under
rational basis and need not be justified by a compelling interest (Employment Division v.
Smith); a law that targets religious conduct or is not generally applicable triggers strict
scrutiny.
*Apply: ask whether the law is neutral and generally applicable.*

### Establishment Clause 🟡
The Establishment Clause bars government from establishing or endorsing religion. Courts
assess challenged practices by reference to historical practices and understandings, and
strike down government action that coerces religious participation or favors one religion
over another; outright denominational preferences receive strict scrutiny.
*Apply: identify government endorsement, coercion, or preference.*
