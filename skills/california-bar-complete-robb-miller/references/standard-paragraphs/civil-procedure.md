## A. Subject-Matter Jurisdiction

### Subject-Matter Jurisdiction 🟢
A federal court is one of limited jurisdiction and may hear a case only if it has
subject-matter jurisdiction, which exists through either federal-question jurisdiction or
diversity jurisdiction. Subject-matter jurisdiction can never be waived and may be raised
by any party, or by the court itself, at any time, including for the first time on appeal.
*Apply: open every federal-court essay by confirming a basis for SMJ exists.*

### Federal-Question Jurisdiction 🟢
Federal-question jurisdiction exists when the plaintiff's claim arises under the
Constitution, laws, or treaties of the United States, and the federal issue appears on the
face of a well-pleaded complaint — not through an anticipated defense or a counterclaim.
*Apply: ask whether a federal right is an essential element of the plaintiff's own claim.*

### Diversity Jurisdiction 🔴
Diversity jurisdiction exists when (1) there is complete diversity of citizenship — no
plaintiff is a citizen of the same state as any defendant — and (2) the amount in
controversy exceeds $75,000, exclusive of interest and costs.
*Apply: confirm complete diversity and a sufficient amount.*
> **CA divergence:** California superior courts are courts of general jurisdiction — there
> is no diversity requirement and no $75,000 floor to sue in state court. (CA's $35,000
> figure only divides limited from unlimited civil cases; it is not a barrier to the
> courthouse.)

### Citizenship of the Parties 🟢
A natural person is a citizen of the state of his domicile — the place where he is
physically present and intends to remain indefinitely. A corporation is a citizen of both
its state of incorporation and the state of its principal place of business, which is its
"nerve center," where its officers direct and control corporate activities.
*Apply: fix each party's citizenship, then test for complete diversity.*

### Amount in Controversy 🟡
The amount in controversy is measured by the plaintiff's good-faith allegation, which
controls unless it appears to a legal certainty that the claim is for less. A single
plaintiff may aggregate all of its claims against a single defendant to meet the threshold.
For injunctive or other equitable relief, the amount may be measured from either the
plaintiff's viewpoint (the value of the benefit) or the defendant's (the cost of
compliance).
*Apply: total the plaintiff's claims, or value the equitable relief from either side.*

### Supplemental Jurisdiction 🟡
A federal court with original jurisdiction over one claim may exercise supplemental
jurisdiction over additional claims that share a common nucleus of operative fact — that
is, that form part of the same case or controversy. In a case founded solely on diversity,
however, the statute withholds supplemental jurisdiction over certain claims by plaintiffs
against parties joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24 where doing so would destroy complete
diversity. A court may decline supplemental jurisdiction in its discretion.
*Apply: tie the extra claim to the same facts, then check the diversity carve-out.*

### Removal 🟡
A defendant may remove a state-court action to the federal district court embracing the
state court if that federal court would have had original jurisdiction. All defendants must
join in removal, which must occur within 30 days of service of the removable pleading. A
case removable solely on diversity may not be removed if any defendant is a citizen of the
forum state, and generally not more than one year after the action commenced.
*Apply: confirm original jurisdiction, unanimity, timing, and no forum-state defendant.*

## B. Personal Jurisdiction

### Personal Jurisdiction 🟢
A court has personal jurisdiction over a defendant only if a statute — the forum's long-arm
statute — authorizes jurisdiction and the exercise comports with constitutional due process.
Due process is satisfied by a traditional basis (the defendant's domicile in the forum,
physical presence when served, or consent) or by sufficient minimum contacts with the forum.
*Apply: identify the statutory basis, then test due process.*
> **CA divergence:** California's long-arm statute (CCP § 410.10) reaches as far as the
> Constitution permits, so in California the statutory inquiry collapses into the
> constitutional minimum-contacts analysis.

### Minimum Contacts and Fair Play 🟢
Minimum contacts require that the defendant have purposefully availed itself of the benefits
of the forum — such that it could reasonably foresee being haled into court there — and that
the claim either arise out of those contacts (specific jurisdiction) or that the contacts be
so continuous and systematic as to render the defendant essentially at home in the forum
(general jurisdiction). The exercise must also be fair, weighing the burden on the
defendant, the forum's interest, the plaintiff's interest in convenient relief, the judicial
system's efficiency, and shared substantive policies.
*Apply: show purposeful availment and relatedness, then weigh fairness.*

### Notice and Service of Process 🟡
Due process also requires that a defendant receive notice reasonably calculated, under the
circumstances, to apprise it of the action and afford an opportunity to be heard, accomplished
through proper service of process. Federal service may be made by personal delivery, by leaving
process at the defendant's dwelling with a person of suitable age and discretion, by delivery to an
authorized agent, or by the method permitted in the state where the court sits or service is made.
*Apply: confirm the manner of service satisfied the rule and gave reasonable notice.*
> **CA divergence:** California permits substituted service (leaving papers with a competent
> household member or office person, plus mailing) only if process cannot, with reasonable
> diligence, be personally delivered to the defendant.

## C. Venue and Transfer

### Venue 🟢
In federal court, venue is proper in any judicial district where any defendant resides, if
all defendants reside in the same state, or in a district where a substantial part of the
events or omissions giving rise to the claim occurred or where a substantial part of the
property at issue is located.
*Apply: locate the residence of the defendants or the situs of the events.*

### Transfer of Venue and Forum Non Conveniens 🟡
Where venue is proper, a court may transfer the case under § 1404(a) to another district
where it could have been brought, for the convenience of the parties and witnesses and in
the interest of justice. Where venue is improper, § 1406(a) permits dismissal or transfer to
a proper district. The doctrine of forum non conveniens allows dismissal when a far more
convenient forum lies in a different judicial system, such as a foreign country.
*Apply: ask whether venue is proper, then choose the right transfer mechanism.*

## D. The Erie Doctrine

### Erie Doctrine 🔴
A federal court sitting in diversity applies federal procedural law but the substantive law
of the state in which it sits, including that state's choice-of-law rules. When a Federal
Rule of Civil Procedure or a federal statute is directly on point and valid, the federal
court applies it. When no federal rule is on point, the court asks whether the state rule is
outcome-determinative in light of the twin aims of Erie — discouraging forum-shopping and
avoiding the inequitable administration of the laws.
*Apply: classify the issue as substantive (state law) or procedural, then run the analysis.*

## E. Pleadings

### The Complaint and Notice Pleading 🔴
A federal complaint must contain a short and plain statement of the grounds for
subject-matter jurisdiction, a short and plain statement of the claim showing entitlement to
relief, and a demand for relief. Under notice pleading, the complaint must state a claim
that is plausible on its face, pleading enough factual matter to permit a reasonable
inference of liability rather than a merely conceivable one.
*Apply: check the three required components and the plausibility of the claim.*
> **CA divergence:** California is a fact-pleading state — the complaint must allege the
> ultimate facts constituting each element of the cause of action, a higher bar than federal
> notice pleading, and California does not follow the federal plausibility standard. California
> also uses the **demurrer** (not the 12(b)(6) motion) as the primary pre-answer challenge to the
> legal sufficiency of a pleading.

### Pleading Fraud with Particularity 🟢
Although most claims require only notice pleading, allegations of fraud or mistake must be
stated with particularity — the who, what, when, where, and how of the misrepresentation.
Conditions of the mind, such as malice, intent, and knowledge, may be alleged generally.
*Apply: test whether the fraud allegations specify the circumstances of the wrong.*

### Rule 11 Sanctions 🟡
By signing a pleading or motion, an attorney certifies that, after a reasonable inquiry, the
filing is not for an improper purpose, the legal contentions are warranted, and the factual
contentions have evidentiary support. Before filing a sanctions motion, the moving party must
serve it and allow a 21-day safe harbor for the offending party to withdraw the challenged
filing.
*Apply: identify the deficient filing and confirm the safe harbor was honored.*

### Amendments and Relation Back 🟡
A party may amend a pleading once as of right within 21 days of serving it, or within 21 days
of a responsive pleading or motion; otherwise amendment requires leave of court, which is
freely given when justice so requires. An amendment adding a claim relates back to the
original filing if it arose from the same conduct, transaction, or occurrence; an amendment
changing a party also relates back if, within the service period, the new party knew of the
action and knew that it would have been named but for a mistake.
*Apply: ask whether the amendment shares the original transaction or corrects a misnamed
party with notice.*

## F. Joinder

### Joinder of Claims 🟢
A plaintiff may join as many claims as it has against an opposing party, whether or not the
claims are related, so long as each joined claim has an independent or supplemental basis for
subject-matter jurisdiction.
*Apply: note that any number and type of claims may be joined.*

### Counterclaims 🟢
A counterclaim is compulsory — and is waived if not asserted in the answer — when it arises
out of the same transaction or occurrence as the opposing party's claim; any other
counterclaim is permissive. A compulsory counterclaim falls within supplemental jurisdiction,
while a permissive counterclaim requires its own jurisdictional basis.
*Apply: ask whether the counterclaim shares the same transaction as the original claim.*

### Crossclaims 🟡
A crossclaim is a claim against a co-party that arises out of the same transaction or
occurrence as the original action or that relates to property at issue in the action.
Crossclaims are always permissive.
*Apply: confirm a same-transaction claim between co-parties.*

### Permissive Party Joinder 🟡
Multiple plaintiffs may join, or multiple defendants may be joined, in one action if the
claims by or against them arise out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of
transactions, and there is a question of law or fact common to all of them.
*Apply: test for the same transaction plus a common question.*

### Necessary and Indispensable Parties 🟡
A party is necessary if, in its absence, complete relief cannot be accorded among the
existing parties, or the absentee's interest would be impaired, or an existing party would
face a substantial risk of multiple or inconsistent obligations. A necessary party must be
joined if feasible; if joinder is not feasible, the court decides whether the party is
indispensable — such that in equity and good conscience the action cannot proceed — and if
so, dismisses.
*Apply: classify the absentee as necessary, then ask whether the case can proceed without it.*

### Impleader 🟡
A defending party may, as a third-party plaintiff, implead a non-party who is or may be
derivatively liable to it for all or part of the plaintiff's claim, such as on a theory of
indemnity or contribution.
*Apply: confirm the third party's liability is derivative of the original claim.*

### Intervention 🟡
A non-party may intervene as of right when it has an interest in the subject matter that may
be impaired by the action and is not adequately represented by the existing parties.
Permissive intervention lies in the court's discretion when the non-party's claim or defense
shares a common question of law or fact with the action.
*Apply: ask whether the intervenor's interest is impaired and unrepresented.*

### Class Actions 🟡
A class action requires numerosity (joinder of all members is impracticable), commonality
(questions common to the class), typicality (the representatives' claims are typical of the
class), and adequacy (the representatives will fairly protect the class), plus one of three
categories: a risk of inconsistent adjudications, injunctive relief appropriate to the class
as a whole, or common questions predominating such that a class action is the superior
method (which requires notice and an opportunity to opt out).
*Apply: run the four prerequisites, then fit the case into a Rule 23(b) category.*

### Interpleader 🟡
Interpleader lets a party holding property or funds subject to competing claims force the claimants
to litigate among themselves, protecting the stakeholder from multiple liability. Rule 22
interpleader requires the normal diversity or federal-question basis with the stakeholder diverse
from claimants, whereas statutory interpleader requires only minimal diversity between any two
claimants and a stake of $500 or more, with nationwide service.
*Apply: identify the stakeholder facing inconsistent claims and pick the interpleader route.*

## G. Discovery and Disposition

### Discovery Scope and Work Product 🟡
Parties may discover any non-privileged matter that is relevant to a claim or defense and
proportional to the needs of the case. Work product — material prepared in anticipation of
litigation — is protected from discovery absent a showing of substantial need and undue
hardship, and an attorney's mental impressions and legal theories receive near-absolute
protection.
*Apply: ask whether the material is relevant, privileged, or protected work product.*

### Dismissal for Failure to State a Claim 🟢
A motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim tests the legal sufficiency of the
complaint: accepting all well-pleaded facts as true and drawing reasonable inferences in the
plaintiff's favor, the court asks whether the complaint states a plausible claim for relief,
disregarding mere legal conclusions.
*Apply: take the allegations as true and ask whether an essential element is missing.*

### Summary Judgment 🟢
Summary judgment is proper when, viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to the
non-moving party, there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the moving party is
entitled to judgment as a matter of law. The movant bears the initial burden, after which the
non-movant must point to specific evidence establishing a triable issue.
*Apply: ask whether any material fact is genuinely disputed.*

### Judgment as a Matter of Law 🟡
Judgment as a matter of law may be granted after a party has been fully heard at trial when
no reasonable jury could find in that party's favor. A renewed motion after the verdict is
available only if the party first moved for judgment as a matter of law before the case was
submitted to the jury.
*Apply: ask whether the evidence permits only one reasonable conclusion.*

### Motion for a New Trial 🟡
A court may grant a new trial for a legal error that made the trial unfair (such as an erroneous
jury instruction or evidentiary ruling), newly discovered evidence that could not have been found
with due diligence, prejudicial misconduct, a verdict against the clear weight of the evidence, or
a clearly excessive or inadequate verdict (which the court may instead address by remittitur or
additur).
*Apply: identify the specific ground rendering the verdict unsound.*

### Appealability — Final Judgment Rule 🟡
As a general rule, a federal court of appeals has jurisdiction only over final judgments that
dispose of all claims as to all parties. Exceptions allow earlier appeal of certain interlocutory
orders, of a final judgment on some claims certified under Rule 54(b), and of collateral orders
that conclusively determine an important issue separate from the merits.
*Apply: ask whether the order being appealed is final or fits an exception.*

## H. Right to Jury Trial

### Seventh Amendment Jury Right 🔴
The Seventh Amendment preserves the right to a jury trial in civil actions at law where the
amount in controversy exceeds twenty dollars, but it does not extend to equitable claims.
Where legal and equitable claims share common fact issues, the legal issues are tried first
to the jury, and the jury's findings bind the court on the equitable claims. A party waives a
jury unless it serves a written demand within 14 days after the last pleading directed to the
triable issue.
*Apply: separate the legal claims (jury) from the equitable (court) and confirm a timely
demand.*
> **CA divergence:** The Seventh Amendment is not incorporated against the states;
> California's civil jury right derives from Article I, § 16 of the California Constitution,
> which turns on the same legal-versus-equitable ("gist of the action") distinction.

## I. Preclusion

### Claim Preclusion (Res Judicata) 🔴
Claim preclusion bars relitigation of the same claim where there was a final judgment on the
merits, rendered between the same parties or those in privity with them, and the later claim
arises from the same transaction or occurrence as the first. It bars not only what was
litigated but everything that could have been litigated in the first action.
*Apply: confirm a prior merits judgment, the same parties, and the same transaction.*
> **CA divergence:** California defines a "claim" by the primary-rights theory — one primary
> right and one injury — which can be narrower than the federal transactional test, so
> California may permit a second suit the federal rule would bar.

### Issue Preclusion (Collateral Estoppel) 🟢
Issue preclusion bars relitigation of an issue of fact or law that was actually litigated and
determined, was essential to a valid and final judgment, and is asserted against a party (or
one in privity) who had a full and fair opportunity to litigate it. Many courts no longer
require mutuality, so a non-party may sometimes invoke the doctrine offensively or
defensively.
*Apply: confirm the issue was actually litigated and necessary to the prior judgment.*
