# Criminal Law & Procedure — Essay Bank

> Two subjects in one. CA tests common-law/MPC criminal law and federal
> constitutional criminal procedure. Watch CA statutory divergences (felony-murder
> reform, Prop 8). All rule language original. Flag uncertain law `> ⚠️ VERIFY`.

**High-frequency note:** Criminal Law essays anchor on **homicide** (sort every
killing through murder → felony murder → voluntary → involuntary) and **inchoate +
accomplice** liability. Criminal Procedure anchors on the **Fourth Amendment** search
chain and **Miranda**. A combined essay often runs the substantive crime, then the
arrest/search/confession that produced the evidence.

## Part 2 — IRAC Rule Blocks (full, memorizable)

## CRIMINAL LAW

### Elements of a Crime — Actus Reus, Mens Rea, Concurrence, Causation
🟢

**Rule.** A crime requires a voluntary act (or an omission where a legal duty exists), a
culpable mental state, concurrence of the act and intent, and — for result crimes —
causation (actual cause, where the result would not have occurred but for the act, and
proximate cause, where the result is a foreseeable consequence). Common-law intents are
specific intent, malice, general intent, and strict liability; the MPC grades intent as
purposely, knowingly, recklessly, and negligently.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] [acted voluntarily] with [the required mental state], and the
act caused [the result].

**Thus.** Thus, the elements of [crime] are [met/not met].

### Murder
🟢

**Rule.** Murder is the unlawful killing of a human being with malice aforethought. Malice
exists with (1) intent to kill, (2) intent to inflict great bodily harm, (3) reckless
indifference to an unjustifiably high risk to human life ("depraved heart"), or (4) intent
to commit a felony (felony murder).

**Here.** Here, [defendant] killed [victim] with [intent to kill / depraved-heart
recklessness].

**Thus.** Thus, [defendant] committed murder.

### Degrees of Murder
🟡

**Rule.** First-degree murder is a killing committed with premeditation and deliberation
(a cool, reflective intent to kill, however brief) or during an enumerated felony. All
other murder is second degree.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] [reflected before killing / killed during an enumerated
felony].

**Thus.** Thus, the killing is [first/second]-degree murder.

> **CA divergence (statutory murder):** California codifies murder at **Penal Code §187**
> (unlawful killing of a human being *or a fetus* with malice aforethought). **§188** defines
> express vs. implied malice (and, post-SB 1437, limits imputed malice). **§189** fixes the
> degrees — first degree covers premeditated/deliberate killings, killing by destructive
> device, poison, lying in wait, or torture, the enumerated-felony murders, and **drive-by**
> shootings; all other murder is second degree. CA also recognizes **implied-malice "Watson"
> murder** — second-degree murder for a vehicular killing by a driver (often a previously-
> warned repeat-DUI offender) who acted with conscious disregard for life. `> ⚠️ VERIFY current §§187–189.`

### Felony Murder
🔴  ·  *CA-divergent — flag SB 1437*

**Rule.** A killing committed during the commission or attempted commission of an
inherently dangerous felony is murder. Limitations: the underlying felony must be
independent of the killing (the **merger** doctrine bars predicate assaults/manslaughter),
the death must be foreseeable and during the felony (res gestae), and under the agency
theory the killing must be by a felon, not a victim/police shooting a bystander.

**Here.** Here, the death occurred during [defendant]'s commission of [inherently dangerous
felony].

**Thus.** Thus, the killing is felony murder.

> **CA divergence:** California's **SB 1437** (Penal Code §§188–189) sharply narrowed
> felony murder — a non-killer is liable only if (1) the actual killer, (2) an aider/abettor
> who acted with intent to kill, or (3) a **major participant** in the felony who acted with
> **reckless indifference to human life**. Limits the natural-and-probable-consequences
> theory for murder.  `> ⚠️ VERIFY current §§188–189.`

### Voluntary Manslaughter
🟢

**Rule.** Voluntary manslaughter is an intentional killing mitigated by adequate
provocation — a heat of passion that would cause a reasonable person to lose self-control,
with no cooling-off period — or by imperfect (unreasonable but honest) self-defense.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] killed in the heat of passion after [adequate provocation],
before cooling off.

**Thus.** Thus, the killing is voluntary manslaughter, not murder.

### Involuntary Manslaughter
🟡

**Rule.** Involuntary manslaughter is an unintentional killing resulting from criminal
negligence (a gross deviation from the reasonable standard of care) or during the
commission of an unlawful act (a misdemeanor or a non-dangerous felony — the
misdemeanor-manslaughter rule).

**Here.** Here, [defendant]'s [grossly negligent act / misdemeanor] caused [victim]'s death.

**Thus.** Thus, [defendant] is guilty of involuntary manslaughter.

### Property Crimes — Distinguishing the Theft Offenses
🟢

**Rule.** **Larceny** is the trespassory taking and carrying away of another's personal
property with intent to permanently deprive at the time of taking. **Larceny by trick**
is larceny where possession is obtained by fraud. **False pretenses** is obtaining
**title** by a knowing false representation of fact with intent to defraud. **Embezzlement**
is the fraudulent conversion of property by one already in lawful possession. **Robbery**
is larceny from the person or presence of the victim by force or intimidation. **Burglary**
(common law) is breaking and entering the dwelling of another at night with intent to
commit a felony inside.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] [obtained possession/title] of [property] by [means], with
intent to [deprive/defraud].

**Thus.** Thus, the offense is [larceny / false pretenses / embezzlement / robbery /
burglary].

> **CA divergence (property crimes — high-yield):**
> • **Theft consolidated (§484/§487).** California folds larceny, larceny by trick,
>   embezzlement, and false pretenses into a single crime of **"theft,"** which may be charged
>   generically — but the prosecution must still prove the elements of **at least one** theory.
>   Value sets the grade: **grand theft** generally above **$950** (**§487**), petty theft
>   otherwise; under **Prop 47 (§490.2)** most thefts of property ≤ $950 are misdemeanor petty
>   theft regardless of the taking method.
> • **Burglary (§459/§460).** CA burglary requires **no breaking, no nighttime, and no
>   dwelling** — it is **entry** into any building, room, locked vehicle, etc. with intent to
>   commit larceny or **any** felony, the intent existing **at entry**. **§460** grades burglary
>   of an **inhabited dwelling** as first degree; all else is second degree. **Prop 47 (§459.5)**
>   carves out **"shoplifting"** — entering an **open** commercial establishment during business
>   hours intending to steal ≤ $950 — as a misdemeanor, not burglary.
> • **Robbery (§211).** Felonious taking of property from the person or immediate presence of
>   another, against his will, by **force or fear**. Under **People v. Estes**, force or fear used
>   to **retain** the property or **escape** (before reaching a place of temporary safety) elevates
>   a theft to robbery ("Estes robbery"). Under **People v. Tufunga**, a good-faith **claim of
>   right to specific property** negates the felonious-taking element (not available to collect a
>   debt). Degrees under §212.5 (inhabited dwelling, ATM user, transit driver = first degree).
> `> ⚠️ VERIFY current §§211, 459–460, 484–490.2.`

### Inchoate Crimes — Attempt, Solicitation, Conspiracy
🟢

**Rule.** **Attempt** requires the specific intent to commit a crime plus a substantial
step (or, at common law, an act dangerously close to completion) beyond mere preparation.
**Solicitation** is enticing, encouraging, or commanding another to commit a crime, with
intent that it be committed. **Conspiracy** is an agreement between two or more persons to
commit a crime, with intent to agree and intent to achieve the unlawful objective; most
jurisdictions also require an overt act in furtherance. Co-conspirators are liable for
crimes committed in furtherance and reasonably foreseeable (Pinkerton).

**Here.** Here, [defendant] [took a substantial step / agreed with another] intending
[the target crime].

**Thus.** Thus, [defendant] is guilty of [attempt/solicitation/conspiracy].

### Accomplice Liability
🟡

**Rule.** An accomplice is one who aids, abets, counsels, or encourages the principal with
the intent to assist and the intent that the crime be committed. An accomplice is liable
for the target crime and for other crimes that are the natural and probable consequences of
the criminal scheme.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] [encouraged/assisted] the principal intending the crime succeed.

**Thus.** Thus, [defendant] is liable as an accomplice.

### Defenses — Self-Defense, Insanity, Intoxication
🟡

**Rule.** **Self-defense** permits non-deadly force reasonably necessary to repel an
imminent unlawful threat, and deadly force where the defender reasonably believes deadly
force is about to be used against him (no duty to retreat in many states). **Insanity** is
tested by M'Naghten (defect of reason so as not to know the nature/quality of the act or
that it was wrong), irresistible impulse, the MPC substantial-capacity test, or Durham
(product test). **Voluntary intoxication** may negate specific intent only; **involuntary
intoxication** is treated like insanity.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] [reasonably defended against an imminent threat / lacked
capacity to know the act was wrong].

**Thus.** Thus, [defense] [applies / fails].

## CRIMINAL PROCEDURE

### Fourth Amendment — Threshold (Government Action, REP/Standing)
🟢

**Rule.** The Fourth Amendment protects against unreasonable searches and seizures by the
government. It applies only where the defendant has a reasonable expectation of privacy in
the place or thing searched (standing) and there is government action. A search occurs when
the government intrudes on a reasonable expectation of privacy or physically trespasses to
obtain information.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] had a reasonable expectation of privacy in [place], and the
[police] conducted a search/seizure.

**Thus.** Thus, the Fourth Amendment applies.

### Warrant Requirement & Probable Cause
🟢

**Rule.** A search or seizure generally requires a warrant issued by a neutral and
detached magistrate, supported by probable cause and describing with particularity the
place to be searched and items to be seized. Warrantless searches are per se unreasonable
unless an exception applies.

**Here.** Here, the police acted [with/without] a valid warrant supported by probable cause.

**Thus.** Thus, the search is presumptively [valid/invalid] absent an exception.

### Warrant Exceptions
🔴  ·  *memorize the list cold*

**Rule.** A warrantless search is reasonable if it fits an exception: (1) **search incident
to lawful arrest** (person + area within immediate control; vehicle if arrestee could
access it or evidence of the arrest crime is reasonably present), (2) **automobile**
(probable cause to believe a vehicle contains contraband/evidence — search any container
that may hold it), (3) **plain view** (officer lawfully present, incriminating nature
immediately apparent), (4) **consent** (voluntary, by one with apparent authority), (5)
**stop and frisk** (Terry — reasonable suspicion to stop; pat-down for weapons), (6)
**exigent circumstances** (hot pursuit, imminent destruction of evidence, emergency aid),
and (7) **inventory** searches per standard policy.

**Here.** Here, the search qualifies as [exception] because [facts].

**Thus.** Thus, the warrantless search was [reasonable/unreasonable].

### Exclusionary Rule & Fruit of the Poisonous Tree
🟢

**Rule.** Evidence obtained in violation of the Fourth, Fifth, or Sixth Amendments is
generally inadmissible in the prosecution's case-in-chief, as is derivative evidence ("fruit
of the poisonous tree"). The taint is removed by (1) an **independent source**, (2)
**inevitable discovery**, (3) **attenuation** of the connection, or (4) the **good-faith**
reliance on a defective warrant.

**Here.** Here, the [evidence] derived from the unlawful [search/confession], and [no
exception removes the taint].

**Thus.** Thus, the evidence must be suppressed.

> **CA divergence:** Under **Proposition 8** ("Truth-in-Evidence"), California courts admit
> all relevant evidence in criminal cases except as required by the **federal** Constitution
> (and certain statutory privileges) — so CA cannot exclude evidence on independent state
> constitutional grounds broader than federal law.

### Fifth Amendment — Miranda
🟢

**Rule.** Before **custodial interrogation**, police must warn a suspect of the right to
remain silent, that statements may be used against him, the right to counsel, and the right
to appointed counsel. Custody means a reasonable person would not feel free to leave (a
formal arrest or its functional equivalent); interrogation means express questioning or its
functional equivalent (words/actions likely to elicit an incriminating response). An
unwarned statement is inadmissible in the case-in-chief. The suspect may waive knowingly and
voluntarily; an unambiguous invocation of silence or counsel must be honored.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] was [in custody] and [interrogated] without/with valid warnings
and waiver.

**Thus.** Thus, the statement is [admissible/inadmissible].

### Sixth Amendment — Right to Counsel & Confrontation
🟡

**Rule.** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the initiation of formal
adversary proceedings and is **offense-specific**, applying to all critical stages.
Ineffective assistance is shown by deficient performance and prejudice (Strickland). The
Confrontation Clause bars admission of **testimonial** hearsay by an unavailable declarant
unless the defendant had a prior opportunity to cross-examine (Crawford).

**Here.** Here, [the right had attached for this offense / the statement was testimonial and
untested].

**Thus.** Thus, [the Sixth Amendment was violated / the statement is barred].

## Part 3 — Crossovers & Exam Tactics

- **Common pairings:** Crim Law ↔ Crim Pro in one fact pattern (the crime, then the
  search/confession). Confrontation Clause ↔ **Evidence** (hearsay). Identifications ↔ due
  process.
- **Sleeper issues:** the felony-murder merger doctrine; that voluntary intoxication negates
  only specific intent; standing in Fourth Amendment suppression; that Miranda's "interrogation"
  includes the functional equivalent; double jeopardy attaching at jury empanelment.
- **CA-divergence cheat list:** SB 1437 felony-murder limits; murder §§187–189 + implied-malice
  **Watson** murder; **theft consolidated** under §484/§487 with the **$950** grand/petty line and
  Prop 47 §490.2; **burglary §459/§460** (no breaking, nighttime, or dwelling required; §459.5
  shoplifting carve-out); **robbery §211** + **Estes** (force to retain/escape) + **Tufunga**
  (claim-of-right); arson §451 (reaches own property); Prop 8 truth-in-evidence; CA's
  two-/three-strikes sentencing (rarely essay-tested). `> ⚠️ VERIFY any CA statutory specifics before relying on them.`
- **Call-of-the-question patterns:** "With what crimes may D be charged?" → run every actor
  through each offense + inchoate + accomplice. "Should the evidence be suppressed?" → 4th/5th/6th
  chain + exclusionary exceptions.
