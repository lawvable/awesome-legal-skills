# Remedies — Essay Bank

> Remedies is rarely a standalone essay; it is the back half of a Torts or Contracts
> question. Organize by the two big buckets: **tort remedies** and **contract
> remedies**, each split legal / restitutionary / equitable. All rule language
> original. Flag uncertain law `> ⚠️ VERIFY`.

**High-frequency note:** The grader wants you to identify the wrong, then march
through the remedy menu: legal damages → restitution → equitable relief → defenses to
equity. The damages four-part test (causation, foreseeability, certainty, mitigation)
and the inadequate-legal-remedy gateway to injunctions/specific performance are the
anchors. Always pair with the underlying Torts or Contracts bank.

## Part 1 — Attack Outline (condensed, one-screen)

```
TORT REMEDIES
   LEGAL — DAMAGES
      ├─ compensatory — 4 reqs: causation, foreseeability, certainty, unavoidability(mitigation)
      ├─ nominal — vindicate rights, no actual injury
      ├─ punitive — needs compensatory/nominal first; fault > negligence; ~single-digit ratio
   RESTITUTIONARY (unjust enrichment — value to D)
      ├─ legal: restitutionary damages; replevin (personal prop); ejectment (real prop)
      ├─ equitable: constructive trust (value ↑), equitable lien (value ↓); tracing; BFP cuts off
   EQUITABLE — INJUNCTIONS
      ├─ TRO → preliminary (irreparable injury, likelihood of success, balance, bond) → permanent
      ├─ permanent: inadequate legal remedy, feasibility, balance of hardships, defenses
      ├─ defenses: unclean hands, laches, impossibility, free speech
CONTRACT REMEDIES
   LEGAL — DAMAGES (causation, foreseeability@formation/Hadley, certainty, mitigation)
      ├─ expectation (direct), consequential, incidental, nominal; NO punitive; liquidated
   RESTITUTION — value of benefit conferred (unenforceable K; breach by either party)
   EQUITABLE — specific performance (valid K, inadequate remedy, feasibility, mutuality, defenses)
                reformation; rescission
```

## Part 2 — IRAC Rule Blocks (full, memorizable)

### Compensatory Damages (the four requirements)
🟢

**Rule.** Compensatory damages place the injured party in the position they would have
occupied but for the wrong. They require (1) **causation** — the defendant's conduct was
the actual cause (but-for / substantial factor); (2) **foreseeability** — proximate
cause, the harm being foreseeable (in tort, at the time of the act; in contract, at the
time of contracting under Hadley v. Baxendale); (3) **certainty** — the amount is proven
with reasonable certainty and is not speculative; and (4) **unavoidability** — the
plaintiff must take reasonable steps to mitigate.

**Here.** Here, [defendant]'s conduct caused [harm], the loss was foreseeable, [P] can
prove $[amount] with reasonable certainty, and [P] [did/did not] mitigate.

**Thus.** Thus, [P] recovers $[amount] in compensatory damages.

### Punitive Damages
🟡

**Rule.** Punitive damages punish and deter and require that (1) the plaintiff first
recover compensatory, nominal, or restitutionary damages, (2) the defendant's fault
exceeds ordinary negligence (malice, oppression, fraud, or reckless indifference), and
(3) the award be reasonably proportionate to actual harm — due process generally caps
the ratio at a single digit absent extreme conduct.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] acted with [malice], and the requested amount bears a
reasonable ratio to the compensatory award.

**Thus.** Thus, punitive damages are [available/excessive].

### Restitutionary Damages (Unjust Enrichment)
🟡

**Rule.** Restitution measures the **benefit conferred on the defendant** rather than the
plaintiff's loss, preventing unjust enrichment. A plaintiff may not recover both
compensatory and restitutionary damages for the same harm but may plead both and elect
the larger.

**Here.** Here, [defendant] was enriched by $[value] at [P]'s expense.

**Thus.** Thus, [P] may recover that benefit in restitution.

### Replevin & Ejectment
🟡

**Rule.** **Replevin** recovers possession of specific personal property: the plaintiff
must show a right to possession and a wrongful withholding, often posting a bond, and may
add damages for loss of use. **Ejectment** recovers possession of specific real property
from one who is wrongfully in possession (e.g., a holdover tenant or adverse possessor),
enforced by the sheriff, with damages for the withholding.

**Here.** Here, [P] has a right to possession and [D] wrongfully withholds [the
chattel/land].

**Thus.** Thus, [replevin/ejectment] lies.

### Constructive Trust & Equitable Lien
🔴  ·  *cross-ref: Trusts, Wills*

**Rule.** Where legal remedies are inadequate (property is unique or the defendant is
insolvent) and the defendant holds title to improperly acquired property, equity imposes
a **constructive trust** — the defendant holds as trustee and must reconvey the property
(advantageous when the property has appreciated) — or an **equitable lien** — the property
is sold under court direction and proceeds go to the plaintiff, with a deficiency judgment
for any shortfall (advantageous when the property has depreciated). The plaintiff may
**trace** into substitute assets, but a bona fide purchaser for value without notice cuts
off the remedy.

**Here.** Here, [D] holds [P]'s [traced property], which has [appreciated/depreciated].

**Thus.** Thus, a [constructive trust / equitable lien] should be imposed.

### Injunctions — Preliminary & Permanent
🟢

**Rule.** A **preliminary injunction** preserves the status quo pending trial and requires
(1) irreparable injury without it, (2) likelihood of success on the merits, and (3) the
balance of hardships favoring the plaintiff, with a bond. A **permanent injunction** after
trial requires (1) an **inadequate legal remedy** (damages too speculative, defendant
insolvent, irreparable injury, or to avoid a multiplicity of suits), (2) **feasibility of
enforcement** (especially for mandatory injunctions needing supervision), (3) the
**balance of hardships** favoring the plaintiff, and (4) the absence of equitable
**defenses**.

**Here.** Here, [P] faces irreparable harm, damages are inadequate, enforcement is
feasible, and the hardships favor [P].

**Thus.** Thus, [a preliminary / permanent] injunction should issue.

### Equitable Defenses (Laches, Unclean Hands)
🟡

**Rule.** Equitable relief may be barred by **laches** (an unreasonable, prejudicial delay
in bringing suit), **unclean hands** (the plaintiff engaged in related improper conduct),
impossibility of performance, or, for speech, the First Amendment bar on enjoining
defamation.

**Here.** Here, [P] [unreasonably delayed and prejudiced D / acted improperly in the same
transaction].

**Thus.** Thus, the equitable claim is barred.

### Contract Damages — Expectation, Consequential, Liquidated
🟢  ·  *cross-ref: Contracts*

**Rule.** Contract damages protect the **expectation** interest — the benefit of the
bargain. **Consequential** (special) damages are recoverable only if foreseeable at the
time of contracting (Hadley). **Incidental** and **nominal** damages are allowed; **punitive
damages are not** available for breach of contract. **Liquidated damages** are enforceable
where (1) actual damages were difficult to estimate at formation and (2) the sum is a
reasonable forecast; an amount operating as a penalty is void, leaving actual damages. All
damages remain subject to certainty and the duty to mitigate.

**Here.** Here, [P]'s expectation loss is $[amount], plus foreseeable consequential losses,
and the liquidated clause is [a reasonable forecast / a penalty].

**Thus.** Thus, [P] recovers [measure].

### Contract Restitution
🟡

**Rule.** Restitution returns the value of any benefit conferred to prevent unjust
enrichment. When a contract is unenforceable, a plaintiff recovers the reasonable value of
the benefit conferred even above the contract rate. On breach, the non-breaching party may
elect restitution; a breaching party may, under the modern view, recover for benefits
conferred, capped at the contract rate and offset by the other party's damages.

**Here.** Here, [P] conferred a benefit worth $[value] before the contract [failed/was
breached].

**Thus.** Thus, [P] recovers that value in restitution.

### Specific Performance
🟢  ·  *cross-ref: Contracts (land, unique goods)*

**Rule.** Specific performance compels contractual performance and requires (1) a valid
contract with sufficiently certain and definite terms, (2) satisfaction of all conditions
(plaintiff has performed or is ready and able), (3) an **inadequate legal remedy** (the
subject matter is unique — land is always unique, as are rare goods), (4) feasibility of
enforcement (courts will not specifically enforce personal-service contracts), and (5) the
absence of equitable defenses (laches, unclean hands, sale to a BFP). Mutuality concerns
are largely satisfied if the plaintiff can be compelled to perform.

**Here.** Here, the contract is for [unique land/goods], damages are inadequate, and [P]
has performed.

**Thus.** Thus, specific performance is [available/unavailable].

### Reformation & Rescission
🟡

**Rule.** **Rescission** unwinds a contract and restores the parties to their precontract
positions where grounds exist (mutual mistake, misrepresentation, duress, etc.).
**Reformation** rewrites a writing to reflect the parties' true agreement where, due to
mutual mistake (or fraud), the writing fails to express it.

**Here.** Here, the writing [misstates the agreed term due to mutual mistake / was induced
by fraud].

**Thus.** Thus, [rescission / reformation] is the proper remedy.

## Part 3 — Crossovers & Exam Tactics

- **Common pairings:** Remedies is the tail of a **Torts** or **Contracts** essay; constructive
  trust/equitable lien bridge to **Trusts** and **Wills**; specific performance bridges to
  **Real Property** land contracts.
- **Sleeper issues:** electing between compensatory and restitutionary (take the larger); the
  no-punitives rule in contract; mitigation; the inadequate-legal-remedy gateway as the first
  question for any equitable remedy; tracing limits and the BFP cutoff.
- **CA notes:** California follows the general restitution/equity framework; CA recognizes
  constructive trusts and equitable liens (Civ. Code §2224). `> ⚠️ VERIFY` specific CA punitive
  damages procedure (Civ. Code §3294, bifurcation) if tested.
- **Call-of-the-question patterns:** "What remedies are available to P?" → run legal damages →
  restitution → equitable, with defenses. "Can P get the [item/land] back?" → replevin/ejectment
  or specific performance / constructive trust.
