# Trusts — Essay Bank

> CA tests general trust law under the CA Probate Code. The anchors are **creation of a
> valid private express trust**, **trustee duties**, and the **various other trusts**
> (charitable, resulting, constructive). Frequently crossed with **Wills** and
> **Community Property**. All rule language original. Flag uncertain law `> ⚠️ VERIFY`.

**High-frequency note:** Most Trusts essays start by testing whether a valid trust was
created (the five elements), then run trustee duties and remedies, then sweep the
non-express trusts (resulting/constructive) and restrictive provisions (spendthrift). Cy
pres and the charitable-trust rules are recurring satellites.

## Part 1 — Attack Outline (condensed, one-screen)

```
PRIVATE EXPRESS TRUST — 5 elements: settlor + intent + res(property) + trustee + beneficiary + legal purpose
   ├─ intent — present manifestation; mandatory not precatory words
   ├─ res — existing transferable interest; no illusory/expectancy
   ├─ trustee — needed but court can appoint; trust won't fail for lack of trustee
   ├─ beneficiary — ascertainable person/group (charitable = exception)
   ├─ creation — inter vivos (transfer in trust / declaration in trust) vs testamentary (in will)
TRUSTEE DUTIES (7) — loyalty, due care/prudence, account, invest (prudent investor + diversify),
                      earmark, segregate, not to delegate
   ├─ remedies of beneficiary — damages/surcharge, constructive trust, tracing/equitable lien, remove trustee
   ├─ liability to 3rd persons — contract & tort (CL personal / modern fault-based)
VARIOUS OTHER TRUSTS
   ├─ charitable — public benefit, no ascertainable ben, RAP n/a, cy pres
   ├─ honorary — no ben, no public benefit; RAP problem
   ├─ resulting — implied-in-fact; reverts to settlor; purchase-money; semi-secret
   ├─ constructive — equitable remedy for fraud/unjust enrichment; secret trust; oral real estate trust
   ├─ totten trust — bank account POD
RESTRICTIVE PROVISIONS — spendthrift, support, discretionary
MODIFICATION / TERMINATION — settlor power, Claflin, changed circumstances
```

## Part 2 — IRAC Rule Blocks (full, memorizable)

### Private Express Trust — Creation
🟢  ·  *lead with this*

**Rule.** A private express trust is a fiduciary relationship in which a trustee holds legal
title to property for the benefit of beneficiaries, arising from the settlor's manifested
intent to create it for a legal purpose. It requires (1) a **settlor** with capacity who (2)
manifests **present intent** to create a trust, (3) over identifiable **trust property** (the
res), (4) for the benefit of **ascertainable beneficiaries**, with (5) a **trustee** holding
legal title for a (6) **valid legal purpose**.

**Here.** Here, [settlor] manifested intent to hold [property] for [beneficiary]'s benefit.

**Thus.** Thus, a valid private express trust [was/was not] created.

### Intent & the Precatory-Words Problem
🟡

**Rule.** The settlor must presently manifest an intent to impose enforceable duties on a
trustee; no particular words are required, but **mandatory** language is needed. Merely
**precatory** language (hope, wish, desire) is presumptively insufficient and leaves the
transferee owning outright, though precatory words plus corroborating parol evidence may
establish a trust.

**Here.** Here, [settlor]'s words were [mandatory / merely precatory].

**Thus.** Thus, the intent element is [satisfied/not satisfied].

### Trust Property (Res)
🟡

**Rule.** The res must be a presently existing interest in property that can be transferred
(including a future interest or a life-insurance policy). An illusory interest, a mere
expectancy, or a promise of future profits cannot be a res.

**Here.** Here, [the asset] is a presently existing, transferable interest.

**Thus.** Thus, a valid res exists.

### Creation — Inter Vivos vs. Testamentary
🟡  ·  *cross-ref: Wills*

**Rule.** A trust effective at death must satisfy the will formalities (be a testamentary
trust in a valid will). A lifetime trust is created either by a **transfer in trust** (a
third party is trustee — for real property the settlor must execute and deliver a deed; for
personal property, deliver the property) or a **declaration in trust** (the settlor is
trustee — for real property a writing satisfying the Statute of Frauds; for personal
property, a present manifestation of intent).

**Here.** Here, [settlor] [delivered the deed to the trustee / declared himself trustee in
writing].

**Thus.** Thus, the trust was validly created during life.

### Trustee Duty of Loyalty
🟢

**Rule.** The trustee must administer the trust solely in the beneficiaries' interest. Under
the **no-further-inquiry** rule, self-dealing (buying or selling trust assets to itself or a
spouse, or borrowing trust funds) is a breach regardless of good faith or fairness. On
breach causing loss the trustee is surcharged; on profit the trustee holds it as constructive
trustee.

**Here.** Here, [trustee] [engaged in self-dealing] with trust assets.

**Thus.** Thus, [trustee] breached the duty of loyalty.

### Trustee Duty of Care & Prudent Investment
🟢

**Rule.** The trustee must act as a reasonably prudent person managing the trust. The modern
**Uniform Prudent Investor Act** judges investment decisions on the **entire portfolio**
(not asset-by-asset), measured by the prudent-investor standard, and imposes a duty to
**diversify** so that a single loss does not destroy the trust.

**Here.** Here, [trustee] [diversified / imprudently concentrated] the portfolio.

**Thus.** Thus, [trustee] [satisfied / breached] the duty of prudent investment.

### Other Trustee Duties (Account, Earmark, Segregate, No Delegation)
🟡

**Rule.** The trustee must also (1) **account** — periodically report income and expenses; (2)
**earmark** trust property as such (modern view: liable only if the failure caused loss); (3)
**segregate** — not commingle trust assets with personal assets or other trusts; and (4) not
improperly **delegate** discretionary decisions (the trustee may rely on advisors but cannot
delegate the decision itself). Co-trustees must generally act unanimously.

**Here.** Here, [trustee] [failed to earmark / commingled / delegated discretion].

**Thus.** Thus, [trustee] breached and is liable for the resulting loss.

### Beneficiary Remedies
🟡

**Rule.** For a breach the beneficiary may seek damages (surcharge), impose a **constructive
trust** on wrongfully held property, **trace** and assert an equitable lien (cut off by a
BFP without notice), ratify a beneficial transaction, or **remove** the trustee.

**Here.** Here, [trustee]'s breach caused [loss / wrongful gain].

**Thus.** Thus, the beneficiary may [surcharge / impose a constructive trust / remove the
trustee].

### Charitable Trusts & Cy Pres
🟢

**Rule.** A charitable trust is created for a purpose conferring a benefit on the public or a
segment of it; it has **no ascertainable beneficiary** (society benefits) and is **not subject
to the Rule Against Perpetuities**. Where the settlor's specific charitable purpose becomes
impossible or impracticable but a **general charitable intent** is found, the court may apply
**cy pres** to modify the trust to fulfill that intent as nearly as possible.

**Here.** Here, the trust serves [a charitable purpose], and [its specific mechanism failed
but general charitable intent appears].

**Thus.** Thus, the court may [uphold the charitable trust / apply cy pres].

### Resulting Trusts
🟡

**Rule.** A resulting trust is implied from the parties' presumed intent and returns the
property to the settlor or the settlor's estate. It arises when an express trust ends without
disposition of the property, fails for lack of a beneficiary or illegality, has excess corpus,
or in the **purchase-money** situation (one who pays for property titled in another is presumed
the beneficiary, absent a close relationship implying a gift) and the **semi-secret** trust
(a will gift to a person "as trustee" without naming a beneficiary).

**Here.** Here, [the express trust failed / A paid for property titled in B].

**Thus.** Thus, a resulting trust arises in favor of [settlor/payor].

### Constructive Trusts
🔴  ·  *cross-ref: Remedies, Wills*

**Rule.** A constructive trust is an equitable remedy, not a true trust, imposed to prevent
fraud or unjust enrichment: the wrongful holder is deemed a trustee and must convey the
property to the rightful party. It arises from a trustee's self-dealing profit, fraud or undue
influence in procuring a will, an **oral real-estate trust** where the Statute of Frauds cannot
be invoked (fiduciary relationship, fraud in the inducement, or detrimental reliance), or a
**secret trust** (an outright gift made on an oral promise to hold for another — parol evidence
admissible).

**Here.** Here, [holder] obtained [property] through [fraud / a broken oral promise].

**Thus.** Thus, a constructive trust should be imposed.

### Restrictive Provisions — Spendthrift, Support, Discretionary
🟡

**Rule.** A **spendthrift** provision bars the beneficiary from voluntarily transferring, and
creditors from attaching, the right to future payments (but a settlor cannot shield his own
interest in a self-settled trust from creditors). A **support** provision limits distributions
to the beneficiary's health, education, support, and maintenance. A **discretionary** provision
gives the trustee sole discretion; the beneficiary's creditors cannot compel payment, but if
the trustee elects to pay after notice of an assignment or judgment, payment must go to the
assignee/creditor.

**Here.** Here, the trust contains a [spendthrift/support/discretionary] clause.

**Thus.** Thus, the beneficiary's interest is [protected from creditors / limited].

### Modification & Termination
🟡

**Rule.** The settlor may modify or revoke if that power is reserved (and in California a trust
is presumptively **revocable** unless stated otherwise). A court may modify or terminate where
all beneficiaries consent and no material trust purpose remains (the Claflin doctrine bars
termination that defeats a material purpose), or under its equitable power when unanticipated
changed circumstances would defeat the settlor's intent.

**Here.** Here, [the settlor reserved revocation / all beneficiaries consent and no material
purpose remains].

**Thus.** Thus, the trust may be [revoked/modified/terminated].

> **CA divergence:** Under CA Probate Code §15400, a trust is **presumed revocable** unless the
> instrument expressly states it is irrevocable — opposite the common-law default.

## Part 3 — Crossovers & Exam Tactics

- **Common pairings:** Trusts ↔ **Wills** (testamentary trusts, pour-over, secret/semi-secret);
  ↔ **Community Property** (trusts of community assets); ↔ **Remedies** (constructive trust,
  tracing).
- **Sleeper issues:** the no-further-inquiry rule (good faith is irrelevant to self-dealing);
  RAP does not apply to charitable trusts; semi-secret = resulting trust but secret =
  constructive trust; self-settled spendthrift trusts don't protect the settlor.
- **CA notes:** CA trusts presumed revocable (Prob. Code §15400); CA has adopted the Uniform
  Prudent Investor Act. `> ⚠️ VERIFY` specific Probate Code sections before relying on numbers.
- **Call-of-the-question patterns:** "Is there a valid trust?" → five elements. "Did the trustee
  breach?" → run the seven duties + remedies. "Who takes the property?" → sort through resulting
  vs. constructive trust.
