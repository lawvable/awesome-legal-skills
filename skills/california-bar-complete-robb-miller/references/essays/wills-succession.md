# Wills & Succession — Essay Bank

> CA tests succession under the CA Probate Code, which has notable divergences
> (holographic wills valid, generous pretermitted-heir and undue-influence statutes).
> Anchors: **execution/validity**, **components**, **revocation**, **capacity & undue
> influence**, and **changes after execution** (lapse/ademption/pretermission).
> Frequently crossed with **Trusts** and **Community Property**. All rule language
> original. Flag uncertain law `> ⚠️ VERIFY`.

**High-frequency note:** Run validity first (was the will properly executed? holographic?),
then capacity/undue influence/fraud, then construe the gifts (lapse, anti-lapse, ademption,
class gifts), then revocation/revival/DRR, then who-takes (intestacy, pretermitted heirs,
spouse's share). California's community-property death rules tie in heavily.

## Part 1 — Attack Outline (condensed, one-screen)

```
INTESTACY — no/partial valid will → spouse + issue shares; CA community vs separate property split
EXECUTION / VALIDITY
   ├─ attested will — writing, signed by T, 2 witnesses present at same time, who understand it's a will
   ├─ holographic will (CA valid) — signature + material provisions in T's handwriting; intent
   ├─ interested-witness; substantial compliance / harmless error
CAPACITY & DEFECTS
   ├─ testamentary capacity — 18 + understand property, natural objects, nature of act
   ├─ insane delusion — false belief, sick mind, affected will
   ├─ undue influence — prima facie / presumption / CA statutory (Prob. Code §21380)
   ├─ fraud — in execution / in inducement / preventing revocation
   ├─ mistake — execution, inducement, description (ambiguity), content, DRR
COMPONENTS
   ├─ integration; incorporation by reference; facts of independent significance; pour-over wills; codicils
REVOCATION
   ├─ by physical act; by subsequent instrument; by operation of law (divorce, omitted spouse)
   ├─ revival; dependent relative revocation (DRR)
CHANGES AFTER EXECUTION
   ├─ lapse & anti-lapse; class gifts; ademption (by extinction / satisfaction); abatement; exoneration
PROTECTED PERSONS
   ├─ pretermitted (omitted) child; omitted spouse; (no forced share for issue in CA)
WILL CONTESTS — standing, no-contest clauses, slayer rule
```

## Part 2 — IRAC Rule Blocks (full, memorizable)

### Attested Will — Formalities
🟢  ·  *lead with validity*

**Rule.** A valid attested will requires that (1) it be in **writing**, (2) **signed** by the
testator (or by another in the testator's presence at her direction, or a conservator by court
order), and (3) signed or acknowledged in the **joint presence of two witnesses** who (4)
understand they are witnessing the testator's will. The witnesses need not sign in each other's
or the testator's presence, and no particular order or location of signatures is required.

**Here.** Here, [testator] signed in the presence of two witnesses who understood the
instrument was her will.

**Thus.** Thus, the will was validly executed.

### Holographic Will
🟢  ·  *CA-valid — high-yield*

**Rule.** California recognizes a **holographic** will, valid without witnesses, if the
**signature and the material provisions are in the testator's own handwriting** and the
testator intended the document to be a will. Testamentary intent may be shown by the document
or by extrinsic evidence, and pre-printed words may be considered to establish intent.

**Here.** Here, [testator] handwrote and signed the dispositive terms intending a will.

**Thus.** Thus, the holographic will is valid.

> **CA divergence:** Many states do **not** recognize holographic wills; California does (Prob.
> Code §6111). California also applies a **harmless-error / clear-and-convincing** dispensing
> power (§6110(c)(2)) to excuse some attestation defects. `> ⚠️ VERIFY` current §6110.

### Testamentary Capacity
🟢

**Rule.** The testator must be at least 18 and, at execution, able to understand (1) the nature
and extent of her property, (2) the natural objects of her bounty (spouse, children, those whose
interests the will affects), and (3) the nature of the act of making a will. The same capacity
standard governs revocation. Lack of capacity invalidates the entire will, passing property by
intestacy (or a prior valid will the defective one purported to revoke).

**Here.** Here, [testator] [could/could not] understand her property, heirs, and the testamentary
act.

**Thus.** Thus, the will is [valid/invalid for lack of capacity].

### Insane Delusion
🟡

**Rule.** An insane delusion invalidates the affected portion of a will where (1) the testator
held a false belief, (2) the belief was the product of a sick mind, (3) there was no factual
basis for it (California: not even a scintilla of supporting evidence), and (4) the delusion
affected the disposition. The tainted gift falls into the residue or passes by intestacy.

**Here.** Here, [testator]'s baseless belief about [fact] caused the gift to [beneficiary].

**Thus.** Thus, the affected disposition is invalid.

### Undue Influence
🔴  ·  *CA statutory presumption — memorize*

**Rule.** Undue influence subjugates the testator's free will. It may be shown three ways: a
**prima facie case** (susceptibility, opportunity, active participation, and an unnatural result
benefiting the wrongdoer); a **common-law presumption** (a confidential relationship, active
participation in procuring the will, and an unnatural result/undue benefit); or by **statute**
(CA Prob. Code §21380 presumes a donative transfer invalid if made to the drafter, a person in a
fiduciary relationship who transcribed the instrument, a care custodian of a dependent adult, or
those persons' relatives/cohabitants — rebuttable for some, conclusive for the drafter), subject
to exceptions (e.g., a transfer to the testator's spouse or one reviewed by an independent
attorney).

**Here.** Here, [influencer] [held a confidential relationship and procured an unnatural gift /
falls within the statutory class].

**Thus.** Thus, the gift is presumed the product of undue influence.

### Fraud
🟡

**Rule.** A will or gift procured by fraud — a knowing misrepresentation of material fact made
to induce, and actually inducing, the testamentary act — is invalid. **Fraud in the execution**
(testator deceived about the nature of the document) voids the entire will; **fraud in the
inducement** (false facts affecting the will's content) voids only the affected gift; **fraud
preventing revocation** leads to a constructive trust for the intended taker.

**Here.** Here, [wrongdoer] [deceived the testator about the document / supplied false facts that
shaped the gift].

**Thus.** Thus, [the will/the affected gift] is invalid [or a constructive trust is imposed].

### Components — Incorporation by Reference & Independent Significance
🟡

**Rule.** **Integration** treats as the will all papers present and intended at execution.
**Incorporation by reference** brings in an extrinsic writing if (1) it existed when the will was
executed, (2) the will manifests intent to incorporate it, and (3) it is clearly identified.
**Facts of independent significance** allow gifts to be determined by reference to acts or events
having significance apart from the will (e.g., "the car I own at death").

**Here.** Here, the will referred to [a writing in existence and identified / an act of
independent significance].

**Thus.** Thus, the extrinsic matter is given effect.

### Pour-Over Wills
🟡  ·  *cross-ref: Trusts*

**Rule.** A pour-over provision devising estate assets to an inter vivos trust is valid if
supported by incorporation by reference, facts of independent significance, or the **Uniform
Testamentary Additions to Trusts Act** (a valid trust executed before or concurrently with the
will, even if later amended).

**Here.** Here, the will pours over to [the inter vivos trust] validly created under UTATA.

**Thus.** Thus, the pour-over is effective.

### Revocation
🟢

**Rule.** A will may be revoked (1) by a **physical act** (burning, tearing, canceling,
destroying with intent to revoke), (2) by a **subsequent instrument** (a later will or codicil
expressly or by inconsistency), or (3) by **operation of law** (in California, dissolution of
marriage revokes provisions for the former spouse). Revoking a later will does not automatically
revive an earlier one unless the testator so intends (revival).

**Here.** Here, [testator] [destroyed the will with intent / executed a later inconsistent will /
divorced].

**Thus.** Thus, the will (or the affected gift) was revoked.

### Dependent Relative Revocation (DRR)
🟡

**Rule.** Under DRR, if the testator revokes a will (or part) under the mistaken belief that a
substantially identical instrument or disposition will take effect, and it does not, the
revocation is deemed conditional and is disregarded, reviving the original disposition — applied
only where it comes closer to the testator's intent than intestacy.

**Here.** Here, [testator] revoked believing [the new gift] would be effective, but it failed.

**Thus.** Thus, DRR ignores the revocation and the original gift stands.

### Lapse & Anti-Lapse
🟢

**Rule.** A gift **lapses** if the beneficiary predeceases the testator, falling into the residue
(or intestacy). An **anti-lapse** statute saves the gift for the predeceasing beneficiary's issue
where the beneficiary was a relative of the testator (in California, kindred of the testator or of
a former spouse) who left issue who survive.

**Here.** Here, [beneficiary], a [relative], predeceased the testator leaving issue.

**Thus.** Thus, the anti-lapse statute passes the gift to that issue.

### Ademption
🟡

**Rule.** A **specific** gift adeems (fails) by **extinction** if the specific property is not in
the estate at death (modern/CA approaches may give the beneficiary the property's value or
proceeds in some circumstances). Ademption **by satisfaction** occurs when the testator gives the
property to the beneficiary during life intending it to satisfy the bequest.

**Here.** Here, the specifically devised [property] [is no longer in the estate / was given to the
beneficiary during life].

**Thus.** Thus, the gift adeems.

### Pretermitted (Omitted) Children & Spouse
🔴  ·  *CA-divergent — memorize*

**Rule.** A child born or adopted **after** all testamentary instruments are executed and not
provided for receives an **intestate share** (pretermitted child); a child living when the will
was executed who is omitted generally takes nothing, unless the omission resulted from the
testator's mistaken belief the child was dead or nonexistent. Similarly, a spouse the testator
**marries after** executing the will, and who is not provided for, receives a statutory share
(omitted spouse) unless the omission was intentional or provided for outside the will.

**Here.** Here, [child/spouse] came into the family after execution and was unmentioned.

**Thus.** Thus, [child/spouse] takes a statutory/intestate share.

### Intestate Succession (CA)
🟢  ·  *cross-ref: Community Property*

**Rule.** When a decedent dies without a valid will, the estate passes by intestacy. In
California, the surviving spouse takes **all community and quasi-community property** and a share
of the separate property (all if no issue/parent/sibling; one-half if one child or no issue but a
parent/sibling; one-third if more than one child), with the remaining separate property passing
to issue, then parents, then collaterals.

**Here.** Here, [decedent] died intestate survived by [spouse and issue].

**Thus.** Thus, the spouse takes the community property and [share] of the separate property.

> **CA divergence:** California's intestacy turns on the **community/separate-property
> characterization** of each asset — always run the Community Property analysis on death. `> ⚠️
> VERIFY` exact separate-property fractions in Prob. Code §6401.

### Will Contests — Slayer Rule & No-Contest Clauses
🟡

**Rule.** Only an interested person with standing may contest a will. A **slayer** who feloniously
and intentionally kills the decedent is treated as having predeceased and cannot take. A
**no-contest** (in terrorem) clause forfeits a contestant's gift, but California enforces it only
against a contest brought **without probable cause**.

**Here.** Here, [contestant] [killed the decedent / challenged the will].

**Thus.** Thus, [the slayer is barred / the no-contest clause does/does not forfeit the gift].

## Part 3 — Crossovers & Exam Tactics

- **Common pairings:** Wills ↔ **Trusts** (testamentary & pour-over trusts, secret/semi-secret
  trusts → constructive/resulting trust); ↔ **Community Property** (always characterize assets at
  death); ↔ **Remedies** (constructive trust for fraud/undue influence).
- **Sleeper issues:** holographic validity; DRR; anti-lapse only for relatives with surviving
  issue; pretermitted status keyed to timing of execution; divorce revoking spousal gifts; the
  same capacity standard for making and revoking.
- **CA-divergence cheat list:** holographic wills valid (§6111); harmless-error dispensing power
  (§6110(c)(2)); statutory undue-influence presumption (§21380); no-contest clause enforced only
  without probable cause; spouse takes all CP/QCP at death; generous omitted-child/spouse statutes.
- **Call-of-the-question patterns:** "Is the will valid?" → execution → capacity → undue
  influence/fraud. "Who takes [asset]?" → construe gifts (lapse/ademption) → protected persons →
  intestacy with CP characterization.
