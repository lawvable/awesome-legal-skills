# Contracts — Essay Bank

> CA tests general (multistate) contract law: common law for services/land, **UCC
> Article 2** for goods. The recurring divergence axis is **Common Law vs UCC** —
> flag it everywhere. Contracts essays frequently cross into **Remedies**. All rule
> language original. Flag uncertain law `> ⚠️ VERIFY`.

**High-frequency note:** Almost every Contracts essay opens with the law-source
question (CL vs UCC), then walks formation → defenses → terms → performance/breach →
remedies. Battle-of-forms (§2-207), the parol evidence rule, anticipatory repudiation,
and the expectation/consequential-damages chain are perennial anchors.

## Part 2 — IRAC Rule Blocks (full, memorizable)

### Applicable Law — Common Law vs UCC
🟢

**Rule.** The common law governs contracts for services and real property; UCC Article
2 governs contracts for the sale of goods (movable, tangible property). For a mixed
contract, courts apply the **predominant-purpose** test, applying one body of law to the
whole contract based on its primary thrust (unless the contract divides payment by
portion).

**Here.** Here, the contract is principally for [goods/services], so [UCC/common law]
governs.

**Thus.** Thus, [UCC Art. 2 / the common law] supplies the governing rules.

### Offer
🟢

**Rule.** An offer is an outward manifestation of present contractual intent,
communicated to the offeree, with terms sufficiently definite and certain that
acceptance forms a contract. Under the UCC, an offer may leave open terms (price,
delivery) so long as the parties intend to contract and there is a reasonably certain
basis for a remedy.

**Here.** Here, [offeror] communicated [the terms] manifesting intent to be bound on
acceptance.

**Thus.** Thus, a valid offer [exists/does not exist].

### Termination of the Offer
🟢

**Rule.** An offer terminates by (1) revocation communicated before acceptance, (2)
rejection or counteroffer by the offeree, (3) lapse of a stated or reasonable time, or
(4) operation of law (death/incapacity of either party, destruction of the subject
matter, or supervening illegality).

**Here.** Here, the offer was [revoked/rejected/lapsed] before [offeree] accepted.

**Thus.** Thus, there was no offer left to accept.

### Irrevocable Offers
🟡

**Rule.** An offer is irrevocable where (1) an **option** is supported by consideration,
(2) under the UCC a **firm offer** by a merchant promises in a signed writing to hold the
offer open (irrevocable for the stated time, not to exceed 90 days), (3) the offeree has
begun performance of a **unilateral** contract (option implied, completion permitted), or
(4) the offeree **foreseeably and detrimentally relied** on the offer.

**Here.** Here, [the firm-offer writing / part performance / reliance] made the offer
irrevocable.

**Thus.** Thus, the offeror could not revoke when he attempted to.

### Acceptance
🟢

**Rule.** Acceptance is an objective manifestation of assent to the terms of the offer
by the person to whom it was addressed. At common law, acceptance must mirror the offer's
terms (mirror-image rule); a varying response is a counteroffer. A bilateral offer is
accepted by a return promise; a unilateral offer is accepted only by completing the
requested performance.

**Here.** Here, [offeree] assented by [promise/performance].

**Thus.** Thus, acceptance [was/was not] effective.

### Battle of the Forms (UCC §2-207)
🔴  ·  *high-yield UCC divergence*

**Rule.** Under the UCC, a definite acceptance or written confirmation operates as an
acceptance even though it states additional or different terms, unless acceptance is
expressly conditioned on assent to the new terms. Between merchants, additional terms
become part of the contract unless they materially alter it, the offer limited acceptance
to its terms, or the offeror objects within a reasonable time. Conduct recognizing a
contract forms one even if the writings don't match (terms = agreed terms + gap-fillers).

**Here.** Here, [offeree]'s form added [term], and because [both are merchants and the
term does/does not materially alter], it [is/is not] part of the contract.

**Thus.** Thus, the contract includes [the disputed term or only the gap-fillers].

> **Divergence:** Common law uses the **mirror-image rule** (additional terms = rejection
> + counteroffer; last-shot doctrine). The UCC's §2-207 rejects that for sales of goods.

### Mailbox Rule
🟡

**Rule.** An acceptance is effective upon dispatch (mailbox rule), provided it is properly
addressed and sent by a reasonable means. A rejection is effective on receipt. If the
offeree sends a rejection then an acceptance, whichever arrives first controls. The rule
does not apply to option contracts, which require receipt by the deadline.

**Here.** Here, [offeree] dispatched acceptance [before/after] the offer was revoked.

**Thus.** Thus, a contract was [formed on dispatch / not formed].

### Consideration
🟢

**Rule.** Consideration is a bargained-for exchange in which each party incurs legal
detriment — doing (or promising to do) something one is not legally obligated to do, or
forbearing something one has a right to do. A promise to perform a **pre-existing legal
duty** is not consideration at common law.

**Here.** Here, [party] [incurred detriment / merely promised an existing duty].

**Thus.** Thus, the promise [is/is not] supported by consideration.

> **Divergence:** Modification — at **common law** a modification needs new consideration
> (pre-existing duty rule), excused only by unforeseen difficulties; under the **UCC** a
> good-faith modification needs **no new consideration**.

### Promissory Estoppel (Detrimental Reliance)
🟡

**Rule.** A promise is enforceable without consideration where the promisor should
reasonably expect it to induce action or forbearance, the promisee does foreseeably and
detrimentally rely, and injustice can be avoided only by enforcement. Recovery may be
limited to reliance damages.

**Here.** Here, [promisee] reasonably relied to its detriment on [the promise].

**Thus.** Thus, the promise is enforceable under promissory estoppel.

### Statute of Frauds
🟢

**Rule.** Certain contracts must be evidenced by a writing signed by the party to be
charged: contracts in consideration of **M**arriage, that cannot be performed within one
**Y**ear, involving **L**and, by an **E**xecutor to pay estate debts personally, for the
sale of **G**oods $500 or more, and **S**uretyship (MY-LEGS). Exceptions include part
performance (land), full performance, judicial admission, merchant confirmatory memo, and
specially manufactured goods.

**Here.** Here, the contract falls within the SOF as a [land/goods ≥$500] contract, and
[no writing exists / an exception applies].

**Thus.** Thus, the contract is [unenforceable / enforceable under an exception].

### Mistake
🟡

**Rule.** A **mutual** mistake — both parties err about a basic assumption materially
affecting the exchange — makes the contract voidable by the adversely affected party,
unless that party bore the risk. A **unilateral** mistake generally does not avoid the
contract unless the non-mistaken party knew or should have known of the mistake, or
enforcement would be unconscionable.

**Here.** Here, [both parties / only one party] were mistaken about [basic fact].

**Thus.** Thus, the contract is [voidable / enforceable].

### Misrepresentation, Duress, Undue Influence
🟡

**Rule.** A contract is voidable for **misrepresentation** (a false assertion of fact,
material or fraudulent, justifiably relied upon), **duress** (an improper threat leaving
no reasonable alternative), or **undue influence** (unfair persuasion of a party under
the domination of, or in a relation of trust with, the other).

**Here.** Here, [party] induced assent by [false statement / improper threat / abuse of a
confidential relationship].

**Thus.** Thus, the contract is voidable.

### Unconscionability
🟡

**Rule.** A court may refuse to enforce a contract or clause that is unconscionable at
the time of formation, requiring both **procedural** unconscionability (unfair surprise,
absence of meaningful choice, adhesion) and **substantive** unconscionability (oppressive
or grossly one-sided terms).

**Here.** Here, the [term] was both unfairly imposed and substantively oppressive.

**Thus.** Thus, the court may decline to enforce it.

### Parol Evidence Rule
🔴  ·  *frequently tested, frequently missed*

**Rule.** Where the parties intend a writing to be a complete and final integration,
the parol evidence rule bars evidence of prior or contemporaneous agreements that
contradict or supplement the writing. It does **not** bar evidence to (1) show a defense
to formation (fraud, mistake, duress), (2) explain an ambiguity, (3) prove a condition
precedent to effectiveness, (4) show a separate consideration, or (5) supply consistent
additional terms to a partial integration. Under the UCC, course of dealing, usage of
trade, and course of performance may always explain or supplement.

**Here.** Here, the prior [oral term] [contradicts / merely explains] the integrated
writing.

**Thus.** Thus, the evidence is [barred / admissible].

### Warranties (UCC)
🟡

**Rule.** Under the UCC a seller may make an **express warranty** (affirmation of fact,
promise, description, or sample that becomes part of the basis of the bargain); an
**implied warranty of merchantability** (goods fit for ordinary purposes — only by a
merchant of such goods); and an **implied warranty of fitness for a particular purpose**
(seller knows buyer's purpose and that buyer relies on seller's skill). Implied warranties
may be disclaimed by conspicuous language ("as is") or course of dealing.

**Here.** Here, [seller] [made an affirmation / is a merchant of such goods], creating a
[warranty], which was [not effectively disclaimed].

**Thus.** Thus, the warranty applies and was breached.

### Conditions
🟡

**Rule.** A condition is an event, not certain to occur, that must occur before
performance is due (condition precedent) or that discharges a duty (condition subsequent).
Express conditions require **strict** compliance; constructive conditions require only
substantial compliance. Conditions may be excused by waiver, estoppel, prevention/
hindrance by the benefited party, or to avoid forfeiture.

**Here.** Here, performance was conditioned on [event], which [did not occur / was excused
by the other party's interference].

**Thus.** Thus, the duty to perform [was/was not] discharged.

### Performance & Breach — Substantial Performance vs Perfect Tender
🔴  ·  *core CL/UCC divergence*

**Rule.** At common law, a party who **substantially performs** (no material breach) may
recover on the contract less damages for the deficiency; a **material** breach excuses
the other party and permits suit. Under the UCC, the **perfect tender** rule lets a buyer
reject goods that fail in any respect to conform, subject to the seller's right to **cure**
within the contract time and the special rules for installment contracts (rejection only
for substantial impairment).

**Here.** Here, [party]'s performance [substantially conformed / failed to conform], and
[cure was/was not available].

**Thus.** Thus, the breach was [minor/material], and the other party [may/may not] withhold
performance.

### Anticipatory Repudiation
🟡

**Rule.** An unequivocal statement or act by a party that it will not perform before
performance is due is an anticipatory repudiation, allowing the non-repudiating party to
sue immediately, suspend its own performance, or await performance. A party with
reasonable grounds for insecurity may demand adequate assurance and suspend performance
until it is received; failure to provide assurance within a reasonable time (UCC: 30 days)
is itself a repudiation. Repudiation may be retracted before the other party relies.

**Here.** Here, [party] unequivocally stated it would not perform.

**Thus.** Thus, the non-repudiating party may sue at once.

### Excuse — Impossibility, Impracticability, Frustration
🟡

**Rule.** Performance is discharged by **impossibility** (objectively impossible — e.g.,
death of a personal-service party, destruction of the specific subject matter),
**impracticability** (an unforeseen event makes performance excessively and unreasonably
burdensome, the non-occurrence of which was a basic assumption), or **frustration of
purpose** (an unforeseen event destroys the value of the performance to a party, known to
both as the foundation of the contract).

**Here.** Here, [the unforeseen event] [made performance impossible / destroyed the
contract's purpose].

**Thus.** Thus, the duty is discharged.

### Third-Party Beneficiaries
🟡

**Rule.** A third party may enforce a contract only if it is an **intended** beneficiary —
the parties intended to benefit it, often shown by naming or designating the party and
either a creditor or donee relationship. An **incidental** beneficiary has no rights.
Intended-beneficiary rights vest when the beneficiary learns of and relies on, assents to,
or sues on the contract, after which the original parties cannot modify without consent.

**Here.** Here, the contract was made for [third party]'s direct benefit.

**Thus.** Thus, [third party] may enforce it as an intended beneficiary.

### Assignment & Delegation
🟡

**Rule.** A party may **assign** contract rights unless the assignment would materially
change the obligor's duty/risk, is barred by the contract, or is prohibited by law; an
assignment for value extinguishes the assignor's rights. A party may **delegate** duties
unless the duty is personal (special skill/trust) or delegation is barred; the delegating
party remains liable unless there is a novation.

**Here.** Here, [party] [assigned its right to payment / delegated performance].

**Thus.** Thus, the [assignment/delegation] is [effective/ineffective].

### Remedies (overview — see Remedies bank)
🟢  ·  *cross-ref: Remedies*

**Rule.** The default remedy is **expectation** damages — the benefit of the bargain,
putting the non-breaching party where full performance would have. **Consequential**
damages are recoverable if foreseeable at formation (Hadley v. Baxendale); **incidental**
damages cover costs of dealing with the breach. Alternatives include **reliance** and
**restitution**. **Liquidated damages** are enforceable if damages were difficult to
estimate and the amount is a reasonable forecast (not a penalty). Equitable **specific
performance** is available for unique goods/land when damages are inadequate. Damages must
be reasonably certain, foreseeable, unavoidable (**mitigation**), and causally linked.

**Here.** Here, [non-breaching party]'s expectation interest is [computed], plus
[foreseeable consequential losses], reduced by [avoidable losses].

**Thus.** Thus, [party] recovers [measure].

## Part 3 — Crossovers & Exam Tactics

- **Common pairings:** Contracts ↔ **Remedies** (always finish with the damages analysis);
  ↔ **Professional Responsibility** in fee-agreement questions; UCC warranty ↔ **Torts**
  (products). Land-sale contracts ↔ **Real Property** (marketable title, equitable
  conversion).
- **Sleeper issues:** forgetting the law-source question; missing §2-207 when both sides
  are merchants; treating a unilateral mistake as automatically voidable; skipping
  mitigation; not separating material vs minor breach before reaching remedies.
- **CL-vs-UCC cheat list:** mirror image vs §2-207; pre-existing-duty vs free modification;
  substantial performance vs perfect tender + cure; firm offer; warranty regime; gap-fillers.
- **Call-of-the-question patterns:** "Is there an enforceable contract?" → law source →
  formation → defenses. "What are X's rights/remedies?" → breach analysis → damages chain.
