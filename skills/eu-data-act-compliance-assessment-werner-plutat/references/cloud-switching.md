# Data-Processing-Service Switching

Use this reference for Chapters VI and VII, Articles 23 to 32. First prove that the offering is a data processing service under Article 2(8) and is provided to a customer in the Union under Article 1(3)(f).

## 1. Scope and Service Classification

Record:

- source provider, customer, destination provider, and contracting entities;
- service model and same-service-type analysis, Article 2(9);
- exportable data, digital assets, and service-generated internal data;
- standard catalogue, custom build, or limited non-production service;
- destination service or on-premises target;
- infrastructure-only service under Article 30(1) or other service under Article 30(2) to (5).

Article 31 removes specified duties for certain predominantly custom-built, non-catalogue services and removes Chapter VI for limited non-production testing/evaluation services. The provider must disclose the non-applicable obligations before contract.

## 2. Article 23 Obstacles

Assess whether contractual, commercial, technical, organisational, or pre-commercial obstacles inhibit:

- termination after the maximum notice period and completed switching;
- a new same-type service contract;
- porting exportable data and digital assets, including from a free tier;
- functional equivalence where Article 24 and Article 30 apply;
- technically feasible unbundling of infrastructure services.

Functional equivalence is not a promise that a different provider's service will be identical. Apply the service-specific rules in Article 30.

## 3. Article 25 Contract Checklist

The written contract, available before signing in storable/reproducible form, must include:

- customer right to switch or port to on-premises;
- maximum transition of 30 calendar days after the notice period;
- reasonable assistance, continuity, known-risk information, and security during switching;
- exit-strategy support and relevant information;
- termination mechanics and notification;
- maximum notice period no longer than two months;
- exhaustive portable-data and digital-asset categories, at least all exportable data;
- narrowly described internal service data excluded for trade-secret risk without impeding switching;
- retrieval period of at least 30 calendar days after transition;
- erasure after retrieval or later agreed period once switching succeeds;
- any switching charges allowed by Article 29.

If 30 days is technically infeasible, Article 25(4) requires notice to the customer within 14 working days of the switching request, a reasoned explanation, and an alternative transition no longer than seven months. The customer also has the Article 25(5) right to extend the transition once for its own purposes.

The two-month period is a maximum notice period, not a minimum. Article 24 itself only defines the scope of technical responsibilities.

## 4. Information and Cooperation

Article 26 requires:

- procedures, methods, formats, restrictions, and known technical limits for switching and porting;
- a reference to an up-to-date provider-hosted online register of data structures, formats, standards, and open interoperability specifications for exportable data.

Article 27 requires all involved parties, including the destination provider, to cooperate in good faith for effective and timely switching and continuity.

## 5. Article 29 Charges

| Period | Rule |
|---|---|
| 11 January 2024 to 12 January 2027 | Reduced switching charges may be imposed, capped at directly linked costs |
| From 12 January 2027 | No switching charges |

Before contract, provide clear information on standard service fees, early-termination penalties, and applicable reduced switching charges. Where relevant, disclose highly complex, costly, or significantly disruptive switching. Make specified information easily accessible.

Do not use 1 January 2027 or 12 September 2027. Do not create separate statutory dates for new and existing contracts.

## 6. Article 30 Technical Duties

### Infrastructure-only services

Take all reasonable measures within the provider's power to help the customer achieve functional equivalence after switching to the same service type. Provide capabilities, information, documentation, support, and appropriate tools.

### Other data processing services

- provide open interfaces equally and free of charge to customers and relevant destination providers;
- implement applicable common specifications or harmonised standards within Article 30(3)'s timetable;
- update the Article 26 register;
- where no relevant published standard/specification exists, export all exportable data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format on request.

Article 30(6) does not require new technologies/services, protected digital-asset or trade-secret disclosure, or compromised security and integrity.

## 7. International Access Transparency and Safeguards

Article 28 requires current website disclosures on:

- jurisdiction governing deployed ICT infrastructure for individual services;
- general technical, organisational, and contractual measures against conflicting international governmental access or transfer.

The contract must list the disclosure website.

Article 32 applies to non-personal data held in the Union. Providers must take adequate technical, organisational, and legal measures against third-country governmental access or transfer that conflicts with Union or Member State law. Validate:

- applicable international agreement;
- Article 32(3) conditions where no agreement exists;
- consultation with the relevant national body where appropriate or required;
- minimum-data response;
- customer notice before compliance, subject to the law-enforcement exception.

Do not treat Article 32 as a GDPR transfer mechanism.

## 8. Evidence and Testing

Collect:

- pre-contract disclosures and executed clauses;
- export catalogue and exclusion rationale;
- interface documentation and online register;
- switching request timestamps;
- assistance, risk, continuity, and security records;
- charge calculation and direct-cost evidence;
- test exports and destination-import results;
- deletion and retrieval-period evidence;
- third-country request playbook and decision records.

Run at least one representative switch for each material service model. Measure completeness, format usability, time, business continuity, security, cost, and erasure. Label internal recovery objectives as implementation choices, not Data Act deadlines.

## 9. Findings Format

For each finding report:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Service and role | why Chapter VI applies |
| Provision | exact article and paragraph/point |
| Contract or system evidence | clause, interface, export, test result |
| Result | compliant, partial, non-compliant, or unresolved |
| Customer impact | switching obstacle or risk |
| Remediation | concrete contract, product, or process change |
| Validation | test or document proving closure |
