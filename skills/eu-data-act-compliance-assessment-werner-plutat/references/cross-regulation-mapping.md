## 1. Mapping Method

For every data flow, create separate rows for:

- Data Act right or duty and article;
- personal-data status and GDPR/ePrivacy legal basis;
- data subject, user, data holder, recipient, controller, processor, and joint-controller roles;
- security and product-law duties;
- intellectual-property and trade-secret constraints;
- sector-specific access, retention, localization, or secrecy rules;
- conflict or cumulative-duty result;
- evidence and decision owner.

Do not assume that the same party has the same role across instruments.

## 2. GDPR and ePrivacy

Article 1(5) Data Act preserves Union and national personal-data and privacy law and gives it priority in a conflict. Data Act Chapter II rights complement GDPR Articles 15 and 20 where the user is the data subject.

Key controls:

- classify personal and mixed data at field level where feasible;
- identify controller/processor roles for each disclosure and subsequent use;
- establish a GDPR Article 6 basis and, where relevant, Article 9 condition;
- address ePrivacy Article 5(3) where terminal-equipment access is involved;
- verify identity, authority, purpose limitation, minimisation, transparency, retention, security, and rights;
- complete DPIA and transfer analysis where independently required.

Articles 4(12) and 5(7) expressly require a valid legal basis where the user is not the data subject. Do not use “Data Act request” as a universal personal-data basis and do not assume consent is always required.

## 3. Digital Markets Act

Article 5(3) Data Act makes a designated DMA gatekeeper ineligible to receive data as a third party under Article 5. Article 6(2)(d) also bars a receiving third party from making that data available to a gatekeeper.

Record current gatekeeper designation from an official Commission decision. Do not infer gatekeeper status from company size or market reputation.

## 4. Trade Secrets and Intellectual Property

The Trade Secrets Directive supplies the definition incorporated by Article 2(18) Data Act. The Data Act adds specific disclosure procedures in Articles 4(6) to (9), 5(9) to (12), and 19(3).

Article 1(8) preserves Union and national intellectual-property law. Separate:

- whether data are within the Data Act duty;
- whether a database, code, content, documentation, or interface is IP-protected;
- what must be disclosed;
- what safeguards or limits apply.

A trade-secret or IP label is not, by itself, a blanket refusal ground.

## 5. Cyber Resilience Act, NIS2, and Sector Security

Map security requirements by product, service, entity, and incident. Relevant intersections can include:

- secure access interfaces and authentication;
- vulnerability handling and secure updates;
- incident reporting and evidence retention;
- supply-chain controls;
- Article 4(2) Data Act restrictions grounded in Union or national product-security requirements.

Do not describe CRA or NIS2 duties as universally applicable to every connected product or data holder. Verify the instrument's own scope, exclusions, transitional rules, and current application dates.

## 6. AI Act

If product or related-service data feed an AI system, separately assess:

- AI-system and GPAI classification;
- provider, deployer, importer, distributor, and product-manufacturer roles;
- training, validation, testing, monitoring, and log-data provenance;
- data governance, technical documentation, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity, and post-market duties where applicable;
- purpose limits imposed by the Data Act user agreement or third-party arrangement.

Use the current consolidated AI Act. Its application timetable can be amended. Record the exact version and do not copy a date from an older skill or project plan.

## 7. Data Governance Act

Where a data intermediary or data-altruism organization is involved, determine whether Regulation (EU) 2022/868 applies independently. The Data Act may create or structure an access right; the DGA may regulate the intermediary or reuse mechanism.

Do not conflate Article 10 Data Act dispute settlement with a DGA data-intermediation service.

## 8. Consumer, Competition, and Contract Law

Article 1(9) preserves EU consumer law. Article 13 Data Act covers specified unilaterally imposed B2B terms, not consumer terms. Also assess:

- consumer information and digital-content rules;
- unfair commercial practices and unfair consumer terms;
- Articles 101 and 102 TFEU and national competition law;
- national standard-term and civil-law controls;
- product liability and warranty effects.

## 9. Sector Data-Access Rules

Article 43 governs interaction with earlier and later sector rules. Record:

- exact sector act and provision;
- whether it entered into force before or after 11 January 2024;
- subject matter, data, holder, recipient, and purpose;
- whether Data Act Chapters II, III, and IV supplement or yield to the sector rule.

Examples may arise in automotive, energy, finance, health, agriculture, mobility, and public administration, but inclusion in a sector is not proof of a specific rule.

## 10. Integrated Evidence Matrix

| Data flow | Data Act | GDPR/ePrivacy | Security/product law | AI/sector law | Contract/IP | Conflict and action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identify system and direction | role, chapter, article | data and legal basis | scope and control | role and requirement | clause and right | controlling rule, owner, evidence |

For each external instrument, log:

- official EUR-Lex or authority URL;
- CELEX/ELI or official identifier;
- consolidation or publication date;
- provision relied on;
- access date;
- whether the proposition is binding law, guidance, or an open question.

## 11. Conflict Rules

When duties appear inconsistent:

1. verify that both instruments and provisions apply to the same facts;
2. read their express relationship clauses;
3. distinguish a true conflict from cumulative obligations;
4. check sector priority, lex specialis, and national-law implementation with counsel;
5. document the least-assumptive compliant route and unresolved legal issue.

Never resolve a conflict by silently dropping one regime.
