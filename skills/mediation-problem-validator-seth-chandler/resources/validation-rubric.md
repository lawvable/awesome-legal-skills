## 1. Source-of-Truth Reconstruction

Treat the general packet and both confidential packets as views of one case.

### Entity register

Record every person, company, agency, jurisdiction, role, party designation, contract, statute, project, product, asset, defined term, abbreviation, currency, and unit. Identify one controlling spelling and meaning.

### Chronology

Record contract formation, amendments, performance, payments, precipitating events, notices, termination, litigation, negotiations, deadlines, and the mediation date. Test whether every event and claimed state of knowledge is temporally possible.

### Financial ledger

For every material number record:

- value and currency;
- unit;
- whether it is cash, revenue, cost, profit, valuation, debt, damages, credit, or capacity;
- payer, recipient, owner, or controller;
- timing;
- visibility;
- calculation and related numbers.

Recompute rather than proofread. Test timing, approximation compatibility, and double counting.

### Authority and resource matrix

For each representative record:

- cash authority;
- rights that may be released;
- property, services, data, licenses, capacity, and opportunities controlled;
- protected interests;
- necessary approvals;
- timing, duration, volume, confidentiality, and performance limits;
- affected third-party rights.

### Knowledge map

Classify each proposition by epistemic status and visibility. Confirm that each party knows only what it could reasonably know.

## 2. Mandatory Consistency Gates

### Identity and roles

- Names, pronouns, titles, corporate relationships, party labels, and representatives agree.
- Requesting and responding roles do not switch.
- Authority matches the represented organization.

### Facts and chronology

- Objective facts have one meaning across packets.
- Different accounts are labeled as allegations, beliefs, inferences, or legal disputes.
- Dates, quantities, performance, notices, and deadlines reconcile.
- A confidential packet does not silently alter a public fact.

### Numbers

Verify:

- contract values from quantities and unit prices;
- payments, credits, deposits, and offsets;
- damages, mitigation, and inventory values;
- ownership percentages;
- financing requirements and deadlines;
- installments and totals;
- reservation conditions and authority;
- economics of every candidate package.

Distinguish:

- cash from accounting value;
- gross from net;
- revenue from profit;
- purchase price from settlement proceeds;
- retained value from new value;
- conditional authority from present authority.

### Logic and causation

- Urgency has a real deadline.
- Financing pressure explains why other proceeds do not solve it.
- suppliers, customers, projects, and technology operate on relevant timelines.
- ownership supports any proposed license or transfer.
- organizations act plausibly on the facts given.

### Information firewall

- Public packet reveals no private minimum, distress, hidden alternative, deadline, or intended trade.
- Neither confidential packet reveals the other's secret as fact.
- Beliefs do not become objective facts elsewhere.
- Packets do not contain mirrored package menus.
- No single packet supplies the complete solution.

### Severity

- **High:** contradiction, broken math, leak, impossible authority, missing premise, impossible corridor, or obvious answer. Blocks release.
- **Medium:** material confusion, imbalance, unrealistic conduct, weak implementation, or avoidable clarification.
- **Low:** nonmaterial wording, redundancy, emphasis, or typography.

## 3. Mediation Usefulness

### Productive legal uncertainty

Legal issues may create risk and explain positions. They should not be the only negotiable variable. Participants should be able to improve outcomes without first proving who would win adjudication.

### Sufficient definition

Participants must be able to determine:

- what happened;
- what each side publicly wants;
- why they disagree;
- what each risks without agreement;
- who can settle;
- what resources or rights could form an agreement.

Classify uncertainty:

- accidental ambiguity: repair;
- legal uncertainty: retain when both arguments have support;
- factual uncertainty: retain when it can be investigated or allocated;
- private information: retain when it rewards inquiry;
- missing premise: repair.

### Bargaining dimensions

Require at least three genuine dimensions from categories such as:

- money, price, valuation, or installments;
- timing, duration, renewal, or staging;
- volume, territory, exclusivity, customer access, or distribution;
- ownership, licensing, branding, data, or know-how;
- control, approval, reporting, audit, or governance;
- service, quality, investment, transition, or operations;
- apology, confidentiality, attribution, or public statements;
- escrow, earn-outs, pilots, options, triggers, or insurance;
- adjacent ventures, substitutes, or future business.

Every dimension must connect something a party values with something the other controls.

### BATNAs

For each alternative, test:

- operational availability;
- money, time, risk, management, reputation, and opportunity costs;
- whether estimates are net or gross;
- ability to address immediate needs;
- comparison with at least one sound agreement.

Do not use an artificially catastrophic or overwhelmingly dominant BATNA.

## 4. Settlement Corridor

Classify:

1. `impossible`
2. `brittle`
3. `narrow_sound`
4. `too_easy`

Require `narrow_sound`.

Construct two to four materially different author-only packages. For each, test:

- authority;
- cash timing and financeability;
- legal and operational availability;
- protected interests;
- third-party rights;
- two or more linked concessions;
- non-cash, contingent, relational, informational, or implementation value;
- monitoring, milestones, metrics, cure, and exit;
- performance under downside scenarios;
- superiority to both BATNAs;
- material difference from other packages.

Construct at least one tempting but unsound package and explain its failure.

## 5. Skill Opportunities

Evaluate each side separately. Opportunities need not be symmetrical, but each side needs all four.

### Firmness

Look for:

- legitimate protected interests or hard constraints;
- a principled reason to reject an attractive proposal;
- BATNA-supported resistance;
- ability to distinguish position from interest;
- a workable alternative to the rejected proposal.

### Cooperation

Look for:

- selective disclosure that unlocks value;
- acknowledgement without merits concession;
- agenda or sequencing choices;
- assistance, data, access, or transition support;
- reciprocal concessions;
- joint testing, monitoring, or implementation.

### Imagination

Look for:

- contingencies and triggers;
- options instead of unconditional promises;
- staged pilots;
- limited or nonexclusive licenses;
- substitute performance or credits;
- benchmark pricing;
- revised risk allocation;
- future business;
- implementation that makes an unsafe bargain sound.

Require participants to assemble packages rather than select matching menus.

### Clarification

For each side identify at least five consequential questions concerning priorities, authority, timing, operations, valuation, mitigation, assets, capacity, data, rights, standards, or BATNA fears.

At least two answers must materially affect package soundness. The other party's packet must contain responsive information that may properly be disclosed.

## 6. Role-Limited Audits

### Party reviewer

Using only the public packet and that party's confidential packet, identify:

1. five principal interests;
2. hard constraints and protected interests;
3. desired information;
4. realistic BATNA;
5. at least two plausible bargaining directions;
6. a tempting but unsound agreement;
7. clarification questions.

### Mediator reviewer

Using only the public packet, assess:

- coherence and balance;
- distributive, integrative, and contingent issues;
- whether the case is sufficiently defined;
- whether the solution is disclosed;
- what process could surface hidden value.

### Full-package reviewer

Using all packets and working tables, assess:

- candidate-package soundness;
- corridor classification;
- contradictions and leaks;
- impossible knowledge;
- party balance;
- the single most threatening defect.

## 7. Clarification Forecast

Simulate at least ten questions. Classify each:

- `answer_in_packet`: cite the location;
- `repair_before_release`: identify the defect;
- `facts_sufficient`: explain the deliberate uncertainty;
- `information_to_elicit`: identify the party holding it;
- `contingency_candidate`: explain how an agreement can manage it.

Never use `facts_sufficient` to hide a missing premise or calculation.

## 8. Quality Scoring

Score 0–3:

- 0: fail;
- 1: weak;
- 2: adequate;
- 3: strong.

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---:|
| Public dispute understandable and balanced | /3 |
| Facts, chronology, numbers, and logic reliable | /3 |
| Legal uncertainty supports rather than consumes mediation | /3 |
| Interests and dimensions create value beyond cash | /3 |
| Settlement corridor narrow but real | /3 |
| BATNAs and incentives credible | /3 |
| Party A has all four skill opportunities | /3 |
| Party B has all four skill opportunities | /3 |
| Confidentiality and asymmetry well designed | /3 |
| Candidate agreements implementable and durable | /3 |
| **Total** | **/30** |

Presumptive competition readiness requires:

- all gates passed;
- no score below 2;
- at least 24/30;
- `narrow_sound`;
- two to four sound packages;
- one tempting unsound package;
- both party audits finding two plausible directions;
- accidental clarification risks repaired.

Scoring never offsets a failed gate.

## 9. Exit Checklist

- [ ] Names, roles, dates, quantities, amounts, and material facts reconcile.
- [ ] Calculations and payment schedules recompute.
- [ ] Commercial logic and causation are plausible.
- [ ] Each party knows only what it could know.
- [ ] No confidential fact or intended package leaks.
- [ ] Public dispute is sufficiently defined.
- [ ] Legal uncertainty does not supply the only resolution path.
- [ ] At least three genuine bargaining dimensions exist.
- [ ] Both BATNAs are credible.
- [ ] Two to four sound packages exist.
- [ ] A simple midpoint or obvious trade is not automatically sound.
- [ ] Both parties can demonstrate all four mediation skills.
- [ ] At least two consequential facts must be elicited.
- [ ] Deliberate uncertainties can be investigated, priced, or managed.
- [ ] Accidental ambiguities and missing premises are repaired.
- [ ] Confidential packets supply ingredients rather than answer menus.
- [ ] Final two audit rounds contain no high or newly discovered high defect.
