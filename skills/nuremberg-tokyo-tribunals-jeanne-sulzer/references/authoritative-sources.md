# Authoritative sources — Nuremberg + Tokyo

Sources that may be cited, and how to access them. Read this whenever verifying a citation or deciding whether a source is authoritative.

## Tier 1 — primary, authoritative

### The official published records

**The Blue Series — IMT.**
*Trial of the Major War Criminals before the International Military Tribunal, Nuremberg, 14 November 1945 – 1 October 1946.* 42 volumes (Nuremberg: IMT, 1947-1949).
- Vol. I — Official documents (Charter, Indictment, Judgment)
- Vols. II-XXII — Proceedings transcripts
- Vol. XXIII — Chronological and subject index
- Vol. XXIV — Document and name index
- Vols. XXV-XLII — Documents and other material in evidence

**The Green Series — NMT.**
*Trials of War Criminals Before the Nuernberg Military Tribunals under Control Council Law No. 10, Nuernberg, October 1946 – April 1949.* 15 volumes (Washington, DC: US Government Printing Office, 1949-1953). Each NMT case is in one or two volumes; identify the volume before citing.

**The Pritchard-Zaide volumes — IMTFE.**
*The Tokyo War Crimes Trial*, ed. R. John Pritchard & Sonia M. Zaide. 22-27 volumes (New York: Garland Publishing, 1981-1987). The published transcripts of the IMTFE Proceedings in Open Session.
- Volumes 1-19 — Proceedings
- Volume 20 — Judgment and Annexes
- Volume 21 — Separate Opinions (Pal, Röling, Bernard, Webb, Jaranilla)
- Indexes and supplementary volumes in the remaining numbers

### Digital authoritative archives

**Avalon Project at Yale Law School.**
URL: `https://avalon.law.yale.edu/subject_menus/imt.asp`
The most accessible single point of entry for IMT materials. Hosts the London Agreement, IMT Charter, Indictment, selected proceedings transcripts, the Judgment, and Robert Jackson's opening and closing statements. Cite by URL and identify as "Avalon Project transcription"; cross-reference Blue Series for paginated citations where possible.

**Harvard Nuremberg Trials Project.**
URL: `https://nuremberg.law.harvard.edu/`
The largest dedicated digital collection of IMT and NMT materials. Approximately 1 million pages, 690 boxes. Project launched 2003; full digitisation completed 2017; metadata analysis ongoing. Covers IMT proceedings and the twelve NMT cases (with NMT 1 — Medical Case — and NMT 2 + NMT 4 fully searchable; expansion continuing). Free, open access.

**Stanford Taube Archive of the International Military Tribunal of Nuremberg.**
Part of the Stanford "Virtual Tribunals" project. Approximately 250,000 pages, 5,000+ trial records. Distinguishing feature: **multilingual transcripts in English, French, German, and Russian** — the only authoritative digital archive offering systematic multilingual access. Includes case files, trial briefs, evidentiary exhibits, opening and closing statements, final pleas, procedural rules, orders, judgments, dissenting opinions, sentences. Released in stages from 2021.

**University of Virginia IMTFE Digital Collection.**
URL: `https://imtfe.law.virginia.edu/`
The dedicated digital hub for the Tokyo Trial. Covers defendants, defence team, prosecution team, judges, and others associated with the trial. The principal academic gateway to IMTFE materials.

**ICC Legal Tools Database.**
URL: `https://www.legal-tools.org/`
Navigation path for IMTFE materials: "Other International(ised) Criminal Jurisdictions" > "International Military Tribunal for the Far East (IMTFE)"; or "United Nations War Crimes Commission" > "Member Governments…". Hosts the full English-language transcripts of IMTFE court proceedings and a near-complete set of English-language exhibits. Also hosts IMT Charter and Judgment and selected NMT materials. Not text-searchable for the IMTFE corpus.

**JACAR — Japan Center for Asian Historical Records (National Archives of Japan).**
Hosts a complete set of IMTFE scanned transcripts and exhibits in **both English and Japanese**. Not text-searchable; PDF-style scanned records. The authoritative Japanese-side mirror of the IMTFE corpus.

**UN Audiovisual Library of International Law.**
URL: `https://legal.un.org/avl/`
The authoritative source for UNGA Resolution 95(I) (Affirmation of the Nuremberg Principles, 11 December 1946), UNGA Resolution 177(II) (Formulation mandate, 21 November 1947), and the ILC 1950 Formulation of the Nuremberg Principles. Includes introductory notes by senior scholars (Cassese, Schabas) on the genesis and significance of each instrument.

### Tier 1 institutional repositories

**Library of Congress (USA).**
URL: `https://www.loc.gov/`
Digitisations of the Blue Series (LCCN 2011525338) and the Green Series, searchable via the LoC catalogue.

**National Archives (NARA, USA).**
URL: `https://www.archives.gov/research/captured-german-records/war-crimes-trials`
Record Groups 153 (Office of the Judge Advocate General), 238 (National Archives Collection of World War II War Crimes Records), 549 (US Army, Europe). The primary US institutional archive.

**Imperial War Museums (UK).**
- IMT records: Public Record Office references **FO 645/1-350**, plus the 42 volumes of the Blue Series.
- IMTFE records: Public Record Office references **FO 648/1-160**, plus the 27 published Pritchard-Zaide volumes.
The UK institutional archive.

**Hoover Institution Library & Archives (Stanford).**
URL: `https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt6b69q2rf/`
The International Military Tribunal for the Far East records collection. 16-volume Narrative Summary of the Records (1946-1948), plus original press releases, verdicts, and procedural materials. Citation form recommended by Hoover: "[Identification of item], International Military Tribunal for the Far East records, [Box no., Folder no. or title], Hoover Institution Library & Archives."

**Peace Palace Library, The Hague.**
URL: `https://peacepalacelibrary.nl/research-guide/tokyo-trial`
Holds the complete 22-volume transcripts of IMTFE Proceedings, plus Index and Guide volumes I-V. The principal European research guide to the Tokyo Trial.

### Tier 1 university collections (specialised)

**Creighton University — Delaney Tokyo Trial Papers.**
IMTFE materials donated by US prosecution staff. Useful for prosecution-side perspective.

**University of Hawaii at Manoa — War Crimes Documentation Initiative (WCDI).**
URL: `https://manoa.hawaii.edu/wcdi/`
Specialised in Asia-Pacific war crimes. The "Documenting Japanese War Crimes at Tokyo" project provides an interactive map of war crimes documented during IMTFE prosecution. Useful complement to legal-tools.org for IMTFE materials.

**University of Connecticut — Dodd Papers.**
Papers of Thomas J. Dodd, US Executive Trial Counsel at the IMT. Strong on the conduct of the prosecution.

**University of North Dakota Nuremberg Trials Digital Collection.**

**Robert H. Jackson Center.**
URL: `https://www.roberthjackson.org/`
For Robert Jackson's papers, speeches, and the texts of his opening and closing statements at Nuremberg. The authoritative source for Jackson-specific materials.

## Tier 2 — secondary (must be labelled when used)

### Institutional voices

- **International Nuremberg Principles Academy** (`https://www.nurembergacademy.org/`) — Based in Nuremberg. The contemporary institutional voice on the Nuremberg legacy. Authoritative for doctrine; Tier 2 only because it is not the issuing body of the original record.
- **United States Holocaust Memorial Museum**, Holocaust Encyclopedia (`https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/`) — Authoritative on historical context; Tier 2 for legal propositions.
- **National WWII Museum (New Orleans)**, Tokyo War Crimes Trial entry — historical context for IMTFE.
- **Famous Trials Project** (Doug Linder, University of Missouri-Kansas City) — accessible but Tier 2.

### Academic commentary — Nuremberg

- **Telford Taylor**, *The Anatomy of the Nuremberg Trials: A Personal Memoir* (Knopf 1992). Taylor served as Chief Counsel for War Crimes at the NMT.
- **Whitney R. Harris**, *Tyranny on Trial: The Trial of the Major German War Criminals at the End of World War II at Nuremberg, Germany, 1945-1946* (Southern Methodist UP 1954, revised 1995). Harris was a prosecutor.
- **Bradley F. Smith**, *Reaching Judgment at Nuremberg* (Basic Books 1977).
- **Robert E. Conot**, *Justice at Nuremberg* (Harper & Row 1983).
- **Kevin Jon Heller**, *The Nuremberg Military Tribunals and the Origins of International Criminal Law* (Oxford 2011). The standard scholarly treatment of the NMT.
- **William A. Schabas**, writings on the Nuremberg legacy across his work on international criminal law; *The Trial of the Kaiser* (Oxford 2018).
- **Hans Kelsen**, "Will the Judgment in the Nuremberg Trial Constitute a Precedent in International Law?", *International Law Quarterly* 1947 — the foundational contemporaneous critique.

### Academic commentary — Tokyo

- **Richard H. Minear**, *Victors' Justice: The Tokyo War Crimes Trial* (Princeton UP 1971). The classic critical study.
- **Yuma Totani**, *The Tokyo War Crimes Trial: The Pursuit of Justice in the Wake of World War II* (Harvard UP 2008). The standard contemporary scholarly account.
- **Neil Boister & Robert Cryer**, *The Tokyo International Military Tribunal: A Reappraisal* (Oxford UP 2008). The leading legal-doctrinal reappraisal.
- **B.V.A. Röling & Antonio Cassese**, *The Tokyo Trial and Beyond: Reflections of a Peacemonger* (Polity 1993). Röling was a dissenting judge at Tokyo.
- **Tim Maga**, *Judgment at Tokyo: The Japanese War Crimes Trials* (UP of Kentucky 2001).
- **Madoka Futamura**, *War Crimes Tribunals and Transitional Justice: The Tokyo Trial and the Nuremberg Legacy* (Routledge 2008).
- **Yuki Tanaka, Tim McCormack & Gerry Simpson (eds)**, *Beyond Victor's Justice? The Tokyo War Crimes Trial Revisited* (Brill 2011).

### Specialised databases

- **Shanghai Jiao Tong University Press — Database of the Tokyo Trials Literature.** Text-searchable English-language transcripts. **Subscription required.**

### Subsequent judicial pronouncements on the Nuremberg / Tokyo legacy

These are not Nuremberg / Tokyo sources but are how the legacy enters the modern record:
- *Attorney General of Israel v. Eichmann*, Supreme Court of Israel, 1962, 36 ILR 277 — extensively engages with the Nuremberg Principles as customary international law.
- *Prosecutor v. Tadić*, ICTY Case No. IT-94-1-AR72, AC, 2 October 1995 — relies on the Nuremberg framework.
- Rome Statute of the ICC — Article 7 (crimes against humanity), Article 25 (individual responsibility), Article 27 (no immunity), Article 33 (superior orders) all trace doctrinally to Nuremberg.
- ECCC engagement with Nuremberg as a source — see Case 002/01 and 002/02 judgments.

When a Tier 2 source is the only available source for a proposition, the output must say so. Example: *"As Telford Taylor recounts (Tier 2; not from the Blue Series)…"*

## Never authoritative

Wikipedia, Grokipedia, social media, AI-generated summaries, blogs (unless written by a serious scholar or practitioner in a formally attributable context — in which case treat as Tier 2 doctrine). If one of these is the only source, the proposition is unverified — narrow the claim or ask the user.

## A note on languages and translations

**Nuremberg.** The original trial proceeded in English, French, Russian, and German with simultaneous interpretation. The Blue Series was published in English, French, Russian, and German editions. The **Stanford Taube Archive** is the most accessible multilingual digital corpus. The **English edition** is typically treated as authoritative in Anglophone scholarship; the **French edition** in Francophone scholarship.

**Tokyo.** The original trial proceeded in English and Japanese with simultaneous interpretation. The Pritchard-Zaide volumes are in English. The **JACAR** archive holds both English and Japanese transcripts. The Tokyo Charter and Judgment exist authoritatively in English; the Japanese versions are subsequent translations.

When citing a non-English edition, identify it. Where the original speaker spoke a specific language, identify it. The English text is typically treated as authoritative for the IMT and IMTFE in Anglophone scholarship, but where the original speaker spoke German, French, or Japanese, the original language may matter for interpretation.
