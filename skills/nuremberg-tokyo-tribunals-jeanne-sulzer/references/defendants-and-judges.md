### Critical historical point — the non-indictment of Emperor Hirohito

**Emperor Hirohito was not indicted** at the IMTFE. This was a deliberate decision by SCAP / MacArthur and the US political authorities. Several IMTFE judges (notably Sir William Webb, the President) believed the Emperor should have been prosecuted. The non-indictment is foundational to the post-colonial critique of the IMTFE (see *jurisprudence-map.md* §11).

### Class A / B / C — the IMTFE counts

The Tokyo Charter Article 5 establishes three classes:
- **Class A — Crimes against Peace** (the dominant basis of conviction at Tokyo)
- **Class B — Conventional War Crimes**
- **Class C — Crimes against Humanity** (comparatively under-used at Tokyo)

The defendants were charged across **55 counts** in the Indictment; the Tribunal narrowed the counts substantially in the course of the trial.

### The five separate opinions at Tokyo

Issued outside the courtroom (the majority Judgment was read in court 4-12 November 1948; the separate opinions were released to the press but not read aloud in court):

1. **Pal dissent** — acquittal of all defendants; the most famous
2. **Röling dissent** — partial dissent
3. **Bernard dissent** — dissent on procedural / fairness grounds
4. **Webb separate concurrence** — agreeing with conviction but expressing concerns about the proceedings and the non-indictment of the Emperor
5. **Jaranilla separate concurrence** — arguing for more severe sentences

All five are in **Pritchard-Zaide Vol. 21**. Citation form: *Dissentient Judgment of Justice [Name]* (IMTFE, 1948), in *The Tokyo War Crimes Trial*, Vol. 21, pp. [n].

## A discipline note

Spellings, diacritics, and positions matter. Use these tables to verify before drafting. Confusing Doihara (general) with Doihara as anything else, or putting an n where there should not be one, or omitting a macron in a Japanese name (Tōjō, Tōgō, Mutō), all signal imprecision. The Pritchard-Zaide volumes and the JACAR archive use slightly different romanisation conventions — be consistent with the source you cite.
