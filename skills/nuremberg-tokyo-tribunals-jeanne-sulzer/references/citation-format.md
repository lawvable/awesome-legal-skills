# Citation format — Nuremberg + Tokyo

How to cite, with worked examples. Read this before drafting any output containing a Nuremberg or Tokyo citation.

## The five citation modes

### 1. Charter / Statute provisions

**IMT Charter:**
> *Charter of the International Military Tribunal*, art. [n], annexed to the *Agreement for the Prosecution and Punishment of the Major War Criminals of the European Axis*, 82 U.N.T.S. 280 (London, 8 August 1945).

Short forms: *IMT Charter*, art. [n].

**Tokyo Charter:**
> *Charter of the International Military Tribunal for the Far East*, art. [n], issued by Special Proclamation of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, Tokyo, 19 January 1946.

Short forms: *Tokyo Charter*, art. [n]; *IMTFE Charter*, art. [n].

**Control Council Law No. 10:**
> *Control Council Law No. 10 (Punishment of Persons Guilty of War Crimes, Crimes against Peace and against Humanity)*, Allied Control Council, 20 December 1945, art. [n].

### 2. The IMT Judgment

**Blue Series form (preferred for paginated pinpoint):**
> *Judgment of the International Military Tribunal*, in *Trial of the Major War Criminals before the International Military Tribunal*, Vol. I (Official Documents) and Vol. XXII (Proceedings), pp. [n] (Nuremberg 1947).

**Oxford ICL form:**
> *France et al. v. Göring (Hermann) et al.*, Judgment and Sentence, [1946] 22 IMT 203; (1946) 41 AJIL 172; (1946) 13 ILR 203; ICL 243 (IMTN 1946), International Military Tribunal, 1 October 1946.

**Avalon Project transcription form:**
> "Judgment of the International Military Tribunal" (Avalon Project, Yale Law School), `https://avalon.law.yale.edu/imt/judgen.asp`, accessed [date].

### 3. NMT cases (the twelve subsequent trials)

**Form:**
> *United States v. [Defendant] et al.*, [case nickname if applicable] (US Military Tribunal [number] at Nuremberg, [date of judgment]), in *Trials of War Criminals Before the Nuernberg Military Tribunals under Control Council Law No. 10*, Vol. [n], pp. [n] (Washington, DC: US GPO, 1949-1953).

**Worked examples:**
- *United States v. Altstötter et al.* (the **Justice Case**) (US Military Tribunal III, Nuremberg, 3-4 December 1947), in *Trials of War Criminals*, Vol. III.
- *United States v. Ohlendorf et al.* (the **Einsatzgruppen Case**) (US Military Tribunal II-A, Nuremberg, 8-9 April 1948), in *Trials of War Criminals*, Vol. IV.
- *United States v. Brandt et al.* (the **Doctors' Trial**) (US Military Tribunal I, Nuremberg, 19-20 August 1947), in *Trials of War Criminals*, Vols. I-II.

### 4. The IMTFE Judgment

**Pritchard-Zaide form (preferred):**
> *Judgment of the International Military Tribunal for the Far East*, 4-12 November 1948, in *The Tokyo War Crimes Trial*, ed. R. John Pritchard & Sonia M. Zaide, Vol. 20 (Judgment and Annexes), pp. [n] (New York: Garland Publishing 1981).

**Short / case-name form (sometimes used in scholarship):**
> *International Military Tribunal for the Far East v. Araki et al.*, Judgment, IMTFE, 4-12 November 1948.

(The defendants are sometimes referred to collectively as "*Araki et al.*" — Sadao Araki was the first-named defendant. Other scholars use "*Tojo et al.*" since Tojo is the best-known defendant. Both are encountered; the Pritchard-Zaide canonical form is recommended.)

**Separate opinions:**
> *Dissentient Judgment of Justice Radhabinod Pal* (IMTFE, 1948), in *The Tokyo War Crimes Trial*, Vol. 21 (Separate Opinions), pp. [n].

Other separate opinions (Röling, Bernard, Webb, Jaranilla) follow the same form.

**Critical citation discipline for the IMTFE:** the Pal dissent is famously cited in post-colonial international legal scholarship. Always distinguish:
- "The IMTFE held …" (= the majority Judgment, Pritchard-Zaide Vol. 20)
- "Justice Pal dissented …" (= the dissenting opinion, Pritchard-Zaide Vol. 21)
- "At Tokyo, it was argued …" (= argument, not holding)

Never present Pal's dissenting view as "the Tribunal said".

### 5. The Nuremberg Principles (ILC 1950)

**Form:**
> *Principles of International Law recognized in the Charter of the Nürnberg Tribunal and in the Judgment of the Tribunal, with Commentaries*, International Law Commission, 1950, Principle [I-VII], in *Yearbook of the International Law Commission*, 1950, Vol. II, paras 95-127.

### 6. UNGA Resolutions

- UNGA Resolution 95(I), UN Doc. A/64, 11 December 1946.
- UNGA Resolution 177(II), 21 November 1947.

**Diacritics and Japanese names.** Japanese names follow the convention "Given name + family name" in IMTFE records (English-language record); Japanese-language records use the conventional Japanese order "Family name + given name" (e.g. Tōjō Hideki / 東條 英機). The Pritchard-Zaide volumes use the English ordering. Cite consistently with the source used.

## When the citation cannot be completed

If, after verification, you cannot identify the volume, page, count, or judicial opinion, the citation is not yet a citation. Narrow the claim, or tell the user what is missing and ask them to provide the source.
