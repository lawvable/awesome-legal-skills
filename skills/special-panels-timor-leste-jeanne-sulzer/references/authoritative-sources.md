# Authoritative sources — Special Panels Timor-Leste

## Tier 1 — primary, authoritative (distributed)

### JSMP — Judicial System Monitoring Programme (jsmp.tl)

The principal **Timor-Leste judicial monitoring NGO**. Founded in 2001 as JSMP under the umbrella of the SPSC monitoring. Archive includes:
- Trial reports from SPSC proceedings (most cases)
- Original SCU indictments (many)
- Verdict summaries
- Procedural analysis

**Tier 1 in practice** for procedural records given JSMP's official observer status.

### UC Berkeley Human Rights Center

**The most comprehensive single digital repository of SPSC records.** Hosts:
- SPSC judgments (substantial collection, though not exhaustive)
- SCU indictments
- Procedural decisions
- Some trial transcripts

The Berkeley collection was assembled in part as an academic preservation project following the SPSC's closure in 2006.

### UN UNTAET archives

UN Mission archives, including:
- All UNTAET Regulations (1999/1, 2000/11, 2000/15, 2000/16, 2000/30, etc.)
- Memorandum of Understanding UNTAET-Indonesia (5-6 April 2000)
- UN Secretary-General reports on the Serious Crimes process (S/2005/310 of 12 May 2005 is a key reference)
- UNMIT reports for the 2008-2012 SCIT phase

Accessible through UN Peacekeeping archives and the UN Digital Library.

### legal-tools.org — ICC Legal Tools Database

Mirrors:
- All UNTAET Regulations
- Many principal SPSC judgments
- SCU indictments (selected)

Navigate via "Other International(ised) Criminal Jurisdictions" > "Special Panels for Serious Crimes in East Timor".

### East Timor and Indonesia Action Network (ETAN) — etan.org

Comprehensive non-governmental archive of documents on the East Timor justice process, maintained since the late 1990s. Useful for advocacy-related context and procedural milestones.

## Tier 2 — secondary (must be labelled)

### Academic literature

- **Caitlin Reiger** — *The Serious Crimes Process in Timor-Leste: In Retrospect* (ICTJ, 2006) — the principal retrospective analytical treatment; comprehensive institutional assessment
- **Suzannah Linton** — multiple articles in *Leiden Journal of International Law*, *Journal of International Criminal Justice*, on SPSC jurisprudence; particularly *Cambodia, East Timor and Sierra Leone: Experiments in International Justice* (Criminal Law Forum, 2001)
- **Sylvia de Bertodano** — *East Timor: Trials and Tribulations* in Cesare Romano, André Nollkaemper and Jann Kleffner (eds.), *Internationalized Criminal Courts and Tribunals: Sierra Leone, East Timor, Kosovo and Cambodia* (Oxford University Press 2004)
- **Hansjörg Strohmeyer** — *Collapse and Reconstruction of a Judicial System: The United Nations Missions in Kosovo and East Timor* (American Journal of International Law, 2001) — UNTAET legal advisor's institutional account
- **David Cohen** — *Indifference and Accountability: The United Nations and the Politics of International Justice in East Timor* (East-West Center 2006) — critical analytical study
- **David Cohen** — *Intended to Fail: The Trials Before the Ad Hoc Human Rights Court in Jakarta* (International Center for Transitional Justice 2003) — on the Indonesian Ad Hoc Tribunal (the parallel, separate Jakarta proceedings)
- **Megan Hirst and Howard Varney** — *Justice Abandoned? An Assessment of the Serious Crimes Process in East Timor* (ICTJ, 2005)

### Hybrid Justice project — hybridjustice.com

Comparative analytical resource on the SPSC alongside other hybrid mechanisms. Useful for institutional comparison with ECCC, SCSL, KSC.

### Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International

Periodic reports on the Serious Crimes process, particularly:
- HRW, *Justice Denied for East Timor* (2002)
- HRW, *Indonesia/Timor-Leste: Securing Accountability* (multiple editions)
- Amnesty, *Indonesia: Justice for Timor-Leste — The Way Forward* (multiple editions)

### CAVR Final Report — "Chega!" (October 2005)

**Comissão de Acolhimento, Verdade e Reconciliação de Timor-Leste** — the truth and reconciliation commission of Timor-Leste. Its Final Report (popularly known as "Chega!") of October 2005 is the **foundational historical documentation** of the violence (1974-1999), the institutional response, and the broader transitional justice landscape. Available at cavr-timorleste.org.

**Note:** the CAVR is **distinct from but procedurally linked to** the SPSC. CAVR ran Community Reconciliation Procedures (CRPs) for lesser crimes that did not warrant prosecution; CRPs were procedurally articulated with the SPSC through Section 23 of UNTAET Regulation 2001/10 (which established the CAVR).

### Journalism

- **East Timor Law Journal** — academic and practitioner journal
- **The Jakarta Post**, **Tempo magazine** — Indonesian coverage
- **The Guardian**, **The New York Times** — Anglo coverage of major developments

## Never authoritative

Wikipedia, Grokipedia, social media, AI summaries.

## A note on languages

SPSC proceedings were conducted in **multiple languages**:
- **Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia)** — the language of pre-1999 administration; many witnesses testified in Indonesian
- **Tetum** — the Timorese vernacular; many witnesses testified in Tetum
- **Portuguese** — the official language of Timor-Leste (post-independence)
- **English** — operational language of UNTAET and international staff

**Translation challenges were systemic.** Many SPSC judgments are available in English and Indonesian; some in Portuguese; comprehensive Tetum versions are less commonly available. For substantive quotations, identify the language of the underlying text and the translation chain.

## A note on archival fragility

Unlike institutions with continuing official websites (ICC, IRMCT, ECCC), the SPSC has **no living institutional successor maintaining a unified archive**. Records are preserved through:
- JSMP, UC Berkeley HRC, ETAN (NGO archives)
- UN UNTAET legacy archives (Mission documents)
- legal-tools.org (selective mirror)
- Personal archives of former international staff (limited public access)

This **distributed and partly fragile** archival state is a verification challenge. Anticipate longer verification times than for active institutions; document Tier 1 sources carefully.
