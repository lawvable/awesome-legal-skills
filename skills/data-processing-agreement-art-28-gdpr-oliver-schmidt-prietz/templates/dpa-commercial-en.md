# Data Processing Agreement Template — English

This template implements all Art. 28(3) GDPR requirements at a balanced T2 baseline. Where a clause has a meaningful negotiating range, alternatives are marked `[ALT 1: controller-favorable]` and `[ALT 2: processor-favorable]` — choose one and delete the others.

Placeholders: `{{CONTROLLER}}`, `{{PROCESSOR}}`, `{{MAIN_AGREEMENT}}`, `{{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}`, `{{GOVERNING_LAW}}`, `{{VENUE}}`, etc.

## Recitals

A. The Parties have entered into `{{MAIN_AGREEMENT_TITLE}}` dated `{{MAIN_AGREEMENT_DATE}}` (the "**Main Agreement**") under which the Processor provides certain services to the Controller (the "**Services**").

B. In the course of providing the Services, the Processor will process Personal Data on behalf of the Controller within the meaning of Article 4(8) GDPR.

C. The Parties enter into this DPA pursuant to Article 28(3) GDPR to set out the rights and obligations of the Parties in relation to the processing of Personal Data.

## 1. Definitions

1.1 In this DPA:

- "**Applicable Data Protection Law**" means (a) the GDPR; (b) the German Federal Data Protection Act (BDSG); and (c) any other data protection law applicable to the processing under this DPA.
- "**GDPR**" means Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016.
- "**Personal Data**" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, processed by the Processor on behalf of the Controller under this DPA.
- "**Personal Data Breach**" has the meaning given in Article 4(12) GDPR.
- "**Sub-processor**" means any third party engaged by the Processor to process Personal Data on behalf of the Controller.
- "**SCCs**" means the Standard Contractual Clauses approved by Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914.
- "**TOMs**" means technical and organisational measures within the meaning of Articles 24, 28 and 32 GDPR.
- Other capitalised terms used but not defined in this DPA have the meaning given in the GDPR.

## 2. Subject matter, duration, nature, purpose, type of personal data, categories of data subjects

2.1 The subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the type of Personal Data and the categories of data subjects are set out in **Annex 1** (Description of Processing).

2.2 This DPA shall remain in force for the duration of the Services. Termination of the Main Agreement shall automatically terminate this DPA, subject to obligations expressed to survive termination.

## 3. Roles and instructions

3.1 The Controller is the controller and the Processor is the processor in respect of the Personal Data processed under this DPA.

3.2 The Processor shall process the Personal Data only on the documented instructions of the Controller, including with regard to transfers of Personal Data to a third country or an international organisation, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law to which the Processor is subject. In such a case, the Processor shall inform the Controller of that legal requirement before the relevant processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of public interest.

3.3 The instructions of the Controller are set out in this DPA, in the Main Agreement, and in any further written instructions issued by the Controller from time to time. The Processor shall acknowledge receipt of any further instructions promptly.

3.4 If the Processor considers that an instruction infringes Applicable Data Protection Law, the Processor shall promptly inform the Controller. Pending resolution, the Processor may suspend execution of the relevant instruction.

3.5 The Processor shall not process the Personal Data for any purpose other than as instructed by the Controller. Without limiting the foregoing:

`[ALT 1: controller-favorable]` The Processor shall not use, disclose, retain, sell, share, or otherwise process the Personal Data for the Processor's own purposes, including for service improvement, product development, analytics, or model training, except as expressly instructed by the Controller in writing.

`[ALT 2: processor-favorable]` The Processor may use Personal Data in aggregated and de-identified form (such that the data no longer relates to an identified or identifiable natural person) solely for the purposes of (i) providing and securing the Services, (ii) detecting and preventing fraud or abuse, and (iii) generating aggregate statistical insights that do not reveal Controller or data subject identities. The Processor acts as an independent controller for any such use.

## 4. Confidentiality

4.1 The Processor shall ensure that all persons authorised to process the Personal Data have committed themselves to confidentiality (e.g. through written confidentiality undertakings) or are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality.

4.2 The confidentiality obligation shall survive termination of the relevant person's engagement with the Processor and termination of this DPA.

## 5. Security of processing (Art. 32 GDPR)

5.1 The Processor shall implement and maintain the technical and organisational measures set out in **Annex 2** (TOMs) to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including as appropriate the measures referred to in Article 32(1)(a) to (d) GDPR.

5.2 The Processor may update the TOMs from time to time provided that the level of security is not materially diminished. The Processor shall notify the Controller of any material change to the TOMs in advance. Material reductions in the level of security require the Controller's prior written approval, which shall not be unreasonably withheld.

5.3 The Processor shall ensure that any natural person acting under its authority who has access to Personal Data does not process them except on instructions from the Controller, unless required by Union or Member State law.

## 6. Sub-processors

6.1 The Controller hereby grants general authorisation for the engagement of the Sub-processors listed in **Annex 3** (Sub-processors) at the date of this DPA.

6.2 The Processor shall notify the Controller in writing at least thirty (30) days before adding or replacing any Sub-processor (the "**Sub-processor Notification**"). The Sub-processor Notification shall identify the Sub-processor by legal name and registered seat, the location(s) of processing, the categories of Personal Data processed, and the processing activity.

6.3 The Controller may object to the engagement of a new Sub-processor on reasonable data-protection-related grounds within ten (10) Business Days of receipt of the Sub-processor Notification.

6.4 If the Controller objects, the Parties shall discuss in good faith for a period of fifteen (15) Business Days. If the objection cannot be resolved, the Controller may terminate this DPA and the corresponding portion of the Main Agreement on thirty (30) days' written notice without penalty, and without prejudice to the Controller's right to a refund of fees paid for Services not yet rendered.

6.5 The Processor shall conclude a written agreement with each Sub-processor imposing data protection obligations on the Sub-processor that are no less stringent than those imposed on the Processor under this DPA, in particular providing sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate TOMs.

6.6 The Processor shall remain fully liable to the Controller for the acts and omissions of any Sub-processor as if they were its own.

## 7. Data subject rights

7.1 Where a data subject contacts the Processor directly with a request to exercise rights under Chapter III GDPR, the Processor shall forward the request to the Controller without undue delay and in any event within five (5) Business Days, and shall not respond substantively unless instructed by the Controller.

7.2 Taking into account the nature of the processing, the Processor shall assist the Controller by appropriate technical and organisational measures, insofar as this is possible, in fulfilling the Controller's obligations to respond to data subject requests, including by providing access to Personal Data, deletion or rectification, restriction, portability, and objection.

7.3 The Processor shall provide such assistance promptly and in any event within ten (10) Business Days of the Controller's request, or such shorter period as may be reasonably required for the Controller to comply with its statutory deadlines.

7.4 `[ALT 1: controller-favorable]` Routine assistance under this Section 7 shall be included in the Service fees and shall not be separately chargeable. `[ALT 2: processor-favorable]` Routine assistance shall be included in the Service fees. Non-routine assistance (such as request volumes materially exceeding ordinary patterns or specialised data extractions) may be charged at the Processor's reasonable rates with the Controller's prior agreement.

## 8. Other compliance assistance (Art. 32–36 GDPR)

8.1 The Processor shall assist the Controller in ensuring compliance with the Controller's obligations under Articles 32 to 36 GDPR, taking into account the nature of processing and the information available to the Processor.

8.2 The Processor shall notify the Controller of any Personal Data Breach without undue delay and in any event within forty-eight (48) hours of becoming aware of it. The notification shall include, to the extent reasonably available at the time:

(a) the nature of the Personal Data Breach, including the categories and approximate number of data subjects and Personal Data records concerned;
(b) the name and contact details of the Processor's data protection officer or other point of contact;
(c) the likely consequences of the Personal Data Breach;
(d) the measures taken or proposed to address the Personal Data Breach and to mitigate its possible adverse effects.

8.3 The Processor shall provide further information as it becomes available and shall cooperate with the Controller's reasonable requests in connection with the Controller's obligations under Articles 33 and 34 GDPR.

8.4 On request, the Processor shall provide the Controller with information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 32 GDPR and to support the Controller's data protection impact assessments under Article 35 GDPR and prior consultations with supervisory authorities under Article 36 GDPR.

## 9. Deletion or return of personal data

9.1 At the choice of the Controller, exercised in writing within thirty (30) days of termination of the Services, the Processor shall:

(a) return all Personal Data to the Controller in a commonly used, machine-readable format; or
(b) delete all Personal Data in its possession or control,

in each case including all copies in production, archive, and backup systems, and shall provide written certification of compliance to the Controller.

9.2 Personal Data in backup systems shall be deleted upon expiry of the standard backup rotation period (which shall not exceed ninety (90) days from termination); restored backups shall be re-deleted promptly. Until expiry, backups shall be access-restricted and not used for any other purpose.

9.3 The Processor may retain Personal Data only to the extent and for such period as required by Union or Member State law to which the Processor is subject. The Processor shall identify the law and the retention period to the Controller in advance, ensure such Personal Data is access-restricted and used only for the legally required purpose, and delete the Personal Data on expiry of the retention period.

## 10. Audits and inspections

10.1 The Processor shall make available to the Controller all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations laid down in this DPA and in Article 28 GDPR.

10.2 The Processor shall allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, conducted by the Controller or another auditor mandated by the Controller.

10.3 The Controller may exercise the audit right under Section 10.2:

(a) once per calendar year on thirty (30) days' written notice; and
(b) on shorter notice (or no notice in case of urgency) following a Personal Data Breach, on request of a competent supervisory authority, or where there is demonstrated material concern about the Processor's compliance with this DPA.

10.4 Audits shall be conducted in a manner that does not unreasonably interfere with the Processor's business operations and shall respect the confidential information of the Processor and its other customers.

10.5 The Processor's current third-party certifications and audit reports (such as ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2 Type II, ISAE 3000) shall be provided on request and shall constitute supporting evidence of compliance, but shall not replace the Controller's audit right under Section 10.2.

10.6 `[ALT 1: controller-favorable]` Each Party shall bear its own costs in connection with audits, except that the Processor shall bear the Controller's reasonable costs (including reasonable third-party auditor fees) where an audit reveals material non-compliance with this DPA or Applicable Data Protection Law. `[ALT 2: processor-favorable]` The Controller shall bear the costs of audits it initiates, including third-party auditor fees, except that the Processor shall bear such costs where an audit reveals material non-compliance with this DPA.

10.7 Findings of any audit may be disclosed by the Controller to its competent supervisory authority where required by law and shall otherwise be treated as confidential information of the Processor.

## 11. International transfers

11.1 The Processor shall not transfer Personal Data to a third country or an international organisation outside the EEA except (a) as expressly instructed by the Controller, and (b) in compliance with Chapter V GDPR.

11.2 Where the Processor transfers Personal Data outside the EEA, the transfer shall be made on the basis of:

(a) an adequacy decision under Article 45 GDPR;
(b) appropriate safeguards under Article 46 GDPR (in particular the SCCs); or
(c) a derogation under Article 49 GDPR (only in exceptional circumstances and with the Controller's prior written consent).

11.3 Where the SCCs apply, the Parties hereby enter into the SCCs as set out in **Annex 4** (International Transfers). The Processor warrants that it has conducted, and shall maintain, a transfer impact assessment in accordance with Clause 14 of the SCCs.

11.4 Where the Processor relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework certification of an importer, and that certification ceases to be effective, the Processor shall promptly notify the Controller and the Parties shall execute the SCCs (with appropriate Annexes) as a fallback transfer mechanism within thirty (30) days. Pending execution, the Processor shall continue to apply at least equivalent safeguards.

## 12. Liability

12.1 The Parties' respective liability under this DPA shall be subject to the limitation and exclusion-of-liability provisions of the Main Agreement, except as provided in this Section 12.

12.2 `[ALT 1: controller-favorable]` Notwithstanding any contrary provision in the Main Agreement, the Processor's aggregate liability for damages arising out of or in connection with breaches of this DPA, breaches of Applicable Data Protection Law, or Personal Data Breaches shall be capped at an amount equal to two (2) times the fees paid or payable by the Controller under the Main Agreement in the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to liability. This cap shall be in addition to (not in lieu of) the general liability cap under the Main Agreement.

`[ALT 2: processor-favorable]` The Processor's aggregate liability for damages arising out of or in connection with breaches of this DPA shall be subject to the general liability cap under the Main Agreement.

12.3 The cap in Section 12.2 shall not apply to liability for: (a) gross negligence or wilful misconduct; (b) breach of confidentiality; (c) infringement of third-party intellectual property rights; or (d) any liability that cannot be limited or excluded under applicable law.

12.4 The Processor shall indemnify the Controller against third-party claims, regulatory fines, and data subject compensation claims (and the Controller's reasonable costs of defence) to the extent caused by the Processor's breach of this DPA or of Applicable Data Protection Law.

## 13. Term and termination

13.1 This DPA shall enter into force on the Effective Date and shall remain in force for the duration of the Services.

13.2 Either Party may terminate this DPA on thirty (30) days' written notice in case of material breach of this DPA by the other Party that has not been cured within the notice period. Termination of this DPA on this basis shall entitle the terminating Party to terminate the corresponding portion of the Main Agreement on the same basis without penalty.

13.3 The following provisions shall survive termination of this DPA: Sections 4 (Confidentiality), 9 (Deletion or return), 10.7 (Confidentiality of audit findings), 11.3 (TIA maintenance, where applicable), 12 (Liability), and 13.3 (Survival), and any provision that by its nature is intended to survive.

## 14. Order of precedence

14.1 In case of conflict between this DPA and the Main Agreement on data protection matters, this DPA shall prevail.

14.2 In case of conflict between this DPA and the SCCs (where executed), the SCCs shall prevail.

## 15. Miscellaneous

15.1 **Notices.** Notices under this DPA shall be given in writing to the addresses set out at the head of this DPA, or to such other address as a Party may notify to the other in writing. Notices shall be effective on receipt.

15.2 **Amendments.** Amendments to this DPA shall be in writing and signed by both Parties.

15.3 **Severability.** If any provision of this DPA is held invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force.

15.4 **Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this DPA without the other's prior written consent, except in connection with a merger, acquisition, or sale of substantially all assets, with notice to the other Party.

15.5 **Governing law and jurisdiction.** This DPA shall be governed by the law of `{{GOVERNING_LAW}}`. The courts of `{{VENUE}}` shall have exclusive jurisdiction.

# Annex 2 — Technical and Organisational Measures (Art. 32 GDPR)

The Processor implements the following TOMs to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk:

## 1. Pseudonymisation and encryption (Art. 32(1)(a) GDPR)

- **Encryption in transit**: All Personal Data in transit between Controller and Processor systems and within Processor's infrastructure is encrypted using TLS 1.2 or higher.
- **Encryption at rest**: Personal Data at rest is encrypted using AES-256 or equivalent. Encryption keys are managed in a key management service with access controls and key rotation.
- **Pseudonymisation**: Where compatible with the purpose of processing, Personal Data is pseudonymised; pseudonymisation key is segregated from the pseudonymised data.

## 2. Confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)

### Confidentiality
- **Access control**: Role-based access control with least-privilege principle. Access to Personal Data is logged and reviewed.
- **Authentication**: Multi-factor authentication required for all access to systems processing Personal Data.
- **Network segmentation**: Production environments are segregated from development and test environments.
- **Personnel screening**: Background checks for personnel with access to Personal Data, where lawful.

### Integrity
- **Change management**: Documented change management process for all changes to systems processing Personal Data.
- **Vulnerability management**: Regular vulnerability scanning and patching of systems.
- **Code integrity**: Code review and signed deployments.

### Availability
- **Redundancy**: High-availability architecture with redundant components.
- **Monitoring**: 24/7 monitoring of system availability and security events.
- **Incident response**: Documented incident response plan with defined roles and escalation paths.

### Resilience
- **Disaster recovery**: Documented disaster recovery plan with defined RTO and RPO.
- **Business continuity**: Business continuity plan with annual testing.

## 3. Restoration of availability and access (Art. 32(1)(c) GDPR)

- **Backups**: Regular backups of Personal Data, encrypted and stored separately from production.
- **Backup testing**: Periodic restore testing.
- **Backup retention**: Backups retained for a period not exceeding `{{BACKUP_RETENTION}}` (typically 30–90 days).

## 4. Regular testing and evaluation (Art. 32(1)(d) GDPR)

- **Penetration testing**: Annual penetration testing by qualified independent third parties.
- **Security audits**: Regular internal security audits.
- **Certifications**: `{{e.g. ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2 Type II, BSI C5 — list and certification body}}`.
- **Annual TOMs review**: Annual review of TOMs effectiveness; adjustments as required.

## 5. Personnel and organisational measures

- **Confidentiality undertakings**: All personnel under written confidentiality obligations.
- **Training**: Mandatory data-protection and security training on hire and at least annually thereafter.
- **Data protection officer / contact**: `{{DPO_NAME_AND_CONTACT}}`.
- **Records of processing**: Maintained per Art. 30 GDPR.

## 6. Sub-processor oversight

- **Due diligence**: Documented sub-processor due diligence.
- **Contracts**: Written contracts with sub-processors imposing data protection obligations no less stringent than those in this DPA.
- **Periodic review**: Periodic re-assessment of sub-processor compliance.

## 7. Special-category data (only where applicable)

[The following enhanced measures apply where Special Categories of Personal Data (Art. 9 GDPR) or data relating to criminal convictions (Art. 10 GDPR) are processed:]

- Mandatory pseudonymisation where technically feasible.
- Enhanced access controls (need-to-know with documented justification).
- Segregated processing environments.
- Enhanced logging and monitoring with extended log retention.
- Sub-processor flow-down requires equivalent enhanced measures.
