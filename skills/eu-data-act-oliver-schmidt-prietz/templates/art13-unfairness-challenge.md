# Template: Art. 13 unfairness challenge

Produces a written challenge to a contractual term unilaterally imposed by one enterprise on another under Article 13 of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act). The challenge identifies the term, runs the Art. 13 three-test structure (general standard, Art. 13(4) "always unfair" list, Art. 13(5) "presumed unfair" list), and identifies the consequence (non-binding term; severability of remaining terms).

Form: letter, memorandum, or pleading-style submission. Markdown by default. Length depends on the number of challenged terms; typically two to four pages.

This template is for the recipient party (the party on whom the term has been unilaterally imposed). Use to support pre-litigation challenge, dispute settlement reference, or competent-authority complaint. Where the challenge is at the contract-drafting stage rather than post-imposition, use as a clause-review checklist instead.

---

```
[Challenging party letterhead placeholder]

To: [Imposing party, full legal entity name and registered
     address]

Date: [Date]

Reference: [Internal reference; reference to the contract dated
            [date] and to any prior correspondence]

Subject: Challenge to unfair contractual term(s) under Article 13
         of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act)

1. The parties and the contract

   The Challenging Party is [name]. The Imposing Party is
   [name]. The contract is [identification: title, date, version,
   reference]. The contract is a B2B contract concerning access
   to and the use of data and/or liability and remedies for the
   breach or termination of data-related obligations within the
   meaning of Art. 13(1) of the Data Act.

2. Unilateral imposition (Art. 13(6))

   The contested term(s) were unilaterally imposed by the
   Imposing Party on the Challenging Party. The Challenging
   Party was not able to influence the content of the term(s)
   despite an attempt to negotiate. The factual basis for the
   unilateral-imposition finding:

   [Specific factual account. Examples:
   - The contract was supplied as standard terms; the
     Challenging Party was offered no meaningful opportunity to
     amend the contested term(s).
   - The Challenging Party requested amendments by [date] and
     the Imposing Party refused without consideration.
   - The contract was presented on a take-it-or-leave-it basis.]

   Under Art. 13(6) second sentence, the burden of proving that
   the term has not been unilaterally imposed rests on the
   Imposing Party. The Challenging Party invites the Imposing
   Party to discharge that burden, failing which the unilateral-
   imposition finding stands.

3. Scope of Art. 13: the contractual subject matter

   Art. 13(1) applies to terms "concerning access to and the use
   of data or liability and remedies for the breach or the
   termination of data related obligations." The contested
   term(s) fall within this scope as follows:

   [Specific identification of each contested term and the way it
   bears on data access, data use, or liability/remedies for
   data-related obligations.]

   Art. 13(2) carves out terms that reflect mandatory provisions
   of Union law or provisions of Union law that would apply
   absent the contractual regulation. The contested term(s) are
   not so carved out because: [specific reason: e.g. the term
   does not reflect mandatory Union law; or it goes beyond what
   the relevant Union law mandates].

   Art. 13(8) carves out terms defining the main subject matter
   of the contract or the adequacy of the price as against the
   data supplied in exchange. The contested term(s) are not so
   carved out because: [specific reason; the contested terms are
   ancillary, not core subject-matter terms].

4. The three-test analysis

   The Challenging Party challenges the contested term(s) under
   Art. 13 on the following bases. Each contested term is run
   through the test sequence below.

   4(a) [Contested term 1: "[verbatim quotation of the term]"]

   Test 1: Art. 13(4) "always unfair" list.

   [If applicable, identify the Art. 13(4) point(s) the term
   falls within and the factual basis. Art. 13(4) lists three
   categories of always-unfair terms:
   (a) exclusion or limitation of liability of the Imposing
       Party for intentional acts or gross negligence;
   (b) exclusion of the Challenging Party's remedies in the
       case of the Imposing Party's non-performance, or
       exclusion of the Imposing Party's liability for breach;
   (c) the Imposing Party's exclusive right to determine
       conformity of supplied data or to interpret any
       contractual term.]

   [If the term is within Art. 13(4): "The term is unfair under
   Art. 13(4)(a) [or (b) or (c)]. Art. 13(4) makes the
   characterisation conclusive; the Imposing Party cannot rebut
   the unfairness by reference to other considerations."]

   Test 2: Art. 13(5) "presumed unfair" list.

   [If applicable, identify the Art. 13(5) point(s) the term
   falls within and the factual basis. Art. 13(5) lists seven
   categories of presumed-unfair terms:
   (a) inappropriate limitation of remedies for non-performance
       or extension of liability of the Challenging Party;
   (b) access and use of the Challenging Party's data in a
       significantly detrimental manner, particularly where the
       data is commercially sensitive or trade-secret-/IP-
       protected;
   (c) prevention of the Challenging Party from using the data
       it provided or generated during the contract;
   (d) prevention of termination within a reasonable period;
   (e) prevention of obtaining a copy of the data provided or
       generated;
   (f) termination at unreasonably short notice;
   (g) unilateral change of price or substantive condition
       without valid reason or termination right.]

   [If the term is within Art. 13(5): "The term is presumed
   unfair under Art. 13(5)(a) [or (b) or (c) ...]. The Imposing
   Party may rebut the presumption by demonstrating that the
   term, despite falling within the list, does not grossly
   deviate from good commercial practice, contrary to good faith
   and fair dealing. The Challenging Party submits that the
   Imposing Party cannot discharge this rebuttal because:
   [specific facts undermining a possible rebuttal]."]

   Test 3: Art. 13(3) general standard.

   [Whether or not the term falls within Art. 13(4) or (5), the
   general standard applies: a term is unfair if its use grossly
   deviates from good commercial practice in data access and
   use, contrary to good faith and fair dealing.

   The contested term grossly deviates from good commercial
   practice for the following reasons:

   - [Specific reason 1]
   - [Specific reason 2]
   - [Specific reason 3]]

   Conclusion on Term 1: the term is unfair under Art. 13(3)
   [and is within Art. 13(4)/(5) where applicable] and is
   therefore not binding on the Challenging Party under Art.
   13(1).

   4(b) [Contested term 2: ...]

   [Repeat the same three-test structure for each contested
   term.]

5. Severability (Art. 13(7))

   Where each unfair contractual term is severable from the
   remaining terms of the contract, the remaining terms continue
   to be binding under Art. 13(7).

   The Challenging Party's position on severability is:

   [Identify, term by term, whether the unfair term is severable
   from the remaining contract and the consequence of severance.
   In some cases the entire contractual package depends on the
   unfair term and severance dismantles the deal. In others the
   unfair term is genuinely peripheral and severance leaves the
   rest standing. The position should be explicit.]

6. Anti-derogation (Art. 13(9))

   The parties cannot, under Art. 13(9), exclude the application
   of Art. 13, derogate from it, or vary its effects. Any term
   in the contract purporting to do so (for example, an
   "exclusion of Art. 13" clause or a "no-challenge" clause) is
   itself unenforceable.

7. Estoppel against the supplier (Art. 13(6) third sentence)

   The contracting party that supplied the contested term cannot
   argue that the term is unfair. The Challenging Party here is
   the recipient of the supplied term, not the supplier;
   Art. 13(6) third sentence is satisfied.

8. Remedy sought

   The Challenging Party seeks:

   (a) confirmation by the Imposing Party that the contested
       term(s) are not binding on the Challenging Party under
       Art. 13(1);

   (b) [where applicable] amendment of the contract to remove
       or replace the unfair term(s) by terms compatible with
       Art. 13;

   (c) [where applicable] specific consequences flowing from
       severance (e.g. restoration of the position absent the
       unfair term; refund of payments made under the unfair
       term; release from obligations the unfair term purported
       to create);

   (d) [where applicable] preservation of all rights to seek
       redress before a court or tribunal, to lodge a complaint
       with the competent authority designated pursuant to
       Art. 37, or to refer the matter to a dispute settlement
       body under Art. 10.

9. Response deadline

   The Challenging Party requests the Imposing Party's
   substantive response within [number] working days. In the
   absence of a substantive response, the Challenging Party
   reserves all remedies, including litigation, complaint, and
   dispute settlement reference.

[Signature block placeholder]

Enclosures:
- Copy of the contract dated [date]
- Where applicable: prior correspondence on the contested terms;
  documentary evidence of unilateral imposition; sector
  comparables on good commercial practice.
```
