# Drafter notes: art4-7-withholding-notice

Stage 2 sits between stage 1 (safeguards) and stage 3 (refusal). Stage 2 is procedurally lighter than stage 3 (no exceptional-circumstances and conjunction tests) but substantively still requires demonstrated facts on one of three specific triggers. Drafters fail this template most often by mixing triggers, by jumping to stage 2 without a stage-1 record, or by treating stage 2 as a unilateral discretion.

## Substantive risks of using the template

- **Stage 2 is trigger-conditional.** Art. 4(7) lists three specific triggers: no agreement on the necessary measures; user fails to implement agreed measures; user undermines confidentiality. The data holder must identify which trigger is engaged and substantiate the facts. The template forces the drafter to pick one trigger; do not pick two or three unless each is genuinely independently engaged on different facts. Mixing triggers weakens the substantiation because the competent authority will look for the weakest link.
- **Disagreement caused by the data holder's disproportionate demands is not a Trigger-1 ground.** Art. 4(6) requires proportionate measures. Where the data holder has demanded disproportionate measures and the user has refused, the data holder cannot then invoke Trigger 1 ("no agreement"). The competent authority will assess the negotiation record. See `references/gates/trade-secrets-directive.md` enforcement-signal section on stage-1 documentation.
- **The "undermining" trigger requires demonstration, not assertion.** Trigger 3 (the user has undermined confidentiality) requires the data holder to show, with evidence, what the user did and how that compromised the trade-secret status. Suspicion, generalised mistrust, and reputational concerns about the user are not sufficient. The template demands a specific factual account in section 2.
- **Withholding versus suspension.** "Withhold" applies to data not yet shared; "suspend" applies to ongoing sharing of data already in flight. The two are not interchangeable. The template asks the drafter to pick one and to address the operational consequence (data already shared remains subject to the existing safeguards under suspension; under pure withholding, no data is in flight).
- **Competent authority notification is constitutive.** Art. 4(7) third sentence: the data holder "shall notify the competent authority... and identify which measures have not been agreed or implemented and, where relevant, which trade secrets have had their confidentiality undermined." A stage-2 decision without notification is procedurally defective. Part 2 of the template is mandatory, not optional. Run it in parallel with Part 1.
- **The "in writing without undue delay" obligation.** Art. 4(7) second sentence requires the decision to be "duly substantiated and provided in writing to the user without undue delay." Verbal warnings, in-person conversations, or generalised emails about safeguards problems do not constitute the stage-2 decision. The stage-2 decision is the written notice. The drafter should ensure that the written notice is sent at or near the moment of the data holder's decision; delays expose the data holder to argument that stage 2 was effectively in operation before notification.
- **The Stage-2-to-Stage-3 boundary.** Stage 2 is "we can't share for now until [remediation]." Stage 3 is "we refuse to share at all." Drafters sometimes draft stage-2 notices that operate as effective stage-3 refusals (no remediation path, indefinite withholding, no substantive engagement). That is procedurally a stage-3 refusal and must meet stage-3 substantive requirements, which are harder. Where the data holder genuinely intends to refuse outright, use the stage-3 template (`art4-8-refusal-letter.md`) and meet the conjunction.

## Common drafting mistakes the drafter should check for

- Asserting the trigger without substantiating it. Section 2 of the template requires specific facts. A notice that says "the parties could not agree" without explaining the negotiation is procedurally insufficient.
- Identifying the trade-secret data inconsistently with the stage-1 identification. The withheld data should be a subset of (or coextensive with) the data identified as trade-secret in the stage-1 agreement. Inventing new trade-secret categories at stage 2 invites a challenge on whether the identification was timely.
- Treating Trigger 3 (undermining) as covering past minor incidents that did not actually compromise trade-secret status. Trigger 3 requires actual undermining, not theoretical risk of undermining.
- Indefinite withholding without remediation pathway. Section 7 of the template requires a remediation pathway where appropriate. A notice that withholds indefinitely with no path to resumption is operating as a stage-3 refusal without meeting stage-3 substantive thresholds.
- Failing to enclose the supporting documentation in the competent authority notification. Section 5 of Part 2 lists the documents. The authority will assess the stage-2 decision on the record provided; weak or absent supporting documents lead to resumption orders.
- Drafting on Trigger 2 (failure to implement) without giving the user an opportunity to remedy. While Art. 4(7) does not require a cure period as a matter of regulation, competent authorities look favourably on data holders that gave reasonable notice. The McIntyre OSS enforcement signals suggest data holders who jump to withholding without prior notice are treated more sceptically.
- Using stage 2 to escalate competitive disputes. Where the underlying issue is that the user is using the data in ways the data holder dislikes but that are permitted under Art. 4(10), stage 2 is not the right tool. Address the use issue under Art. 4(10) enforcement, not the trade-secret regime.
- Sending the notification to the wrong competent authority. Art. 37(10) places the entity under the competence of the Member State of establishment. Verify the actual designated authority for that Member State; the Commission's register at Art. 37(7) is the source of truth.

## Length and tone

Factual, restrained, specific. The notice is a regulatory action with a defined statutory predicate; it is not a complaint about the user. The competent authority notification (Part 2) is even more restrained: it documents the data holder's exercise of an Art. 4(7) right and provides the regulator with the material to assess.
