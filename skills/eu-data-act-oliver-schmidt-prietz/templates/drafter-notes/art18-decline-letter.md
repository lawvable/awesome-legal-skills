# Drafter notes: art18-decline-letter

The Art. 18 decline window is short and the grounds are limited. Data holders that decline outside the window or on extra-regulatory grounds expose themselves to enforcement. Data holders that fail to use the modification mechanism where it would resolve a defective request waste a procedural option.

## Substantive risks of using the template

- **The 5/30 working day window is constitutive.** Art. 18(2) provides that a decline or modification must be "without undue delay and, in any event, no later than" 5 working days (Art. 15(1)(a) emergency) or 30 working days (other exceptional need). A response outside the window is not a valid Art. 18(2) decline; the data holder may be in a position of non-compliance with Art. 18(1). The drafter should diary the deadline on receipt and treat it as inflexible.
- **The grounds in Art. 18(2)(a)-(c) are exhaustive.** A data holder cannot decline on policy preference, commercial inconvenience, or generalised concern about the requesting body's purposes. The three grounds are: no control over the data; prior request for the same purpose without erasure notification; the request does not meet Art. 17 conditions.
- **Art. 18(2)(c) is the most-used and most-defective ground.** Drafters often invoke Art. 18(2)(c) without identifying which specific Art. 17(1) or 17(2) conditions are unmet. The template forces field-by-field identification. A decline that says "the request is non-compliant" without saying which item is unmet is procedurally weak; the requesting body can simply resubmit identical content and demand engagement.
- **Modification beats decline where possible.** Where the request is defective but the data holder could comply if specific defects were cured, seeking modification is procedurally cleaner than declining. The requesting body can address the defects and resubmit. Outright decline forces the requesting body to escalate to the competent authority or court. The McIntyre OSS enforcement signals show that data holders that engage constructively are treated more favourably than those that decline reflexively.
- **Art. 19(3) trade-secret discipline is a separate matter from decline.** The data holder cannot decline an Art. 17 request solely because it concerns trade-secret data. Art. 19(3) provides a safeguards framework similar to but not identical to Art. 4(6); the receiving body must take measures to preserve confidentiality before disclosure. Section 5 of the template addresses this where the request is being complied with after modification. Where the trade-secret safeguards are inadequate, the data holder's recourse is to seek modification (e.g. requiring specific safeguards) rather than to decline outright.
- **Art. 18(3) identification requirement.** Where the data holder declines on Art. 18(2)(b), it must indicate the identity of the previously requesting body. Section 3 Option B of the template surfaces this. Failure to identify breaches Art. 18(3) and may invalidate the decline.
- **Cross-border procedure under Art. 22.** Where the data holder is in a different Member State from the requesting body, the matter is examined by the data holder's Member State competent authority. Section 6 of the template surfaces this. The competent authority can transmit the request to the data holder or reject it; the data holder's Art. 18 decline operates within this Art. 22 framework.
- **Art. 17 (no control) cannot be invented.** "Control" is operative legal control, not generalised business relationship. A data holder that has access to data but does not control it (because it is held by a sub-processor under contract, or by an affiliate, or in a position where access requires another party's cooperation) may genuinely lack control. But "the data is hard to extract" or "we don't normally use it" is not lack of control.
- **Redress and modification (Art. 18(5)).** Where the requesting body challenges the decline or the data holder wishes to challenge the request and the matter cannot be resolved by modification, the matter is referred to the competent authority of the Member State of the data holder's establishment. The decline letter should signpost this.

## Common drafting mistakes the drafter should check for

- Missing the deadline. Diary on receipt; do not draft generously in the expectation that the requesting body will accept lateness.
- Invoking Art. 18(2)(c) without identifying which Art. 17 conditions are unmet. The template's section 3 Option C catalogue should be edited down to the specific items; do not include items that are met.
- Declining outright where modification would resolve the matter. The drafter should always ask: can a modified request comply? If yes, propose the modifications.
- Invoking Art. 18(2)(a) loosely. "No control" is a strong substantive ground but it is narrow; the data holder must mean it.
- Failing to identify the previous requesting body under Art. 18(2)(b). Art. 18(3) is mandatory.
- Treating the Art. 19(3) trade-secret reservation as the decline ground. Trade-secret status alone does not justify decline; the right tool is modification or, on compliance, the Art. 19(3) safeguards framework.
- Failing to address the requesting body's Art. 17(2)(g) data coordinator transmission. Where the requesting body bypassed the data coordinator, the procedural deficiency is real and should be identified.
- Forgetting the Art. 22 cross-border note. Where applicable, copy the competent authority of the data holder's Member State.
- Sending the decline only to the requesting body. Where Art. 22 applies, the competent authority of the data holder's Member State has examined the request and should be informed of the response.
- Missing the Digital Omnibus check. The Digital Omnibus does not centrally restructure Art. 17/18, but the drafter should check `sources/digital-omnibus-amendments-tracker.md` for any current adjustments.

## Length and tone

Formal, factual, surgical. The decline or modification is a regulatory communication, not a commercial dispute. The tone should be cooperative where modification is the route; precise and complete where decline is the route. The requesting body may escalate; the data holder's response should be defensible on the face of the document.
