# Template: Provider's Art. 32 third-country request assessment

Produces the data processing service provider's documented assessment of a decision or judgment of a third-country court, tribunal, or administrative authority requiring transfer of, or access to, non-personal data held in the Union, under Article 32 of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act). The assessment determines whether the request is enforceable under Art. 32(2) (international agreement path) or Art. 32(3) (cumulative conditions path), and, if so, the minimum-data response under Art. 32(4) and the customer-notification obligation under Art. 32(5).

Form: written internal assessment, with downstream communications. Markdown by default. Length depends on the complexity of the third-country instrument; typically three to five pages.

PRECONDITION: Art. 32 governs non-personal data only. For personal data, GDPR Chapter V applies (Arts. 44-50 GDPR), including the rules on adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses, derogations, and Art. 48 GDPR (which closely parallels Art. 32 Data Act).

PRECONDITION: The decision or judgment must concern non-personal data falling within the scope of the Data Act held in the Union. Where the data is outside Union holding, Art. 32 does not apply.

---

```
[Provider letterhead or internal memo format]

Assessment of third-country request for non-personal data
under Article 32 of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act)

Provider: [Provider, full legal entity name]
Third-country instrument: [Identification: court/tribunal/
                          administrative authority name; the
                          decision or judgment reference;
                          date of issuance]
Assessment date: [Date]
Assessor: [Assessing party, with legal review by [counsel]]

---

1. Threshold: data scope

   1(a) The data the subject of the third-country request is:
   [Specific identification.]

   1(b) Is the data non-personal data within the meaning of the
   GDPR? [Y/N. Where the data is mixed or includes personal
   data, the GDPR Chapter V regime governs the personal-data
   component independently of Art. 32. The Art. 32 analysis
   continues for the non-personal-data component only.]

   1(c) Is the data within the scope of the Data Act?
   [Identification of the chapter under which the data falls or
   would fall: Ch II product/related service data; Ch III B2B
   data; Ch V exceptional-need data; or Ch VI data processing
   service customer data.]

   1(d) Is the data held in the Union? [Y/N. Where the data is
   stored in or processed within the Union, the Art. 32 regime
   applies. Where the data is held outside the Union, Art. 32
   does not apply, and the analysis runs under other Union or
   national law, contract law, and third-country law.]

   [If 1(b)-(d) are all "Yes," Art. 32 applies and the
   assessment proceeds.]

2. Conflict with Union or national law (Art. 32(1))

   Would transfer or access pursuant to this third-country
   request create a conflict with Union law or with the
   national law of the relevant Member State?

   [Factual and legal analysis. Examples of potential
   conflicts:
   - Conflict with the GDPR if the dataset is mixed and the
     access mechanism is not within GDPR-compatible channels.
   - Conflict with Union sectoral law (e.g. financial-services
     confidentiality; cybersecurity obligations under NIS2).
   - Conflict with national law (e.g. national security or
     defence interests; sectoral confidentiality regimes).
   - Conflict with trade-secret protection obligations under
     Art. 5(9) of the Data Act and the Trade Secrets Directive.]

   [Where conflict is identified, Arts. 32(2) and 32(3) define
   the only paths to lawful compliance. Without one of those
   paths, transfer or access shall not take place.]

3. Path A: Art. 32(2) international agreement

   Is the third-country request based on an international
   agreement, such as a mutual legal assistance treaty (MLAT),
   in force between the requesting third country and the Union,
   or such an agreement between the requesting third country and
   a Member State?

   3(a) Identification of any potentially applicable
   international agreement: [Name, parties, date of entry into
   force, scope.]

   3(b) Does the agreement cover this type of request and this
   type of data? [Analysis of scope: subject matter; data
   categories; procedural requirements; applicable safeguards.]

   3(c) Has the third-country authority followed the agreement's
   procedural requirements in issuing this request? [Y/N. Many
   third-country requests are issued under domestic law without
   reference to or compliance with the international agreement;
   such requests do not satisfy Art. 32(2).]

   3(d) Conclusion on Path A: [Path A applies / does not apply.
   If Path A applies, the request is recognisable and enforceable
   under Art. 32(2); proceed to section 5 (minimum data) and
   section 6 (customer notification). If Path A does not apply,
   proceed to Path B (section 4).]

4. Path B: Art. 32(3) cumulative conditions

   In the absence of an international agreement under Art.
   32(2), transfer or access takes place only where all three
   cumulative conditions are met.

   4(a) Condition (a): The third-country system requires the
   reasons and proportionality of the decision or judgment to
   be set out and requires the decision or judgment to be
   specific in character (for instance by establishing a
   sufficient link to certain suspected persons or
   infringements).

   [Analysis. Identify the third-country system's general
   requirements on reasons and proportionality. Identify
   whether the decision or judgment at issue contains those
   reasons and is sufficiently specific. Generic, broad, or
   reasonless decisions fail this limb.]

   Conclusion on (a): [Met / Not met.]

   4(b) Condition (b): The reasoned objection of the addressee
   is subject to a review by a competent third-country court or
   tribunal.

   [Analysis. Identify the available review mechanism in the
   third-country system. The review must be substantive (the
   objection can affect the outcome), independent (the
   reviewing court or tribunal is independent), and accessible
   (the addressee can bring the objection).]

   Conclusion on (b): [Met / Not met.]

   4(c) Condition (c): The competent third-country court or
   tribunal issuing the decision or judgment or reviewing the
   decision of an administrative authority is empowered under
   the law of that third country to take duly into account the
   relevant legal interests of the provider of the data
   protected by Union law or by the national law of the
   relevant Member State.

   [Analysis. Identify whether the reviewing court or tribunal
   can consider the Union or national law interests of the
   provider when assessing the decision or judgment.]

   Conclusion on (c): [Met / Not met.]

   4(d) Overall conclusion on Path B: All three conditions met:
   [Y/N. If Yes, transfer or access may take place subject to
   sections 5 and 6. If No, the provider may reject the
   request, on the grounds that the conditions are not met.]

5. National body consultation (Art. 32(3) second
   subparagraph)

   The provider may consult the relevant national body or
   authority competent for international cooperation in legal
   matters in the Member State of [provider's establishment /
   data location].

   5(a) Is consultation appropriate? Consultation is
   particularly recommended where:
   - The decision may relate to trade secrets or other
     commercially sensitive data, or to content protected by
     intellectual property rights;
   - The transfer may lead to re-identification of personal
     data (relevant for mixed datasets);
   - The decision may impinge on the national security or
     defence interests of the Union or its Member States (in
     which case the consultation is for the purpose of
     determining whether the data requested concerns national
     security or defence).

   5(b) Consultation status: [Sent on [date] / not sent / reply
   received on [date]]. If no reply within one month, or if the
   opinion concludes that the Art. 32(3) conditions are not
   met, the provider may reject the request on those grounds.

   5(c) Where the national body has consulted the Commission
   pursuant to Art. 32(3) second subparagraph: identification
   of any Commission opinion received.

   5(d) National body opinion (if received): [Summary.]

6. Minimum-data response (Art. 32(4))

   Where the conditions in Art. 32(2) or 32(3) are met, the
   provider shall provide the minimum amount of data permissible
   in response to the request, on the basis of the reasonable
   interpretation of the request by the provider or the
   relevant national body or authority referred to in section 5.

   6(a) Reasonable interpretation of the third-country request:
   [Analysis of the scope of data permitted to be transferred or
   accessed.]

   6(b) Minimum data: [Identification of the minimum data set
   that the provider will transfer or make available, with
   reasons for the categories and items selected and the items
   excluded.]

7. Customer notification (Art. 32(5))

   The provider shall inform the customer about the existence
   of the third-country request before complying, except where
   the request serves law enforcement purposes and for as long
   as necessary to preserve the effectiveness of the law
   enforcement activity.

   7(a) Does the request serve law enforcement purposes? [Y/N]

   7(b) Where Yes, the provider does not inform the customer
   before complying, for the period necessary to preserve the
   effectiveness of the law enforcement activity. The provider
   should diary the date on which notification becomes possible
   and notify the customer at that point.

   7(c) Where No (or after the law-enforcement-effectiveness
   period expires), the provider notifies the customer before
   complying. The notification template is at section 9.

8. Decision

   On the basis of sections 1-7, the provider's decision is:

   [Pick one:
   - The Art. 32 conditions are met under Path A (Art. 32(2));
     the provider will comply with the minimum-data response
     and customer notification as appropriate.
   - The Art. 32 conditions are met under Path B (Art. 32(3));
     the provider will comply with the minimum-data response
     and customer notification as appropriate.
   - The Art. 32 conditions are not met. The provider will
     reject the third-country request and so inform the
     third-country authority in writing, citing the specific
     Art. 32(3) condition(s) not met or the absence of a
     Path-A international agreement.
   - National body opinion is awaited. The provider will
     suspend response pending receipt or expiry of the one-
     month window.]

9. Customer notification template (Art. 32(5))

   [Where customer notification is appropriate, draft
   incorporating the following content:]

```
[Provider letterhead]

To: [Customer]

Date: [Date]

Subject: Notification of third-country authority access
         request under Article 32(5) of Regulation (EU)
         2023/2854 (Data Act)

The provider has received a [decision / judgment] of [third-
country authority] dated [date] requiring [transfer of / access
to] non-personal data held in the Union and concerning the
customer.

In accordance with Art. 32(5) of the Data Act, the provider
informs the customer about the existence of the request before
[complying / making any response].

The data covered by the request is: [identification].

The provider has assessed the request under Art. 32 of the Data
Act. The provider's response will be [comply via Art. 32(2) or
32(3); reject; pending national body opinion].

The customer may [contact the provider with any information
relevant to the assessment, particularly where the data is
trade-secret-protected, contains intellectual property, or
implicates national security or defence interests of the Union
or a Member State].

[Signature block placeholder]
```

10. Rejection letter template (where Art. 32 conditions not
    met)

    [Where the provider rejects the request, draft
    incorporating the following content:]

```
[Provider letterhead]

To: [Third-country authority]

Date: [Date]

Subject: Response to your [decision / judgment] dated [date]

The provider has received your [decision / judgment] dated
[date] requiring [transfer of / access to] non-personal data
held in the Union and falling within the scope of Regulation
(EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act).

Compliance with the [decision / judgment] would risk putting
the provider in conflict with Union law or with the national
law of the relevant Member State. The provider has assessed the
[decision / judgment] under Art. 32 of the Data Act. The
provider has determined that:

- The [decision / judgment] is not based on an international
  agreement, such as a mutual legal assistance treaty, in
  force between [third country] and the Union, or between
  [third country] and the relevant Member State (Art. 32(2)
  does not apply); and

- The cumulative conditions in Art. 32(3) are not met
  because: [specific identification of which condition(s) is
  unmet, citing Art. 32(3)(a), (b), or (c) and the factual
  basis].

[Where applicable: "The provider has consulted the relevant
national body competent for international cooperation in legal
matters pursuant to Art. 32(3) second subparagraph. The
opinion received on [date] [or, after expiry of the one-month
window]: [confirms the assessment / supports rejection].]

The provider therefore rejects the [decision / judgment]. The
provider [reserves all rights / is prepared to engage with a
revised request that satisfies the Art. 32 conditions].

[Signature block placeholder]
```

11. Documentation and audit trail

    This assessment is retained in the provider's compliance
    records. The provider may also need to provide it to the
    competent authority designated pursuant to Art. 37 on
    request.

[Reviewer sign-off]
```
