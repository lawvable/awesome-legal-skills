# Template: Customer-side Art. 25 switching-clause review checklist

Produces a structured review of a data processing service contract (cloud services contract) from the customer's perspective, identifying compliance with Article 25 of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act) (mandatory switching contract terms) and flagging non-compliant or under-compliant clauses for renegotiation.

Form: clause-by-clause review checklist. Markdown by default. Length depends on the contract's complexity; typically three to six pages.

Use this template at three points:
- Pre-contract review (before signing): identify defects in the proposed contract that the customer should renegotiate.
- Existing-contract review (during the term): identify defects to demand the provider remedy, particularly in light of the 12 September 2025 application date for Ch VI and the 12 January 2027 abolition of switching charges.
- Pre-switching review (before initiating a switch): identify the contractual basis for the customer's exit and any defects that will impede the switch.

```
[Customer letterhead or internal memo format]

Review of data processing service contract for Art. 25 (Data
Act) compliance

Contract: [Contract identification: title, date, parties,
           service description]
Reviewer: [Reviewing party]
Review date: [Date]
Purpose: [Pre-contract / existing / pre-switching review]

---

1. Provider and service scope

   Provider: [Provider, full legal entity name and registered
   address].

   Service(s) covered by the contract: [Specific identification.
   IaaS / PaaS / SaaS; the actual data processing services
   within the meaning of Art. 2(8) of the Data Act.]

   Custom-built carve-out under Art. 31: [Does the provider
   claim or could the provider claim that the majority of main
   features have been custom-built, with the consequence that
   Arts. 23(d), 29, 30(1) and 30(3) do not apply? If yes, see
   `art31-custom-built-assessment.md` template.]

   Member State of provider establishment: [Identification, for
   the competent authority under Art. 37(4)(b).]

2. Art. 25(1) compliance: written contract

   [Verify: The rights of the customer and the obligations of
   the provider in relation to switching are clearly set out in
   a written contract. The provider makes the contract available
   to the customer prior to signing in a way that allows the
   customer to store and reproduce it.]

   Finding: [compliant / non-compliant. If non-compliant, the
   specific defect.]

3. Art. 25(2)(a) compliance: switching mechanism and
   transitional period

   [Verify: The contract contains clauses allowing the customer,
   upon request, to switch to a different data processing
   service or port to on-premises ICT infrastructure, without
   undue delay and in any event not after the mandatory maximum
   transitional period of 30 calendar days, initiated after the
   maximum notice period under Art. 25(2)(d), during which the
   service contract remains applicable and during which the
   provider shall provide:

   (i) reasonable assistance to the customer and third parties
       authorised by the customer in the switching process;
   (ii) due care to maintain business continuity, and continued
        provision of the functions or services;
   (iii) clear information on known risks to continuity in the
         provision of the functions or services;
   (iv) high level of security maintained throughout the
        switching process, in particular for data during
        transfer and during retrieval.]

   Findings:
   - [Item (i) - reasonable assistance: compliant / defective.
     Specific clause references. Defects identified.]
   - [Item (ii) - business continuity, continued service:
     compliant / defective. Specific clause references.]
   - [Item (iii) - continuity risk disclosure: compliant /
     defective.]
   - [Item (iv) - security: compliant / defective.]

4. Art. 25(2)(b) compliance: exit-strategy support

   [Verify: An obligation of the provider to support the
   customer's exit strategy, including by providing all relevant
   information.]

   Finding: [compliant / defective. Specific clause references.]

5. Art. 25(2)(c) compliance: contract termination clauses

   [Verify: A clause specifying that the contract is to be
   considered terminated and the customer notified of the
   termination:
   (i) upon successful completion of the switching process; or
   (ii) at the end of the maximum notice period under Art.
        25(2)(d), where the customer does not wish to switch
        but to erase its exportable data and digital assets.]

   Finding: [compliant / defective.]

6. Art. 25(2)(d) compliance: maximum notice period

   [Verify: A maximum notice period for initiation of the
   switching process, not exceeding two months.]

   Finding: [compliant / defective. The contract's notice period
   is [period]. Where this exceeds two months, the clause is
   unenforceable to the extent of the excess. Where the contract
   provides for the legally maximum two months but the customer
   prefers a shorter notice period, the customer may negotiate
   for one.]

7. Art. 25(2)(e) compliance: exhaustive specification of
   exportable data and digital assets

   [Verify: An exhaustive specification of all categories of
   data and digital assets that can be ported during the
   switching process, including, at a minimum, all exportable
   data.]

   Finding: [compliant / defective. Where the contract is silent
   or vague on what is exportable, the provider may attempt to
   exclude key data at switching time. The customer should
   require a written exhaustive list. The list should at minimum
   include all customer data and customer-generated digital
   assets.]

8. Art. 25(2)(f) compliance: exemption specification for
   internal-functioning data

   [Verify: An exhaustive specification of categories of data
   specific to the internal functioning of the provider's
   service that are exempted from the exportable data under Art.
   25(2)(e), where a risk of breach of trade secrets of the
   provider exists, provided such exemptions do not impede or
   delay the switching process.]

   Finding: [compliant / defective. Common defect: the provider
   asserts broad trade-secret exemptions covering data the
   customer reasonably expects to receive. The exemption is
   only available where a risk of breach of trade secrets
   genuinely exists; vague claims of "proprietary
   configuration" do not qualify.]

9. Art. 25(2)(g) compliance: minimum retrieval period

   [Verify: A minimum period for data retrieval of at least 30
   calendar days, starting after the termination of the
   transitional period agreed between the parties under Art.
   25(2)(a) and (4).]

   Finding: [compliant / defective.]

10. Art. 25(2)(h) compliance: full erasure post-retrieval

    [Verify: A clause guaranteeing full erasure of all
    exportable data and digital assets generated directly by
    the customer or relating to the customer directly, after
    expiry of the retrieval period under Art. 25(2)(g) or after
    expiry of an alternative agreed period later than the
    retrieval period expiry, provided the switching process has
    been completed successfully.]

    Finding: [compliant / defective.]

11. Art. 25(2)(i) compliance: switching charges

    [Verify: Switching charges may be imposed by the provider
    in accordance with Art. 29. Note: from 12 January 2027,
    Art. 29(1) abolishes switching charges; switching is to be
    provided at no charge. Before that date, switching charges
    must comply with Art. 29.]

    Finding: [compliant / defective. Where the contract still
    provides for switching charges beyond 12 January 2027, the
    customer should secure an amendment or rely on the direct-
    effect application of Art. 29(1).]

12. Art. 25(3) compliance: customer's choice on termination

    [Verify: Clauses providing that the customer may notify the
    provider, upon termination of the maximum notice period, of
    its decision to:
    (a) switch to a different provider;
    (b) switch to on-premises ICT infrastructure;
    (c) erase its exportable data and digital assets.]

    Finding: [compliant / defective.]

13. Art. 25(4) compliance: technical-infeasibility procedure

    [Verify: Where the mandatory maximum transitional period of
    30 calendar days under Art. 25(2)(a) is technically
    unfeasible, the provider notifies the customer within 14
    working days of the switching request, duly justifies the
    technical unfeasibility, and indicates an alternative
    transitional period not exceeding seven months. Service
    continuity is ensured throughout the alternative
    transitional period.]

    Finding: [compliant / defective. Where the contract does not
    provide for the procedure, the customer is exposed in the
    event that the provider invokes technical unfeasibility at
    switching time.]

14. Art. 25(5) compliance: customer's right to extend

    [Verify: Clauses providing the customer with the right to
    extend the transitional period once for a period more
    appropriate for its own purposes.]

    Finding: [compliant / defective.]

15. Charges (Art. 29)

    [Verify the switching-charges clauses against Art. 29:
    (1) From 12 January 2027, switching charges, including data
    egress charges, are abolished for switching out.
    (2) Before that date, charges must reflect costs only,
    without exceeding such costs, and the provider's burden of
    proving the cost basis is real.
    (3) Data egress charges for in-parallel use under Art. 34
    are permitted but only at cost.]

    Finding: [compliant / defective. Where defective, identify
    the specific clauses and the basis for non-compliance.]

16. Functional equivalence (IaaS) and open interfaces
    (PaaS/SaaS) (Art. 30)

    [Verify the provider's compliance with Art. 30:
    - For IaaS (Art. 30(1)): provider takes all reasonable
      measures to enable functional equivalence in the use of
      the new service.
    - For PaaS and SaaS (Art. 30(2)-(5)): open interfaces,
      machine-readable export formats, harmonised standards
      where adopted.]

    Finding: [compliant / defective.]

17. Related issues

    17.1 Pre-contractual transparency (Art. 26)

    [Verify the provider has provided pre-contractual
    information about the switching process and the customer's
    rights under Ch VI.]

    17.2 In-parallel use (Art. 34)

    [Where relevant: are the in-parallel-use provisions of Art.
    34 reflected? In-parallel use is permitted; data egress
    charges for in-parallel use are permitted at cost only.]

    17.3 Anti-derogation

    [Verify: the contract does not purport to exclude or limit
    the customer's Ch VI rights. Any term doing so is void.]

    17.4 Art. 13 unfairness exposure

    [Where the customer is an enterprise on the receiving end
    of unilaterally imposed terms: identify any Ch VI terms
    that may also be unfair under Art. 13. Use the `art13-
    unfairness-challenge.md` template for the substantive
    challenge.]

18. Summary of findings and renegotiation priorities

    [Tabulate the findings. Identify which defects are
    blocking (the customer cannot switch effectively without
    remediation), which are material (the customer's switching
    rights are constrained but the switch is feasible), and
    which are minor.]

    Renegotiation priorities:
    1. [Priority 1]
    2. [Priority 2]
    3. [...]

19. Recommended action

    [Specific recommendations:
    - Pre-contract: which clauses to renegotiate before signing.
    - Existing contract: which clauses to demand the provider
      amend by [date]; reservation of rights pending amendment.
    - Pre-switching: which contractual basis to rely on for
      switching execution; which provider obligations are
      operationally critical.

    Also: whether to escalate to the competent authority under
    Art. 37 in case of non-cooperation by the provider; whether
    to use the dispute settlement body under Art. 10(4).]

[Signature block placeholder]
```
