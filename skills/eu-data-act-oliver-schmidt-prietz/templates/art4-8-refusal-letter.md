# Template: Art. 4(8) refusal letter (stage 3)

Produces the data holder's stage-3 written refusal of an Art. 4(1) data access request under Article 4(8) of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act). The highest-risk drafting in Chapter II. The template tracks the eight-step regulatory structure of Art. 4(8) and the operative requirements of Recital 31.

Form: formal letter. Markdown by default. Typically one and a half to two and a half pages.

PRECONDITION: Do not use this template unless ALL eight limbs are independently satisfied on the facts. In particular, the demonstration must establish that disclosure would cause damage that is highly likely, serious, AND irreparable (the Recital 31 conjunction). Trade-secret status alone never satisfies Art. 4(8). If the conjunction cannot be demonstrated, the refusal cannot be drafted; use the Path-2 output in `references/scenarios/ch2-trade-secret-stage-3-refusal.md` instead.

PRECONDITION: Stage 1 (Art. 4(6) safeguards) must have been attempted. Stage 2 (Art. 4(7) withholding) may also have been engaged but is not a precondition for stage 3. Where neither stage 1 nor stage 2 has been engaged, route to `art4-6-trade-secret-safeguards-agreement.md` or `art4-7-withholding-notice.md`.

PRECONDITION: The competent authority notification under Art. 4(8) third sentence runs in parallel; it is not subsequent to the refusal. The template's section 7 corresponds.

```
[Data holder letterhead placeholder]

To: [Requesting user, full legal entity name / natural person
     name and registered address]

Date: [Date of refusal; no later than "without undue delay" after
       the triggering event, which is typically the exhaustion of
       stage-1 negotiation or the data holder's identification of
       stage-3 conditions]

Reference: [Internal reference; reference to the user's request
            date and any prior correspondence]

Subject: Refusal of data access request dated [request date]
         pursuant to Article 4(8) of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854
         (Data Act)

---

1. The request

   On [request date], the User submitted a request for access to
   data under Art. 4(1) of the Data Act, in which the User
   requested [brief restatement of the scope of the request as the
   Data Holder understands it].

   [Where stage 1 has been engaged: "The Data Holder identified
   certain of that data as trade-secret-protected and, in
   accordance with Art. 4(6), proposed proportionate technical and
   organisational measures by [letter / agreement] dated [date].
   The parties [agreed safeguards on [date] / engaged in good-
   faith negotiation between [date] and [date]]."]

   [Where stage 2 has been engaged: "The Data Holder, on [date],
   gave notice of [withholding / suspension] under Art. 4(7) on
   the basis of [stage-2 trigger]. Notwithstanding [the further
   safeguards / the remediation], the Data Holder is unable to
   make the affected data available without exposing itself to
   serious and irreparable economic damage."]

2. Trade-secret status of the requested data

   The data the disclosure of which is the subject of this
   refusal is identified specifically as:

   [Specific identification, category-by-category or field-by-
   field, with cross-reference to the metadata tagging used in
   any prior delivery or in the stage-1 agreement.]

   The Data Holder is the trade-secret holder within the meaning
   of Art. 2(19) Data Act and Art. 2(2) of Directive (EU)
   2016/943 (Trade Secrets Directive) for this data. The data
   satisfies the Art. 2(1) Trade Secrets Directive cumulative
   test:
   (a) [Specific factual basis that the data is not generally
       known among or readily accessible to persons within the
       circles that normally deal with the kind of information];
   (b) [Specific factual basis that the data has commercial value
       because it is secret];
   (c) [Specific factual basis that the Data Holder has taken
       reasonable steps to keep the data secret, including the
       safeguards proposed under Art. 4(6) above].

   [Where the trade secret holder is not the same person as the
   Data Holder: identify the trade secret holder and confirm the
   Data Holder's authority to assert the trade-secret protection
   on its behalf or jointly. Where the Data Holder is not the
   trade secret holder for Art. 4(8) purposes, this refusal
   cannot be sustained; consult `references/gates/trade-secrets-
   directive.md` on the licensed-in scenario.]

3. Exceptional circumstances

   The Data Holder relies on Art. 4(8) in the present matter on
   the following exceptional circumstances:

   [Specific factual circumstances that distinguish this matter
   from the ordinary case of an Art. 4(1) request involving
   trade-secret data. Generic competitive concern is not
   sufficient. Examples of factually exceptional circumstances:
   (i) the User is established in a third country with weak or
   absent trade-secret enforcement; (ii) the data is tied to a
   uniquely novel connected product whose competitive position
   is fragile; (iii) the User is a direct competitor in a market
   defined to a degree that disclosure would have effects
   different in kind from the ordinary B2B data-sharing context;
   (iv) other specific scenario-driven elements.]

   The Data Holder does not treat all Art. 4(1) requests
   involving trade-secret data as exceptional. The
   exceptional-circumstances analysis is specific to the facts
   of this request.

4. Stage-1 safeguards: status and insufficiency

   The Data Holder proposed and the parties [agreed / negotiated
   without agreement] the following Art. 4(6) safeguards in
   relation to this request:

   [Specific identification of the safeguards proposed: technical
   measures and organisational measures.]

   [Either:]

   [Option A, where safeguards were agreed but are insufficient
   to mitigate the damage demonstrated in section 5: "The
   agreed safeguards, although in place, are insufficient to
   mitigate the damage demonstrated in section 5 below. The
   reasons the safeguards are insufficient are: [specific
   factual reasons, e.g. the User's establishment in a third
   country prevents practical enforcement of the safeguards;
   the nature of the data is such that even the strongest
   technical measures permit re-identification of the trade
   secret; the trade secret is reverse-engineerable from the
   disclosed data even with the safeguards in place]."]

   [Option B, where stage-1 negotiation did not yield agreement:
   "The parties engaged in good-faith stage-1 negotiation from
   [date] to [date]. The Data Holder proposed proportionate
   measures; the User [accepted some and rejected others / did
   not accept the Data Holder's proposals; the Data Holder did
   not accept the User's counter-proposals]. The reasons the
   negotiation did not yield safeguards adequate to mitigate the
   damage demonstrated in section 5 below are: [specific
   reasons]."]

5. Demonstration of highly likely serious AND irreparable
   economic damage

   The Data Holder demonstrates that, despite the Art. 4(6)
   safeguards, disclosure of the trade-secret data identified in
   section 2 is highly likely to cause it serious and irreparable
   economic damage. Recital 31 reads "serious economic damage" as
   "serious and irreparable economic loss." Both elements are
   required and are demonstrated separately below.

   5(a) Highly likely.

   [Specific factual demonstration that the damage is highly
   likely. Recital 31 last paragraph: "the concrete risk of
   serious economic damage expected to result from a specific
   data disclosure." The standard is more demanding than
   "possible" or "likely." Identify the specific causal pathway
   from disclosure to damage and the probability factors that
   make it highly likely on the facts.]

   5(b) Serious.

   [Specific factual demonstration of the magnitude of the
   damage. Identify the affected commercial activities. Quantify
   where quantification is possible (e.g. revenue exposure,
   market share at risk, R&D investment at risk). Where
   quantification is not possible, identify the qualitative
   measures that make the damage serious (e.g. the Data Holder's
   business viability in the relevant product market; the
   competitive position of the connected product).]

   5(c) Irreparable.

   [INDEPENDENT FROM 5(b). The most common drafting failure in
   stage-3 refusals is collapsing the conjunction. "Irreparable"
   means not recoverable through ordinary commercial mitigation,
   not covered by insurance, not undone by contractual remedies
   against the User, not diminishing through ordinary commercial
   action.

   Specific factual demonstration that the damage cannot be
   repaired. Examples of irreparability:
   - Once disclosed, the trade secret will become generally known
     and lose its protection under the Trade Secrets Directive
     definition; protection cannot be restored.
   - Damages against the User in a Member State court will not
     compensate the loss because [specific reasons, e.g. the
     User's third-country establishment makes enforcement
     practically impossible; damages cannot replicate the
     competitive position lost; the User's insolvency profile
     makes recovery impossible].
   - The trade secret, once compromised, exposes downstream
     systems or future product generations to harm that cannot
     be remediated.]

   5(d) Objective elements relied on.

   The Data Holder relies on the following objective elements,
   in accordance with Art. 4(8) and Recital 31:

   [Recital 31 expressly identifies three elements:
   (i) the enforceability of trade secrets protection in third
       countries;
   (ii) the nature and level of confidentiality of the data
        requested;
   (iii) the uniqueness and novelty of the connected product.

   The list is illustrative ("in particular") and not
   exhaustive. Other admissible objective elements (see
   `references/gates/trade-secrets-directive.md`) include:
   the competitive relationship between the Data Holder and the
   User; the inadequacy of the User's safeguards in light of past
   incidents or compliance record; the scale of data requested
   in relation to the User's stated purpose; reverse-engineering
   risk; cybersecurity exposure; AI-training risk where
   applicable.

   For each element relied on, the Data Holder identifies the
   specific factual basis. Vague reliance on "competitive harm"
   is insufficient.]

6. Case-by-case basis

   This refusal is specific to this request and is not a blanket
   policy. The Data Holder [has previously honoured Art. 4(1)
   requests from [other users / the same User in different
   contexts]; [where relevant: "the Data Holder honours Art. 4(1)
   requests where the conditions of Art. 4(8) are not met"]. The
   case-by-case analysis is based on the facts identified in
   sections 3, 4, and 5 of this letter.

7. Notification to the competent authority

   The Data Holder has, in parallel with this refusal, notified
   the competent authority of [Member State of establishment]
   designated pursuant to Art. 37 of the Data Act. The
   notification was made on [date] under reference [reference
   number, if assigned].

8. The User's redress options

   Without prejudice to the User's right to seek redress at any
   stage before a court or tribunal of a Member State, the User
   may:

   (a) lodge a complaint with the competent authority designated
       pursuant to Art. 37 of the Data Act in the Member State
       of [data holder's establishment], in accordance with Art.
       37(5)(b). The competent authority shall, without undue
       delay, decide whether and under which conditions data
       sharing is to start; or

   (b) agree with the Data Holder to refer the matter to a
       dispute settlement body certified under Art. 10 of the
       Data Act, in accordance with Art. 4(9)(b).

   This letter does not affect the rights of any data subject
   under Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), including the right of
   access under Art. 15 GDPR. Recital 31 last sentence is
   explicit: the exceptions to data access rights in the Data
   Act do not in any case limit the right of access and right to
   data portability of data subjects under the GDPR.

[Signature block placeholder]

Enclosures:
- Where applicable: the Art. 4(6) safeguards agreement and the
  stage-1 negotiation correspondence.
- Where applicable: the Art. 4(7) withholding notice and the
  related competent authority notification.
- Copy of the competent authority notification of this refusal
  under Art. 4(8) third sentence.
- Supporting evidence for the section 5 demonstration where
  appropriate (technical assessments, market analyses, expert
  opinions, third-country legal opinions on enforceability).
```
