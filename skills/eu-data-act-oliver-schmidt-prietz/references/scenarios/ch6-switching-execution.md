# ch6-switching-execution

**Anchor:** Customer × Ch VI × switching execution. The customer has decided to switch providers (or to port to on-premises infrastructure) and needs to manage the live execution: the operational mechanics during the notice period and the transitional period. The deliverable is a switching-execution plan keyed to the regulatory deadlines and the data-and-asset flow.

**Routes from:**

- "We have decided to switch cloud providers; what do we do?"
- "Walk us through what happens during the transition."
- "Our switching request has been served; what should we be doing each week?"
- "We are switching from IaaS / PaaS / SaaS to a different provider; what do we get?"
- "The source provider says functional equivalence does not apply; is that right?"
- "What does our provider have to give us at retrieval?"

## Canonical fact pattern

The customer has served, or is about to serve, a switching request on the provider under Art. 25(2). The customer has identified a destination provider (or has decided to move to on-premises infrastructure) and has, or is about to make, a plan to upload and reconfigure at the destination. The source provider is in scope of Ch VI. The customer counsel is advising on the mechanics from notice through completion: timeline, data and asset flow, technical entitlements, customer obligations to the destination provider, and what to do if the source provider drags its feet.

The service is IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS. The customer is often confused about whether it owes the source provider any cooperation duty, whether it can demand functional equivalence, and what counts as exportable data. The destination provider is a parallel actor with its own Art. 27 good-faith duty.

---

## Critical disciplines

- **The two-phase timing.** Notice runs first, transitional period starts at the end of notice. Per FAQ Q56 this is the Commission's interpretation; the regulation makes it explicit at Art. 25(2)(a) ("to be initiated after the maximum notice period referred to in point (d)"). Maximum notice is two months (Art. 25(2)(d)). Maximum transitional period is 30 calendar days (Art. 25(2)(a)), extendable to seven months on demonstrated technical unfeasibility (Art. 25(4)), or to a customer-elected longer period (Art. 25(5)). Minimum retrieval window is 30 calendar days after the transitional period ends (Art. 25(2)(g)).
- **The IaaS-only functional-equivalence point.** Functional equivalence under Art. 30(1) (re-establishing a minimum level of functionality at the destination, materially comparable outcomes in response to the same input for shared features) is owed only by IaaS providers. PaaS and SaaS customers get the Art. 30(2)-(5) entitlements instead: open interfaces, common-specification compatibility, and exportable data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format. The most common live-switch dispute is a customer demanding functional equivalence from a SaaS provider; the customer is wrong on the law (gotcha 10, Recital 86, FAQ Q58a and Q58b). This does not mean the SaaS customer is left high and dry; the open-interface and machine-readable-export obligations are substantial.
- **The four duties during the transitional period.** Per Art. 25(2)(a)(i)-(iv) the source provider during transition must (i) provide reasonable assistance to the customer and any customer-authorised third parties; (ii) act with due care to maintain business continuity and continue providing the contracted functions; (iii) provide clear information on known continuity risks; (iv) maintain a high level of security throughout, in particular during transfer and during the retrieval period. These are operational obligations, conduct-based, and have teeth even where they look generic on paper.
- **The customer's exportable-data scope is broad but bounded.** Per Art. 2(38) and Recital 82 exportable data covers input and output data including metadata directly or indirectly generated or co-generated by the customer's use of the service. It excludes the provider's IP and trade-secret assets (or third-party assets in the same categories) and data related to service integrity and security where export would create cybersecurity exposure. The Art. 25(2)(f) catalogue should already have spelled out the exclusions; if it has not, the dispute lands mid-switch and slows everything down. Per Art. 30(6) the provider is not required to develop new technologies, transfer IP-protected or trade-secret assets, or compromise security.

## The seven-step walk

### Step 1: Scope check

Verify the Data Act applies and Ch VI is in scope. Confirm the contract was concluded after 12 September 2025 (or has been amended after that date to bring it within Art. 25-compliant drafting). For legacy contracts the customer needs to determine which Ch VI obligations apply now versus prospectively; that is a contract-review question handled on `ch6-customer-contract-review.md`. Confirm the source service is a "data processing service" within Art. 2(8) and not within an Art. 31 carve-out (route to `ch6-custom-built-carve-out.md` if the carve-out is arguable).

### Step 2: Chapter identification

Chapter VI throughout. Specifically Art. 23 (obstacle removal, relevant if the source provider is obstructing in subtle ways), Art. 25 (the switching contract terms and the timing), Art. 26 (information obligations, especially the online register), Art. 27 (good-faith cooperation, by all parties including the destination provider), Art. 30 (technical aspects: which Art. 30 paragraph applies depends on the service type). Art. 28 disclosure is informational. Art. 29 charges run in parallel; route to `ch6-charges.md`.

### Step 3: Role mapping

| Entity | Data Act role | GDPR role (if personal data in scope) | Other |
|--------|---------------|----------------------------------------|-------|
| Customer | Customer (Art. 2(30)) | Controller, typically | The party initiating the switch and electing the post-notice outcome under Art. 25(3) |
| Source provider | Provider of data processing services |  | Bound by Art. 23, 25, 27, 30; bound by Art. 26 information disclosure |
| Destination provider | Provider of data processing services |  | Bound by Art. 27 good-faith cooperation; bound by its own Art. 25 obligations once the customer has signed with it |
| Customer-authorised third party (if a migration vendor is engaged) |  |  | Per Recital 85 rights and obligations of the customer extend to such a third-party entity in those circumstances |

Per Recital 85 if a third-party migration vendor is acting on the customer's behalf, the customer's obligations (including the Art. 27 good-faith duty) apply to that vendor.

### Step 4: Fact-category sorting

Card-specific dimensions to sort the data and assets in play against.

- **Exportable data (Art. 2(38)) vs digital assets (Art. 2(32)) vs out-of-scope.** Exportable data: input, output, and metadata directly or indirectly generated or co-generated by the customer's use, excluding provider IP and trade secrets and security-related data. Digital assets: elements for which the customer has a right of use independent of the contract (applications, virtualisation artefacts such as VMs and containers, metadata for settings, security, access and control rights management). Out of scope: provider IP, provider trade secrets, third-party IP and trade secrets, data related to service integrity and security where export creates cybersecurity exposure (Art. 25(2)(f), Art. 30(6), Recital 82, FAQ Q53).
- **Service type per Art. 30.** IaaS within Art. 30(1) (functional equivalence regime) vs non-IaaS within Art. 30(2)-(5) (open interfaces, common specifications, machine-readable export). The classification was settled in the contract-review phase; if it was not, settle it now.
- **Standalone switching vs in-parallel use.** Art. 34 modifies obligations where the customer is using the destination service in parallel rather than as a replacement; egress charges for ongoing in-parallel use are still permitted at cost under Art. 34(2).
- **Common-specification availability.** Per Art. 30(3) non-IaaS providers must be compatible with common specifications published in the central Union standards repository (Art. 35(8)), within 12 months of publication. Where no common specification is yet published, Art. 30(5) requires structured, commonly used, machine-readable export on customer request. As of today the repository is still being populated (per FAQ Q57 the Commission is mapping existing harmonised standards and open interoperability specifications); operational reliance is mostly on Art. 30(5).

### Step 5: Limb-by-limb application of Art. 25 timing and Art. 30 entitlements

The switching plan is built from the timing limbs of Art. 25(2)-(5) and the entitlement limbs of Art. 30.

**Day 0 (notice). The customer notifies.** Art. 25(2)(d): notice period begins. The contract remains applicable throughout. Maximum two months. The customer should serve the notice in writing, identify the destination provider (or the on-premises destination), and reference the Art. 25(2)(a) switching obligation.

**Days 1 to 14 (working days from request). Provider's window to claim technical unfeasibility.** Art. 25(4): if the 30-day mandatory transitional period is technically unfeasible, the provider must notify the customer within 14 working days of the switching request, justify the unfeasibility, and indicate an alternative transitional period not exceeding seven months. Per Recital 87 the burden of proof falls fully on the provider. If the 14 working days pass without notification, the provider has waived the technical-unfeasibility extension and is on the 30-day track. The customer should diary the 14-working-day deadline.

**Through the notice period (up to two months). Pre-transition preparation.** The source provider's Art. 26 obligations are in active use: information on procedures, methods, formats, restrictions and known technical limitations, and the up-to-date online register (data structures, formats, standards, interoperability specifications). The customer (or its destination provider, or its migration vendor) uses this to plan the upload at the destination. The Art. 27 good-faith duty is live for all parties.

**End of notice. Customer's election under Art. 25(3).** The customer notifies the source provider whether to (a) switch to the named destination provider, (b) switch to on-premises ICT infrastructure, or (c) erase exportable data and digital assets. The election is contractually entrenched per Art. 25(3); the source provider cannot frame any of (b) or (c) as separately paid services.

**Day 0 of transitional period to day 30 (or extended). The four operational duties.** Art. 25(2)(a)(i)-(iv): the source provider during the transitional period (i) provides reasonable assistance to the customer and customer-authorised third parties; (ii) acts with due care to maintain business continuity and continues providing the contracted functions; (iii) provides clear information on known continuity risks; (iv) maintains a high level of security throughout, in particular during transfer and during the retrieval window.

**Throughout. Art. 30 entitlements.** For IaaS within Art. 30(1) scope: functional equivalence support: capabilities, adequate information, documentation, technical support, and where appropriate the necessary tools (Art. 30(1), Recital 92). Per Recital 92 the source provider is not obliged to rebuild the service within the destination provider's infrastructure; its duty is limited to its own service environment. For non-IaaS within Art. 30(2)-(5) scope: open interfaces available free of charge to the customer and the destination provider (Art. 30(2)); compatibility with common specifications or harmonised standards published in the central Union standards repository, within 12 months of publication (Art. 30(3)); the online register kept current (Art. 30(4)); and at customer request, exportable data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format (Art. 30(5)).

**Day 0 of transitional period extension if invoked.** If the customer exercises its Art. 25(5) right to extend once for a period it considers more appropriate, this is in addition to the Art. 25(4) technical-unfeasibility extension. Per Recital 87 the customer's extension right is exclusive to the customer and can be exercised before or during the transitional period.

**End of transitional period. Termination triggers.** Art. 25(2)(c)(i): contract is considered terminated on successful completion of the switching process, and the customer is notified of termination. Erasure-only path: Art. 25(2)(c)(ii) terminates at end of notice if the customer wished only to erase on service termination.

**Days 1 to 30 of retrieval window (after transitional ends).** Art. 25(2)(g): minimum 30 calendar days for data retrieval. The customer may agree a longer alternative period per Art. 25(2)(h). Security obligation under Art. 25(2)(a)(iv) continues to apply during retrieval.

**End of retrieval window. Erasure.** Art. 25(2)(h): full erasure of exportable data and digital assets generated directly by the customer (or related to the customer directly) after retrieval window expiry, on successful switching. The erasure obligation is the source provider's; the customer should obtain an erasure attestation.

### Step 6: Cross-regime gate check

- **GDPR overlay (loaded if personal data in scope).** Load `references/gates/gdpr-overlay.md`. The data processing agreement under GDPR Art. 28 between source provider and customer (controller-processor) must align with the Art. 25 timing. The 30-day retrieval window does not override GDPR storage-limitation obligations; the customer should plan retrieval against its own retention rules. The destination-side controller-processor relationship must be in place before transition starts.
- **Trade Secrets Directive overlay (warn).** Load `references/gates/trade-secrets-directive.md` only where the Art. 25(2)(f) exemption catalogue is in active dispute, e.g. the provider attempts to widen the exemption mid-switch. The carve-out must not impede or delay switching; that bar is operative.
- **DMA gatekeeper exclusion (warn).** Load `references/gates/dma-gatekeeper.md` only where the customer or destination provider is or is acting for a DMA-designated gatekeeper, and the switch involves data also obtained under Art. 4(1) or Art. 5. Pure Ch VI switching between two non-gatekeeper providers does not trigger Art. 5(3) or Art. 6(2)(d).
- **Sectoral lex specialis (warn-only).** DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) imposes parallel exit-execution obligations on financial entities. NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) imposes parallel obligations on essential and important entities. Other sectoral overlays apply where the workload is regulated (health, energy, agriculture, etc.). Load `references/gates/sectoral-lex-specialis.md`.
- **Member State implementing law (warn-only).** If the source provider misbehaves and the customer needs to invoke Art. 38 (complaint to the competent authority) or Art. 10 (dispute settlement), the competent authority is identified by Member State. Load `references/gates/member-state.md`.

### Step 7: Synthesis with current-law-vs-proposal

- **Current law.** Articles 23, 25, 26, 27, 30 of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 (Data Act) govern the live execution. Operative recitals: Recital 82 (exportable data scope); Recital 83 (digital assets); Recital 84 (switching definition); Recital 85 (switching as a customer-driven multi-step process; third-party migration vendor); Recital 86 (functional equivalence IaaS-only); Recital 87 (extension burden of proof on provider; customer's extension right); Recital 92 (limits of source-provider obligations to own service environment).
- **Proposed amendment under the Digital Omnibus.** COM(2025) 833 final (19 November 2025) proposes an expanded Art. 31 custom-built carve-out and SME / small-mid-cap exemption from non-IaaS obligations for pre-12-September-2025 contracts. For a live switch on a post-12-September-2025 contract today, the Omnibus does not change the regime. Status: co-legislator negotiation, not adopted. See `sources/digital-omnibus-amendments-tracker.md`.

The output cites current law as operative. The Omnibus discussion is forward-looking only.

## Decision point

After Steps 5 and 6 the execution plan yields one of three paths.

1. **Clean switch.** Provider is cooperating, classification is settled, timing is on schedule. Output Path 1 below: timeline-keyed plan.
2. **Disputed switch.** Provider has invoked Art. 25(4) technical-unfeasibility (legitimately or not), or is sandbagging Art. 30 obligations, or is widening Art. 25(2)(f) trade-secret exemptions mid-flight. Output Path 2 below: dispute-management plan and escalation to Art. 38 / Art. 10.
3. **Pre-execution issue.** Customer has not yet served notice and the contract has issues that should be addressed first. Route to `ch6-customer-contract-review.md` and defer execution planning.

## Drafter notes

- **The 14-working-day deadline is the bottleneck.** Customer counsel should diary it from the moment the switching request is served. If the source provider does not notify a technical-unfeasibility claim within 14 working days, the source provider is on the 30-day track and cannot later invoke Art. 25(4) to extend. This is the strongest customer position in the live switch.
- **Document everything.** Live-switch disputes go to Art. 38 complaints quickly, and the competent authority will look at the written record. Email and ticket logs of source-provider conduct (delays, omissions, vague answers on register entries, refusals to give open-interface access) are the substance of an Art. 38 file. Build the documentation habit into the customer-side execution playbook on day one.
- **The destination provider's good-faith duty.** Art. 27 binds the destination provider too. Where the destination has not yet onboarded the customer adequately, the destination's own behaviour matters. Customers sometimes focus narrowly on the source provider and miss the destination-side issues until they become the bottleneck.
