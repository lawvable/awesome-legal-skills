# Verification workflow — Reg. 64 Kosovo Panels

## The principle

No case-specific citation appears in an output until verified against an authoritative source in the current conversation. Foundational instruments (UNSC Res 1244, UNMIK Regulations 1999/1, 2000/6, 2000/64, 2001/2, 2003/25, 2003/26, Joint Action 2008/124/CFSP, Law 2008/03-L053) may be cited from project knowledge.

## The fallback ladder

1. **unmik.unmissions.org** — UNMIK official archive; first point of entry for UNMIK Regulations and many procedural documents
2. **unmikonline.org** (legacy site, intermittent availability) — UNMIK historical site
3. **Wayback Machine** (web.archive.org) — for retired UNMIK content
4. **EULEX archives** (eulex-kosovo.eu) — for 2008-2018 successor proceedings
5. **legal-tools.org** — ICC Legal Tools Database mirror
6. **Kosovo Judicial Council** archives — for Kosovo Supreme Court and district court records
7. **USIP Hartmann reports** — for institutional and historical context
8. **OSCE Mission in Kosovo** reports — for procedural and monitoring records
9. **ICTJ Perriello and Wierda 2006** and other academic literature — for context only, never authority for what Reg. 64 Panels have held
10. **Ask the user** if nothing verifies

## Anticipate longer verification times

Unlike active institutions with comprehensive official websites, the Reg. 64 archive is **distributed across multiple repositories**:
- UNMIK regulations on unmik.unmissions.org
- Individual case records in Kosovo court archives (often Albanian-language only)
- Procedural records in OSCE monitoring reports
- Institutional narratives in USIP and academic publications

**Allow more verification time than for active institutions.** When a Tier 1 source cannot be located, **document the search attempt** and consider whether partial verification (existence + Tier 2 description) suffices for the proposition being supported.

## Capture these fields

For every verified citation:
- **District court of issue** — Pristina, Mitrovica, Peja/Peć, Prizren, Gjilan/Gnjilane (or Supreme Court of Kosovo for appeals)
- **Reg. 64 Panel designation** — was this case heard under Reg. 64 (majority international) or by an ordinary Kosovo panel?
- **Period** — UNMIK era (2000-2008), EULEX era (2008-2018), post-EULEX (2018+)
- **Document type** — Indictment, Verdict, Appeal Judgment, Decision
- **Case number** — district-court-specific (P. nr. / Ap. nr. / K. nr. conventions)
- **Date**
- **Paragraph or page**
- **Language version cited** — Albanian, Serbian, English

## Reg. 64-specific traps

### Trap 1 — Reg. 64 Panels are NOT the Kosovo Specialist Chambers (KSC)

**THE most important trap.** The two institutions are **fundamentally distinct**:

| | Reg. 64 Panels | Kosovo Specialist Chambers (KSC) |
|---|---|---|
| **Founding instrument** | UNMIK Regulation 2000/64 (administrative law) | Law No. 05/L-053 of August 2015 (Kosovo statute) + Constitutional Amendment + Council of EU Joint Action 2014/685/CFSP |
| **Period** | 2000-2008 (UNMIK era); EULEX continuation 2008-2018 | 2015-present |
| **Seat** | **Within Kosovo district courts** (Pristina, Mitrovica, etc.) | **The Hague**, Netherlands (relocated outside Kosovo) |
| **Institutional nature** | Panels integrated within ordinary national courts | Separate relocated tribunal with own staff and premises |
| **Jurisdiction** | Range of "important or sensitive" cases (war crimes, inter-ethnic violence, organised crime, corruption) | Specific subset of allegations from 2008 Marty Council of Europe report (KLA crimes 1998-2000) |
| **Substantive law** | Yugoslav Federal Criminal Code (1976) and Provisional Criminal Code Kosovo (2003) | Kosovo Criminal Code applied through KSC framework |
| **Composition** | 3-judge panels with minimum 2 internationals at district court level | KSC judges (all internationals) at separate institution |

**Common error:** treating "Kosovo internationalised criminal jurisdictions" as a single category. They are **two distinct institutions** with distinct skills in this repository.

### Trap 2 — Reg. 64 Panels are panels within ordinary courts, NOT a separate tribunal

Reg. 64 Panels operated **within the existing Kosovo district court structure** — they share buildings, registries, and clerical staff with ordinary Kosovo court proceedings. **No separate "Reg. 64 Court"** exists.

**Common error:** referring to "the UNMIK Tribunal" or "the Reg. 64 Court" as if it were a separate institution. The accurate designation is **"the District Court of [X] sitting as a Reg. 64 Panel"** or "the Supreme Court of Kosovo sitting as a Reg. 64 Panel".

### Trap 3 — KWECC was abandoned, not established

The **Kosovo War and Ethnic Crimes Court (KWECC)** was **proposed** in early-to-mid 2000 as a separate tribunal modeled on the SCSL/ECCC pattern. However, it was **abandoned in autumn 2000** for institutional, political, and resource reasons. **Reg. 64 was the alternative chosen** in lieu of KWECC.

**Common error:** referring to KWECC as if it existed. It did not. Its proposal and abandonment are part of the institutional history that produced Reg. 64.

### Trap 4 — Three institutional phases of international judges in Kosovo

The IJP program evolved in three phases:

1. **Phase 1 (early 2000)** — initial ad hoc appointments of **one international judge and one international prosecutor per district court** following the Mitrovica February 2000 violence. Minority position; outvotable by local lay judges
2. **Phase 2 — UNMIK Reg. 2000/6 of 15 February 2000** — formal legal basis for IJP appointments. Same minority configuration in practice
3. **Phase 3 — UNMIK Reg. 2000/64 of 15 December 2000** — **majority international composition** through 3-judge panels with minimum 2 internationals

**Common error:** treating "international judges in Kosovo" as a single phenomenon. The Reg. 64 majority configuration is distinct from earlier minority configurations.

### Trap 5 — UNMIK era ended December 2008, not February 2008

Kosovo declared independence on **17 February 2008**, but UNMIK did not cease operations on that date. UNMIK's executive judicial functions continued until **9 December 2008**, when **EULEX** took over the international judges and prosecutors role under Joint Action 2008/124/CFSP. UNMIK itself continued (in reduced monitoring mode) until 2016.

**Common error:** dating the "end of UNMIK" to February 2008. The transition was gradual; the executive judicial transfer to EULEX was December 2008.

### Trap 6 — EULEX is the successor mechanism but NOT under Reg. 64

EULEX international judges and prosecutors operate under:
- Joint Action 2008/124/CFSP (EU instrument)
- Law 2008/03-L053 of 13 March 2008 (Kosovo statute)

**Not** under UNMIK Reg. 2000/64. The mechanism is **analogous** (majority international panels for designated cases) but legally distinct.

**Common error:** citing UNMIK Reg. 2000/64 as the legal basis for an EULEX-era case. The applicable basis is Law 2008/03-L053.

### Trap 7 — Substantive law transition April 2004

Reg. 64 Panels applied:
- **2000 to 6 April 2004**: Yugoslav Federal Criminal Code (1976) — as preserved by UNMIK Reg. 1999/24
- **From 6 April 2004**: Provisional Criminal Code of Kosovo (UNMIK Reg. 2003/25)
- **From 2012**: Criminal Code of the Republic of Kosovo (Law 04/L-082) — post-independence

**Common error:** citing the wrong substantive code for the wrong period. The 6 April 2004 transition is critical for crimes prosecuted in that window.

## Translation discipline

Reg. 64 Panel proceedings used Albanian, Serbian, and English. **Judgments were typically issued in multiple language versions** with the Albanian and Serbian versions being the operative national versions and English being the operational UNMIK working version.

**For substantive citation:**
- Identify which language version is being cited
- Recognise the translation chain (e.g., Albanian original → English UNMIK working text)
- For paragraph-level citation, prefer the language version with the most authoritative paragraph numbering

## Partial verification is acceptable if disclosed

If you can confirm a case existed and was heard by a Reg. 64 Panel but cannot locate the specific judgment text, **disclose this**. The archival distribution means partial verification with transparent disclosure is sometimes the best achievable; **silent overstatement is not acceptable**.
