# DPDPA Legal Framework Reference

## Statute Details
- **Act**: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023), received Presidential assent August 11, 2023
- **Rules**: Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, notified November 13, 2025 (Rules 1, 2 and 17-21 in force from notification date; Rule 4 one year after; Rules 3, 5-16, 22-23 eighteen months after notification — substantive obligations effective ~May 13, 2027)
- **Key MeitY page for updates**: https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa

## Grounds for Processing Personal Data (Section 4)

A person may process personal data only for a **lawful purpose** (not expressly forbidden by law) AND on one of two grounds:

### Ground 1: Consent (Section 6)
- Most common ground for commercial entities
- Consent must be: **free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous** with a **clear affirmative action**
- Must signify agreement to processing for the **specified purpose** and be limited to data **necessary** for that purpose
- Any consent that waives statutory rights (e.g., right to complain to the Board) is void to that extent
- Data Principal can withdraw consent at any time; ease of withdrawal must be comparable to ease of giving consent
- Withdrawal does not affect lawfulness of prior processing
- Upon withdrawal, Data Fiduciary must cease (and cause processors to cease) processing within reasonable time
- **Burden of proof**: If disputed, the Data Fiduciary must prove that notice was given and consent was obtained (S.6(10))
- Consent can be given/managed/withdrawn through a **Consent Manager** registered with the Board (S.6(7)-(9))

### Ground 2: Certain Legitimate Uses (Section 7)
The following do **not** require consent from the Data Principal:
- **(a)** Voluntarily provided data for a specified purpose with no objection indicated
- **(b)** State/instrumentalities providing prescribed subsidies, benefits, services, certificates, licences, or permits (where prior consent was given or data is from a government database)
- **(c)** State/instrumentalities performing functions under law or for sovereignty/security
- **(d)** Compliance with a legal disclosure obligation
- **(e)** Compliance with a court judgment or order
- **(f)** Medical emergency threatening life or immediate health
- **(g)** Medical treatment or health services during epidemic/outbreak/public health threat
- **(h)** Safety/assistance during disaster or breakdown of public order
- **(i)** Employment purposes (safeguarding employer from corporate espionage, trade secret maintenance, etc.)

> **Practitioner note**: Most private sector Data Fiduciaries dealing with consumers rely on **consent** as the primary ground. Legitimate uses under Section 7 are narrow and must not be used to circumvent the consent requirement.

## Notice Obligations (Section 5 & Rule 3)

### Section 5(1) — Pre-consent Notice
Every request for consent must be **accompanied or preceded** by a notice informing the Data Principal of:
1. The personal data and the **purpose** for which it will be processed
2. How to exercise rights under Section 6(4) (withdraw consent) and Section 13 (Data Principal rights)
3. How to make a complaint to the **Data Protection Board of India**

### Rule 3 — Content and Form of Notice
The notice must:
- **(a)** Be presented independently and understandably, separate from other information provided by the Data Fiduciary
- **(b)** Be in **clear and plain language**, giving the Data Principal sufficient detail to give specific and informed consent, including at a minimum:
  - (i) An **itemised description** of the personal data
  - (ii) The **specific purpose(s)** and a **specific description** of the goods/services/uses enabled by processing
- **(c)** Contain a link or mechanism for the Data Principal to:
  - (i) Withdraw consent (with comparable ease to giving it)
  - (ii) Exercise rights under the Act
  - (iii) File a complaint with the Board

### Section 5(3) / Section 6(3) — Language
- The Data Fiduciary must give the Data Principal the option to access the notice **in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution**
- The **22 Eighth Schedule languages** are: Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu

## Data Fiduciary General Obligations (Section 8)

- Must ensure completeness, accuracy and consistency of personal data used for decisions affecting the Data Principal or disclosed to another Data Fiduciary (S.8(3))
- Must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures (S.8(4))
- Must protect personal data with reasonable security safeguards to prevent breaches (S.8(5))
- Must notify the Board and affected Data Principals of any breach (S.8(6))
- **Erasure obligation** (S.8(7)):
  - Must erase personal data upon withdrawal of consent OR as soon as it is reasonable to assume the specified purpose is no longer being served, whichever is earlier
  - Must cause Data Processors to erase data made available to them
- The purpose is **deemed no longer served** (S.8(8)) if the Data Principal does not:
  - (a) approach the Data Fiduciary for the performance of the specified purpose; AND
  - (b) exercise any rights in relation to that processing
  - ...for the time period prescribed (see Rule 8 and Third Schedule below)
- Must publish contact details of DPO or authorised contact person (S.8(9))
- Must establish effective grievance redressal mechanism (S.8(10))

## Data Retention (Rule 8 & Schedules)

### Rule 8(1) — Third Schedule (Sector-Specific Retention)
Data Fiduciaries falling in the Third Schedule must erase personal data (unless retention required by law) if the Data Principal does not engage for the specified period:
- **E-commerce entities** with 2 crore+ registered users: **3 years** from last login or transaction
- **Online gaming entities** with 50 lakh+ registered users: **3 years** from last login
- **Social media entities** with 2 crore+ users: **3 years** from last login

### Rule 8(2) — Pre-erasure Notification
At least **48 hours before** scheduled erasure, the Data Fiduciary must notify the Data Principal that the data will be erased unless the Data Principal logs in, contacts the Data Fiduciary, or exercises rights.

### Rule 8(3) — Seventh Schedule (Log Retention)
Regardless of the above, every Data Fiduciary must retain **personal data, associated traffic data, and processing logs** for a **minimum of 1 year** from the date of processing for the purposes specified in the Seventh Schedule (primarily audit, accountability and security purposes), after which they must be erased unless further retention is required by law.

## Data Principal Rights (Section 13)

1. **Right to Obtain Information**: Right to summary of personal data processed and processing activities
2. **Right to Correction, Completion, Updating and Erasure**: Right to correct inaccurate data, complete incomplete data, update outdated data, or erase data no longer necessary
3. **Right to Grievance Redressal**: Right to raise grievances with the Data Fiduciary and, if not satisfactorily resolved, to the Data Protection Board

## Children's Data (Section 9 & Rules 10-12, Schedule 4)

### Section 9 — Core Rules
- Before processing any personal data of a **child** (person under 18), the Data Fiduciary must obtain **verifiable consent** of the parent or lawful guardian (S.9(1))
- A Data Fiduciary must **not** undertake processing likely to cause **detrimental effect on the well-being** of a child (S.9(2))
- A Data Fiduciary must **not** undertake **tracking or behavioural monitoring** of children or **targeted advertising** directed at children (S.9(3))
- Sub-sections (1) and (3) do not apply to classes of Data Fiduciaries or purposes prescribed in rules (S.9(4))

### Rule 10 — Verifiable Parental Consent Mechanism
Data Fiduciaries must adopt appropriate technical and organisational measures to:
- Obtain verifiable parental consent before processing any child's data
- Exercise due diligence to verify the parent is an adult, by reference to:
  - (a) Identity and age details already held by the Data Fiduciary, OR
  - (b) Identity and age details voluntarily provided — directly by the person, or via a virtual token from a government-authorised entity (e.g., Digital Locker/DigiLocker)

### Rule 12 & Fourth Schedule — Exemptions from S.9(1) and S.9(3)
**Part A (Entity-Based Exemptions)** — entities fully exempt (with conditions):
- Clinical establishments, mental health establishments, healthcare professionals (limited to necessary health-related services for the child)
- Entities allied with healthcare professionals (limited to supporting prescribed treatment)

**Part B (Purpose-Based Exemptions)** — specific purposes exempt:
- Provision of subsidies/benefits/services under S.7(b) for the child's benefit
- Creation of an email account for communication purposes
- Processing solely to prevent access to information harmful to child's well-being
- Processing limited to confirming the user is not a child (i.e., age verification under Rule 10 due diligence)

> **Practitioner note**: If the user's service is not intended for children and they have adequate technical age-verification measures, they may rely on a confirmation by the user that they are 18+ and disclaim liability. However, a robust legal position requires a mechanism to screen out minors.

## Security Safeguards (Rule 6)

Data Fiduciaries must implement at minimum:
- Appropriate data security measures (encryption, obfuscation, masking, virtual tokens)
- Measures to control access to computer resources
- Visibility of access to personal data (logs, monitoring, review)
- Measures to preserve confidentiality, integrity or availability (including data backup)
- Retention of logs and personal data for a minimum of **1 year** for security/audit purposes (Seventh Schedule)
- Appropriate security provisions in contracts with Data Processors
- Technical and organisational measures for effective observance

---

## Grievance Redressal & Data Protection Board (Section 13 & 14)

- Data Fiduciary must establish an effective grievance mechanism (S.8(10))
- Data Principals can approach the Board if not satisfied with the Data Fiduciary's response (S.14)
- The Board adjudicates complaints and can impose financial penalties
- Maximum penalty for breach of consent obligations: up to ₹250 crore per instance (Second Schedule)

---

## Significant Data Fiduciary Additional Obligations (Section 10, Rule 13)

If notified as a Significant Data Fiduciary:
- Annual Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) and audit
- Data Protection Officer must be appointed
- Data Localisation for notified personal data (no cross-border transfer)

---

## Cross-border Data Transfers

- General rule: No restriction on transfer outside India unless specifically restricted
- Government may restrict transfer to certain countries by notification
- Significant Data Fiduciaries: additional localisation obligations may apply
- Always check current MeitY notifications before advising on transfers
