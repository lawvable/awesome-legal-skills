# EU Labelling Icons & Deployer Labelling Design — Reference

Implementation reference for the **deployer** side of Art. 50(4): the official EU icon set and the
design / placement requirements finalised by the Code of Practice (Section 2, Deployers, Annex 1).

> **What is mandatory vs. optional (read this first).** The **legal duty** (Art. 50(4)) is to disclose
> clearly and human-perceivably. The **EU icons are optional** and "**do not establish legal compliance by
> themselves**" (official EU icons page). For **signatories**, the Code makes the *design and placement*
> rules below binding commitments; for everyone else they are strong best practice. Official assets and
> guidance: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content>.

---

## The official EU icon set — three icons

The AI Office published a set of **three empirically user-tested icons** (Code Section 2, Annex 1). Each icon
comes in **four variations**: black, white, black at 50% transparency, and white at 50% transparency.

| Icon | When to use | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Basic icon** | AI was involved in creating deepfake content (image/audio/video) **or** published text — **or** when a custom text label or an **interactive second layer** is used | Deepfake video captioned "voices generated with" + the basic icon |
| **Fully AI-Generated** | The entire deepfake/text is generated by AI with **no human-created elements or editorial control** (apart from prompting) | Fully AI-generated video of a politician; AI-composed music/art\*; AI-generated news summaries |
| **Partially AI-Modified** | Pre-existing, **human-made** content was **partially modified** with AI, turning it into a deepfake or public-interest text | A real photo with a face swapped by AI; an empty room AI-furnished |

\* Fully AI-generated *artistic/creative/fictional/satirical* works may enjoy the limited (proportionate)
disclosure regime — see below.

> **This resolves the earlier "third icon undefined" placeholder.** The three icons are **Basic**,
> **Fully AI-Generated**, and **Partially AI-Modified**. The **Basic icon** is the general-purpose /
> interactive-layer anchor; the other two mark the *degree* of AI involvement. A task force will later add
> an **audio** version and an interactive second layer.

**Assets:** download all icons/variations as [SVG](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/129546)
or [PNG](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/129547). Free to use without attribution;
non-signatory use is **not** a signal of adherence to the Code.

> On activation, web-search `EU official AI-generated content labelling icons set 2026` to pick up the
> audio-icon / interactive-layer additions and any updated assets.

## What is mandatory in the label — the "AI" acronym

The Code (Section 2, Measure 1.1) draws a sharp mandatory/optional line for **signatories**:

| Element | Status | Detail |
|---------|--------|--------|
| The capitalised acronym **"AI"** (in English) | **Mandatory** | A national-language version (e.g. "KI", "IA") is permitted **only** where using English would be legally incompatible |
| The second-layer word **"GENERATED" / "MODIFIED"** | **Encouraged, optional** | Signals the *degree* of AI involvement — see the copyright note below |
| Audio-only content: an **audible disclaimer at the start** | **Mandatory** | Spoken at the beginning; add the visual icon too wherever a screen is available |

> **Why GENERATED/MODIFIED is left optional — a copyright trap.** Had the generated-vs-modified split been
> compulsory, every deployer would have to disclose the *extent* of AI involvement. "Generated" implies
> **no human authorship**, which can be used to attack the **copyright protectability** of the work (human
> authorship is a copyright prerequisite). Where the facts allow, presenting content as **"modified"** is
> safer for protectability and, per the Commission's user-testing, better understood by audiences. Advise
> the deployer to choose the accurate-but-protectability-aware label, not to over-disclose reflexively.

## Placement by modality (Code Section 2, Measure 1.2)

**Default rule: embed the icon in the content** ("the icon travels with the content"). A **UI overlay** is
allowed **only** as an *equivalent alternative* and only where it still meets the principles below and the
deployer makes best efforts to keep the disclosure intact on download / screenshot / re-upload. The label
must be recognisable **without any further interaction**, stay visible long enough to be noticed, and appear
**at the latest at first exposure**.

| Modality | Mandatory (signatory "will") | Flexibility ("may / encouraged") |
|----------|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Static image** | Visible icon placed where no overlay intervenes (e.g. top-right corner) | Size/style may vary if it stays clear and distinguishable |
| **Video** | Icon **at the beginning** and, at minimum, **after interruptions**; at regular intervals where possible | Continuous on-screen display throughout is encouraged |
| **Audio (with a screen)** | Audible disclaimer at the start **plus** an additional visual icon where a screen is available | Additional audible cues (tones / earcons) |
| **Published text** | Icon **above or near the headline**, or in the **colophon**; short text must still be labelled | May label only the AI part; short text may use a contextual UI notice |
| **Closed professional setting** | Clear, distinguishable disclosure **before** exposure | May be placed in the UI, the physical setting, or another suitable medium |
| **Multimodal** | Apply the rule for each modality present; label both streams where content combines video + audio deepfake elements | — |

## Accessibility of the disclosure

Art. 50(5) requires disclosures to conform to the **applicable accessibility requirements** (it does *not*
name a specific directive). Assess whether the **European Accessibility Act** (Directive (EU) 2019/882)
applies to your product/service, and use **WCAG AA** as the practical design benchmark where a web/mobile UI
is involved. The official icon guidance recommends:

- a clearly visible icon size; any accompanying label in **plain language**, avoiding jargon and
  **abbreviations other than "AI"**;
- alt text / ARIA labels indicating the content is AI-generated or manipulated;
- if the disclosure is time-limited, keep it visible long enough for users with cognitive/processing
  difficulties;
- if a **second interactive layer** carries more detail, indicate that it exists and make it navigable by
  assistive technology.

Contrast: aim for **≥ 4.5:1** (WCAG AA) between label and background as a design benchmark.

## Proportionate disclosure for artistic / creative works

Where deepfake content forms part of an **evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous**
work, the Code permits a **proportionate** approach (Section 2, Commitment 3): the disclosure may live
**adjacent** to the work — in credits, a loading screen, an exhibition leaflet, an entrance ticket, or on
packaging — rather than as an in-frame indicator, and may appear at the end rather than the start. Limits:

- the work must **"evidently"** fall in one of those categories — content that is *ambiguous* to the
  audience does not qualify;
- **advertising gets no blanket pass** — content that is *primarily commercial and recognisable as such*
  needs ordinary full disclosure; only an *evidently* creative/fictional ad might qualify, case by case;
- "proportionate" ≠ "hidden"; the disclosure must remain clear and reasonably discoverable, and the
  exception is **lost** when the content leaves its original artistic context;
- the carve-out softens the **form of disclosure only** — personality rights, IP and data-protection
  duties continue in full (Recital 134). See [obviousness-and-exceptions.md](obviousness-and-exceptions.md) §3.

## Relationship to provider marking

Deployer labelling (50(4), human-perceptible, **this file**) is **distinct** from provider marking
(50(2), machine-readable, [code-of-practice-final.md](code-of-practice-final.md) Section 1). A deepfake may
need **both**: the provider marks the output machine-readably, and the deployer labels it visibly. An
upstream 50(2) watermark does **not** discharge the deployer's 50(4) duty, and vice versa. The labelling
duty stays with the **deployer** — the actor with authority over the AI's use; a platform that merely passes
on third-party content is **not** a deployer (draft Guidelines para. 12). Where several agencies feed one
brand (or many sellers one marketplace), uniformity is a **contractual** matter: standardise on the EU icon,
require conformity with the Code's design/placement rules, and agree who preserves the label down the chain.
