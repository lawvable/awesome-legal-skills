# Worked Example

A short before-and-after on a real submission, for calibration when the correct disposition of
a borderline point is unclear. Read for the reasoning, not the subject matter.

## The target

An unsuccessful bar-examination candidate wrote to a state supreme court after the July 2026
sitting was abandoned mid-morning owing to a network failure at the venue and then cancelled
outright. The letter described the day at length, asserted that the applicants were blameless,
and asked the court to exercise its inherent authority to admit them without examination —
relying on that court's 2020 grant of diploma privilege — or at minimum to relieve them of the
resulting costs. A makeup sitting five weeks later, deferral, and a full refund of the
examination fee had been offered.

## Pass 1 produced twelve points. Pass 2 kept four.

### Cut

**"The letter is dated 'today' but reports the following day's events."** Presented as a
surgical strike; alleged the document was revised after the fact without disclosure or
circulated as a template. Mode 4 and mode 3. The obvious explanation — she began writing on
the first day and added a paragraph before sending on the second — is the one a reader reaches
first, and there was no evidence of any template. The point invited a free reply about
counsel's fairness and was the most damaging item in the memo.

**Hearsay, unsourced crowd estimates, second-hand distress evidence.** Mode 2 and mode 1.
Correspondence to a court exercising administrative authority over admissions is not an
evidentiary proceeding, and the institution had publicly confirmed the failure. The heaviest
artillery was aimed at the one part of the letter that was uncontested.

**"This is not a petition; it is unsworn correspondence."** Mode 7. The 2020 order on which
the strongest surviving head depended had itself arisen from public pressure and petitioning
of exactly this kind. The objection was incoherent alongside the point it sat next to.

**"She omits the refund entirely and writes mitigation out of the account."** Mode 6. The
letter listed withdrawal from the July examination as an option, which is the refund. And a
refunded examination fee does not answer the travel expense complained of, so the omission
cost her nothing. Downgraded to a single clause noting the refund stands against her pleaded
loss.

**"Maintaining peak readiness for five further weeks is unsupported, and is at least as likely
false as true."** Mode 5. The second clause was counsel's own unsupported empirical claim.
Rebuilt as a proves-too-much objection instead: if five further weeks of preparation is a
compensable injury, so is any deferral for any reason, and the proposition has no stopping
point.

**"She does not disclose her law school." "She does not produce her registration agreement."**
Mode 8, twice. Neither bore on anything in issue.

**"The harm pleaded is airfare and a hotel; the remedy sought is a licence for life."** Mode 9.
Rhetorically the best line in the memo and a strawman: her case was not about airfare but
about who should bear an accidental loss. Rebuilt at the higher level — the harm at its
highest is delay and expense, both of which have direct remedies, whereas the remedy sought
answers a different harm she has not suffered because the opportunity was rescheduled.

### Kept

1. **The 2020 precedent is her best point and it fails.** In 2020 there was no examination to
   be had for an indefinite period; in 2026 there was one, five weeks later. Rebuilt to open
   by *conceding* that the court plainly has the power — which converts the question from
   authority, which she wins, to occasion, which she loses.
2. **Her fairness argument destroys her remedy.** She invoked the candidates who received
   accommodations and completed the examination as proof of unequal conditions. Follow it:
   admit everyone else without examination and the only members of the cohort held to the
   competence standard are those who complied and sat — including any who fail. The obvious
   reply (extend the privilege to them too) was pre-empted within the head.
3. **The letter identifies no wrong, and does not say who should bear an accidental loss.**
   Replaced the weak causation material. Blamelessness was universal; it decides nothing. The
   argument she needed — that an accidental loss should fall on the public rather than on the
   applicants — is available but asymmetric, and she never attempts the comparison.
4. **The remedy does not fit the harm.** Reimbursement, fee waiver, and a guaranteed early
   sitting answer every harm pleaded; she asks for none of them as principal relief.

### The closing note

The rebuilt attack ended by stating that the factual account was not challenged, that the
institution had confirmed the failure, and that disputing the distress or the technician's
remark would be both futile and unattractive — the weakness was not that the letter
misdescribed the day but that nothing in the day yielded the remedy.

## The lesson

The four surviving heads were all present in Pass 1. Pass 2 added no new argument. What it
added was the removal of eight points that were diluting the four, and the reframing of two
survivors that had been stated in defeasible form. The deliverable was roughly half the length
and considerably harder to answer.
